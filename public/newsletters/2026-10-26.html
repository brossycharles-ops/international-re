Uruguay's 12% Gains Tax, 9% Gross in Punta Cana

Issue of October 26, 2026 · By Charles Brossy

Hi there,

This week we published new guides to Punta Cana, Ecuador's pensioner visa, Mexico's residency income rule and Panama's lower Pensionado income bar for homeowners. Our fact-checkers also re-verified the Punta del Este and Costa Rica retirement guides, and both had real errors that are now fixed.

This week's reading

Buying in Punta del Este as a foreigner
Re-verified on October 22: Uruguay taxes resale gains and rental income at 12%, buyers pay a 2% transfer tax plus notary fees of about 3% plus VAT, and the property route to the tax holiday for new residents now needs property worth more than UI 12.5 million (roughly US$2.1 million).

Punta Cana real estate investment 2026
New this week: median asking prices (US$2,278 per m² in Punta Cana–Bávaro, US$3,465 in Cap Cana), the 3% transfer tax, the new 10% capital gains tax under Law 30-26, what CONFOTUR really covers, and the hurricane and oversupply risks to price in.

Retire in Costa Rica: pensionado visa, Caja and costs
Re-verified on October 23: the pensionado route needs a permanent pension of at least US$1,000 a month and enrolment in the Caja, and a bank deposit paid back to you in monthly instalments no longer counts toward the US$2,500 rentista income.

Ecuador pensioner visa: US$1,446 a month
New this week: the pension threshold is three basic salaries (US$1,446 in 2026, plus US$250 per dependent), the visa costs US$320 in fees, and a registered property worth at least US$48,200 opens a separate investor route.

Panama's Pensionado drops to $750 with a home
A one-minute read: the usual B/.1,000 pension minimum falls to B/.750 a month if you bought Panamanian property for more than B/.100,000 in your own name, not through a company.

Mexico temporary residency income requirement
New this week: the legal test is after-tax income of 680 UMA days a month for six months (about MXN 79,771 at the 2026 UMA) or 11,460 UMA days in average savings, and why the dollar figure you are quoted depends on which consulate you apply at.

Number of the week

9.07%

Global Property Guide’s Q3 2026 survey gives Punta Cana and Bávaro an average gross rental yield of 9.07%. That is a gross figure from asking prices and asking rents; net yields are typically 1.5 to 2 percentage points lower. See the data

What we corrected this week

Our Punta del Este guide used to say Uruguay has no capital gains tax; that was wrong, and the gain on selling urban property is taxed at 12%, withheld by the notary. Our Costa Rica retirement guide no longer shows a Caja contribution table we could not confirm against an official 2026 CCSS scale; ask the CCSS for a quote instead.
