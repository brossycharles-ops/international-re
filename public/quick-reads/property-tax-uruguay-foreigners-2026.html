Property Tax in Uruguay for Foreigners 2026

By Charles Brossy June 3, 2026 · Updated October 27, 2026 3 min read

Foreigners pay the same property taxes in Uruguay as Uruguayan citizens: the country's investment law gives foreign investors the same treatment as nationals, and anyone can own real estate. Owners pay an annual Contribución Inmobiliaria set by each department, plus the national Primaria tax; buyers pay a 2% transfer tax (ITP) at purchase; and the gain on a resale is taxed at 12%. Earlier versions of this page said Uruguay has no capital gains tax and quoted an 11-year tax holiday under an older law; both were wrong or out of date, and the page was corrected and re-sourced on October 27, 2026.

The Contribución Inmobiliaria: How It Works

Uruguay's main annual property tax is the Contribución Inmobiliaria. It is charged by each department (Montevideo, Maldonado for Punta del Este, Rocha for La Paloma and so on), and each department sets its own rates and rules, so the bill depends on where the property is. Ask the escribano (notary) handling your purchase, or check the department's own website, for the current rate and the amount owed on a specific property before you buy.

Property taxes in Uruguay are generally charged on the cadastral value set by the Dirección Nacional de Catastro rather than on the price you paid, and the cadastral value is usually well below the market price, according to the Montevideo city guide. We have not found a reliable published figure for the size of that gap, so we do not quote one.

The Primary-School Tax (Primaria)

On top of the Contribución there is a national Impuesto de Enseñanza Primaria, charged on cadastral value at progressive rates of 1.5 to 3 per thousand (0.15% to 0.3%), with lower-value property below the scale, according to the tax code (Texto Ordenado 2023, Título 13) as cited in our Punta del Este buying guide.

Capital Gains: 12% on the Sale

Uruguay does tax gains on property. The DGI taxes the gain on selling urban property at 12%, and the notary acts as withholding agent at the sale. For property bought earlier than July 1, 2007, the seller can instead be taxed on 15% of the price. Rental income is also taxed at 12%: IRPF for residents and IRNR for non-residents. These figures come from the DGI and the tax code and are explained in our Punta del Este guide.

Closing Costs at Purchase

  • Transfer tax (ITP): 2% paid by the buyer and another 2% by the seller, on the cadastral value, according to the DGI.
  • Notary (escribano): about 3% of the price plus 22% VAT, paid by the buyer, according to Multilaw's Uruguay guide.
  • Agent's commission: 3% plus VAT if an agent is involved, plus small title-study and registration fees.

One Montevideo law firm, Pérez del Castillo & Asociados, puts total buyer costs at 5–9% of the price, as cited in our Montevideo city guide. Budget at the upper end until your escribano gives you a written estimate.

The New-Resident Tax Holiday Changed in 2026

Older guides, including an earlier version of this page, promise a 10- or 11-year tax holiday for anyone who moves to Uruguay. Law 20.446 (the 2025–2029 budget law) changed the rule from January 1, 2026. New tax residents can opt to pay non-resident income tax (IRNR) instead of IRPF on foreign investment income for the year they become resident plus the next ten, if they spend more than 183 days a year in Uruguay or meet an investment test, such as more than UI 12.5 million (roughly US$2.1 million, according to Forvis Mazars) in real estate, and were not tax residents in the previous two years. People who secured the old regime before the end of 2025 keep its terms. The holiday covers income tax only; it does not reduce the Contribución Inmobiliaria or the Primaria tax. Confirm your case with a Uruguayan tax adviser.

Bottom Line

Foreign owners in Uruguay are taxed exactly like locals. Plan for an annual departmental Contribución Inmobiliaria (ask for the figure on the specific property), the Primaria tax of 1.5 to 3 per thousand of cadastral value, 2% ITP plus notary fees at purchase, and 12% tax on rental income and on the gain when you sell. The generous new-resident tax holiday now applies only to people who meet the Law 20.446 tests.

For more, see our Montevideo market guide and our Punta del Este guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do foreigners pay more property tax in Uruguay?

No. Uruguayan law gives foreign investors the same treatment as nationals, so foreign owners pay the same property taxes as Uruguayans: the departmental Contribución Inmobiliaria, the national Primaria tax and, at purchase, the 2% ITP.

Is there capital gains tax on property in Uruguay?

Yes. The DGI taxes the gain on selling urban property at 12%, and the notary handling the sale withholds it. For property bought earlier than July 1, 2007, the seller can instead be taxed on 15% of the price.

Does Uruguay still have a 10- or 11-year tax holiday for new residents?

In a changed form. Under Law 20.446, people who become tax residents from January 1, 2026 can opt to pay non-resident income tax on foreign investment income for the year they become resident plus ten more, if they spend more than 183 days a year in Uruguay or meet an investment test such as more than UI 12.5 million in real estate, and were not tax residents in the previous two years.
