Property Tax in Uruguay for Foreigners 2026

By Charles Brossy June 3, 2026 · Updated October 28, 2026 3 min read

Foreigners pay the same property taxes in Uruguay as Uruguayan citizens: the country's investment law gives foreign investors the same treatment as nationals, and Multilaw's 2026 guide finds no restrictions on foreigners owning real estate. Owners pay an annual Contribución Inmobiliaria set by each department, plus the national Primaria tax; buyers pay a 2% transfer tax (ITP) at purchase; and the gain on a resale is taxed at 12%. Earlier versions of this page said Uruguay has no capital gains tax and quoted an 11-year tax holiday under an older law; both were wrong or out of date, and the page was corrected and re-sourced on October 27, 2026.

The Contribución Inmobiliaria: How It Works

Uruguay's main annual property tax is the Contribución Inmobiliaria. It is charged by each department (Montevideo, Maldonado for Punta del Este, Rocha for La Paloma and so on), and each department sets its own rates and rules, so the bill depends on where the property is. Ask the escribano (notary) handling your purchase, or check the department's own website, for the current rate and the amount owed on a specific property before you buy.

The bill is not based on the price you paid. National taxes such as the Primaria tax and the transfer tax use the valor real set by the Dirección Nacional de Catastro, which the government updates each year by decree (Decree 334/025 raised the 2025 values by a coefficient of 1.0425) and which Uruguay XXI, the state investment agency, says is generally below market value. Departments can use their own base: in Maldonado, which includes Punta del Este, the department’s own catastro office sets the taxable value and may set different values by zone according to the property market. We have not found a reliable published figure for the gap between taxable and market values, so we do not quote one.

The Primary-School Tax (Primaria)

On top of the Contribución there is a national Impuesto de Enseñanza Primaria, paid by the owner on the Catastro valor real at progressive rates of 1.5 to 3 per thousand (0.15% to 0.3%), according to the tax code (Texto Ordenado 2023, Título 13). In pesos as of January 1, 2025 (Decree 140/025), property valued below UYU 271,091 is exempt; the rate is 0.15% up to UYU 474,406, 0.2% up to UYU 2,033,154, 0.25% up to UYU 4,744,017 and 0.3% above that. The escribano cannot authorise a sale until the tax is paid up, so check the certificate before you buy.

Capital Gains: 12% on the Sale

Uruguay does tax gains on property. The DGI taxes the gain on selling urban property at 12%, and the notary acts as withholding agent at the sale. The purchase cost is adjusted for inflation by the change in the indexed unit (UI) before the gain is calculated, and the seller’s ITP and documented improvements can also be deducted. For property bought earlier than July 1, 2007, the seller can instead be taxed on 15% of the price or of the valor real, whichever is higher.

Rental Income: 12%

Rent from a Uruguayan property is taxed at 12%: IRPF for residents and IRNR for non-residents. For non-residents, some payers must withhold the tax at 10.5% of the gross rent, including companies that pay corporate income tax (IRAE), public bodies and agencies that manage the property and collect the rent (Decree 149/007). If your tenant is a private individual and no agency collects the rent, nobody withholds: you must appoint a representative resident in Uruguay to file and pay for you. Withholding on tourist lets of 31 days or less has been suspended in some summer seasons (Decree 309/024 covered December 2024 to April 2025), so ask your agency how it handles the current season.

The Wealth Tax (Impuesto al Patrimonio)

Individuals also pay an annual wealth tax on their net assets in Uruguay, including property, which is valued at the Catastro valor real rather than market value. Only the amount above a tax-free threshold is taxed: UYU 6,653,000 per person for 2025 (about US$165,000; Decree 334/025), doubled for a family unit. Uruguay XXI says individuals pay progressive rates ranging from 0.1% or 0.7% up to 1.5%, depending on whether the owner is resident. Under the tax code (Título 14, art. 53), the scale’s annual rate cuts stop at 1.5% for non-residents who do not pay IRNR, so a non-resident with no Uruguayan income pays the older scale of 0.7%, 1.1%, 1.4% and then 1.5%. An escribano or accountant can tell you which scale applies to you.

Closing Costs at Purchase

  • Transfer tax (ITP): 2% paid by the buyer and another 2% by the seller, on the cadastral value, according to the DGI.
  • Notary (escribano): about 3% of the price plus 22% VAT, paid by the buyer, according to Multilaw's Uruguay guide.
  • Agent's commission: 3% plus VAT if an agent is involved, plus small title-study and registration fees.

One Montevideo law firm, Pérez del Castillo & Asociados, put total buyer costs at 5–9% of the price in a 2021 article, as cited in our Montevideo city guide. Budget at the upper end until your escribano gives you a written estimate.

The New-Resident Tax Holiday Changed in 2026

Older guides, including an earlier version of this page, promise a 10- or 11-year tax holiday for anyone who moves to Uruguay. Law 20.446 (the 2025–2029 budget law) changed the rule from January 1, 2026. New tax residents can opt to pay non-resident income tax (IRNR) instead of IRPF on foreign investment income for the year they become resident plus the next ten, if they spend more than 183 days a year in Uruguay or meet an investment test, such as more than UI 12.5 million in real estate (about US$2.07 million on October 28, 2026, at the DGI’s indexed-unit value of UYU 6.6803 and dollar rate of UYU 40.286), and were not tax residents in the previous two years. People who secured the old regime before the end of 2025 keep its terms. The holiday covers income tax only; it does not reduce the Contribución Inmobiliaria or the Primaria tax. Confirm your case with a Uruguayan tax adviser.

Bottom Line

Foreign owners in Uruguay are taxed exactly like locals. Plan for an annual departmental Contribución Inmobiliaria (ask for the figure on the specific property), the Primaria tax of 1.5 to 3 per thousand of cadastral value, 2% ITP plus notary fees at purchase, the wealth tax above about US$165,000 of net assets, and 12% tax on rental income and on the gain when you sell. The generous new-resident tax holiday now applies only to people who meet the Law 20.446 tests.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do foreigners pay more property tax in Uruguay?

No. Uruguayan law gives foreign investors the same treatment as nationals, so foreign owners pay the same property taxes as Uruguayans: the departmental Contribución Inmobiliaria, the national Primaria tax and, at purchase, the 2% ITP. One difference is the wealth tax: non-residents who pay no Uruguayan income tax do not get its reduced rates.

Is there capital gains tax on property in Uruguay?

Yes. The DGI taxes the gain on selling urban property at 12%, and the notary handling the sale withholds it. For property bought earlier than July 1, 2007, the seller can instead be taxed on 15% of the price or of the valor real, whichever is higher.

Does Uruguay still have a 10- or 11-year tax holiday for new residents?

In a changed form. Under Law 20.446, people who become tax residents from January 1, 2026 can opt to pay non-resident income tax on foreign investment income for the year they become resident plus ten more, if they spend more than 183 days a year in Uruguay or meet an investment test such as more than UI 12.5 million in real estate, and were not tax residents in the previous two years.
