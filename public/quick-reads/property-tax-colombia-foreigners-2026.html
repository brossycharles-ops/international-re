Property Tax in Colombia for Foreigners 2026

By Charles Brossy May 31, 2026 3 min read

Colombia treats foreign property owners exactly like citizens for tax purposes — no surcharge, no nationality-based restrictions. The annual Impuesto Predial Unificado (predial) runs 0.5%–1.2% of cadastral value for residential properties in Medellín and Bogotá. Here is everything you need to budget correctly in 2026.

The Annual Predial: How It Works

Colombia's predial is a municipal tax calculated on valor catastral — the government's assessed value of the property. In high-demand neighborhoods like El Poblado (Medellín) or Chapinero (Bogotá), cadastral value typically runs 20–40% below market price, which keeps annual bills lower than the headline rate suggests.

Current residential predial rates by city:

  • Medellín: 0.5%–1.2% of cadastral value per year for residential properties
  • Bogotá: 0.7%–1.1% of cadastral value per year for residential properties
  • Cartagena & other municipalities: typically 0.4%–1.0%, set locally

On a $150,000 apartment in El Poblado with a cadastral value of $100,000, your annual predial bill would be roughly $700–$1,200 — a fraction of what comparable properties cost to hold in the US or Europe.

The Early-Payment Discount

Most Colombian municipalities offer a 10% discount if you pay your predial before the deadline — typically March or April. In Medellín the 2026 early-payment window runs through mid-April; in Bogotá, through March. Miss the window and you pay the full amount, but there is no penalty until late in the year. Set a calendar reminder: the 10% discount is free money.

Valorización: The Tax Most Buyers Miss

Beyond the annual predial, Colombian municipalities can levy a one-time contribución de valorización — a special assessment charged when public infrastructure (roads, metro lines, parks) is built near your property. Medellín has used this tool aggressively as it expanded its metro and cable-car network.

Valorización is not annual and not predictable, but it can be substantial — sometimes running $5,000–$20,000 on a single property when a major project is approved. Ask your notario to confirm whether any valorización charges are outstanding before you close, and check whether new infrastructure projects are planned for the neighborhood you are buying in.

Closing Costs at Purchase

One-time taxes at closing add to the picture:

  • Registro (transfer tax): ~1.67% of purchase price, paid to the local registro office
  • Notary fees: ~0.3% split between buyer and seller
  • Legal fees: typically 1–2% depending on complexity

All-in, expect closing costs of 3–4% of purchase price — competitive within Latin America.

Capital Gains on Sale

If you sell a Colombian property, the ganancia ocasional (capital gains) tax is 15% on the gain above the registered purchase price. Accurate purchase-price registration at notarization protects your basis — underreporting to reduce stamp duty at closing is common but creates a larger tax liability on exit. Document everything at fair market value.

Bottom Line

Colombia's annual property tax is low, foreigners get no special penalty, and the early-payment discount rewards organized owners. The one cost to stress-test is valorización — particularly in Medellín, where infrastructure investment has been rapid. Budget for it as a contingency, ask questions before you close, and your ongoing holding costs should remain well under 1.5% of market value per year.
