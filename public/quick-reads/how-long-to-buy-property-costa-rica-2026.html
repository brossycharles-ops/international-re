How Long Does It Take to Buy Property in Costa Rica?

By Charles Brossy October 16, 2026 3 min read

How long does it take to buy property in Costa Rica? No official source gives an end-to-end figure. The closest benchmark is the World Bank's Doing Business 2020 profile (data collected in May 2019; the series was discontinued in 2021): the five official steps to transfer an already-registered, dispute-free property in San José took 11 days. It doesn't count negotiating, due diligence or arranging finance, so your full timeline depends mostly on those.

The Steps, in Order

  1. Offer and option contract. Buyer and seller agree on price and terms, and a deposit is commonly placed in escrow. This stage is outside the World Bank benchmark and has no official duration.
  2. Municipal tax clearance. The seller gets a certificate from the municipality showing property taxes and charges are paid: 1 day in the benchmark (see how Costa Rica property tax works).
  3. Title study. The seller or a notary pulls a literal certification from the Registro Nacional showing liens and mortgages, plus the cadastral plan. The World Bank recorded this as an online step taking less than a day; a full title study by your own attorney is a separate piece of work.
  4. Escritura before a notary. In the benchmark, drafting the public deed took 2 days. Costa Rican notaries are lawyers, and the law requires them to study the property.
  5. Transfer tax and stamps. These are paid through Banco de Costa Rica (online or in person) in less than a day. Ley 6999 sets the transfer tax at 1.5%.
  6. Registration. Filing the deed at the national property registry took 7 days in the benchmark. A defective deed is returned for correction and refiling.

The data dates from May 2019 and assumes a clean commercial title: treat it as a minimum.

What Slows It Down

Beach concessions. Under Ley 6043, the first 50 of the 200 coastal metres are public; the other 150 can only be granted by concession, and transferring one needs municipal plus ICT (or land institute) approval. Concessions can't go to foreigners with fewer than five years of residence, or to companies domiciled abroad, formed in Costa Rica by foreigners, or more than 50% foreign-owned, and shares of a concession-holding company can't be transferred to foreigners.

Corporations. If either party is a company, the notary must also check its articles and who has authority to sign.

Financing. No official source sets a duration for loan approval, so ask your lender for its own timeline.

What Foreigners Need

Residency isn't required. Article 19 of the Constitution gives foreigners the same rights as citizens, except where the Constitution or the law sets limits, such as the maritime zone rule above. Foreign parties identify themselves to the notary with a passport. Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need residency to buy property in Costa Rica?

No. Article 19 of the Costa Rican Constitution gives foreigners the same individual and social rights as citizens, with exceptions only where the Constitution or law sets them. Titled property can be bought without residency. The main exception is concession land in the maritime zone.

How long does registration at the Registro Nacional take?

In the World Bank's Doing Business 2020 benchmark, filing the deed at the national property registry took 7 days, and all five official transfer steps took 11 days. That benchmark used May 2019 data and assumed a clean title in San José. No official figure covers due diligence or financing, so the full purchase varies from deal to deal.

What is the transfer tax when buying property in Costa Rica?

Article 8 of Ley 6999 sets the transfer tax at 1.5%. The World Bank profile says it is calculated on the higher of the stated price and the registry value. Registry, municipal and agrarian stamps (0.5%, 0.2% and 0.15%) are added on top.
