Airbnb Income in Montevideo, Uruguay 2026

By Charles Brossy April 26, 2026 · Updated October 30, 2026 5 min read

A typical Montevideo Airbnb earns about US$7,800 a year on AirROI's data for October 2025 to September 2026 (UYU 312,057, roughly US$650 a month), with average occupancy near 44%. Another provider, Airbtics, reports a median about twice that average. Either way, at today's Pocitos prices short-term letting yields roughly 4–8% gross, about the same as a long-term lease, before Airbnb's fee, cleaning and tax.

The Baseline Numbers

Short-term rental data providers scrape Airbnb listings and estimate occupancy, so their figures differ. For Montevideo:

Dollar conversions use AirROI's rate of about 40 pesos per dollar. Treat the gap between providers as the honest uncertainty: underwrite on the lower figure. On AirROI, top-quartile listings achieve at least 71% occupancy and nightly rates of about UYU 3,245 (≈US$81) or more, so a well-run flat in Pocitos, Punta Carretas or Ciudad Vieja can do much better than the average — but the average includes many part-time listings.

Costs come off the top. Airbnb is moving hosts to a single host-only fee of about 15.5% of the booking; the old split fee, where the host paid about 3%, is being phased out. Add cleaning, utilities, furniture, building fees and, if you live abroad, a local manager.

Why Montevideo Holds Up Year-Round

Montevideo is a capital city rather than a beach resort, so it is less seasonal than Punta del Este, and it is close to Argentina: Buquebus's direct ferry from Buenos Aires takes about 2 hours 30 minutes. Uruguay's currency is the peso, but its financial system is partly dollarised (the IMF flags dollarisation as a risk), and apartments are advertised in US dollars, as in the INGAR index below.

Punta del Este: Higher Rates, Sharper Seasonality

Punta del Este charges much more per night — about US$150 on Airbtics and about UYU 7,833 (≈US$196) on AirROI — but its occupancy is low outside summer. AirROI measures 35.5% average occupancy over the year, peaking at 66% in January 2026 and falling to about 20% in August; Airbtics puts it at 47%. Underwrite a Punta del Este flat on the full-year average, not on January.

What Buyers Are Paying, and the Yield

Apartments in Pocitos and Punta Carretas, the barrios most short-term renters look at, ask a median of US$3,956 and US$4,184 per m² in the Índice INGAR for September 2026. At the Pocitos median a 50 m² apartment costs about US$198,000. AirROI's average income of about US$7,800 gives a gross yield of about 3.9%; Airbtics' US$16,000 gives about 8%. INGAR estimates a 5.3% gross yield for a long-term lease in Pocitos (5.1% in Punta Carretas), so short-term letting only pays clearly better if you beat the average. Corrected October 28 and 30, 2026: earlier versions quoted $1,200–$2,200 per m² and unsourced income, occupancy and fee figures.

Foreign buyers are treated like Uruguayans: the US State Department notes that Uruguay's law treats foreign and national investment equally and most investments need no prior authorisation (only foreign governments are barred from buying land).

Tax and Registration

  • Income tax: rent from Uruguayan property is taxed at 12% — under IRPF if you are tax resident, under IRNR if you are not (25% if you own through a company in a low- or no-tax jurisdiction).
  • Platform reporting: from July 1, 2026, DGI Resolution 1518/2026 requires platforms that intermediate property rentals, such as Airbnb, to report the transactions to the tax office; the first quarterly reports, for July–December 2026, are due in February 2027.
  • Tourism registry: Law 20.352 (2024) requires owners or operators of properties rented for tourist use to file a sworn declaration with the Ministry of Tourism's Registro de Operadores Turísticos and show the registration number in every listing; hosts must also send guests’ identity details to the Ministry of the Interior. When we checked in October 2026 we found no published implementing decree (the draft would give operators 180 days to comply) — confirm the current position with the Ministry of Tourism before you list.
  • Building rules: we found no Montevideo city cap on short-term rentals, but your building's co-ownership rules can restrict them — read them before you buy.

FAQ: Airbnb Income in Montevideo

How much does an Airbnb earn in Montevideo?

Estimates differ by data provider. AirROI puts average revenue at about UYU 312,000 a year (roughly US$7,800, October 2025 to September 2026) with about 44% occupancy, while Airbtics reports a median of about US$16,000 a year with 65% occupancy. Top-quartile listings on AirROI earn at least UYU 58,000 (about US$1,450) a month.

Is Punta del Este better than Montevideo for Airbnb?

Punta del Este charges far more per night (about US$150 on Airbtics, about US$196 on AirROI) but is highly seasonal: AirROI shows 35.5% average occupancy, peaking at 66% in January 2026. Montevideo earns less per night with steadier demand.

Do I need to register a short-term rental in Uruguay?

Law 20.352 of 2024 requires owners of tourist-use properties to file a sworn declaration with the Ministry of Tourism's Registro de Operadores Turísticos and show the registration number in listings. Under DGI Resolution 1518/2026, platforms such as Airbnb must report rental transactions from July 2026, with the first reports due in February 2027. Rental income is generally taxed at 12%.
