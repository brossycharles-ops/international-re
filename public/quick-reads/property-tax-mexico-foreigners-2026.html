Property Tax in Mexico for Foreigners 2026

By Charles Brossy June 7, 2026 · Updated October 27, 2026 5 min read

Mexico charges foreigners the same annual property tax (predial) as Mexican citizens: the municipal tax laws set rates by property type and value, not by nationality. As of October 2026, the statutory rates for built homes are about 0.12% to 0.31% a year in Los Cabos, Playa del Carmen, Tulum and Puerto Vallarta, while Mexico City uses a progressive tariff that works out to roughly 0.5% to 1.25% of cadastral value (for values of MXN 500,000 to 10 million) before any reductions. The larger one-time cost is the acquisition tax at closing: 3% to 4% in the resort towns and about 4% to 6% in Mexico City.

How Predial Works

Predial is set by each municipality (in Mexico City, by the Código Fiscal de la Ciudad de México) and is usually billed in six bimonthly installments, with a discount for paying the whole year in advance. The taxable base is the official cadastral value, and in the Quintana Roo resort towns it is the highest of the cadastral, bank-appraisal, declared or rent-based value. Statutory rates as of October 2026:

  • Mexico City (CDMX): a progressive bimonthly tariff of 16 brackets (Código Fiscal CDMX art. 130). Applied to a cadastral value of MXN 1,000,000 it gives about MXN 6,180 a year (0.62%); at MXN 3,000,000, about MXN 25,450 a year (0.85%), roughly US$1,370. These are tariff amounts before any reduction or subsidy the city grants.
  • Playa del Carmen: a rate of 0.0019 (0.19%) for built urban property (Ley de Hacienda del Municipio de Playa del Carmen, art. 14).
  • Tulum: a rate of 0.0017 (0.17%) for built urban property (Ley de Hacienda del Municipio de Tulum, art. 32).
  • Los Cabos: 1.15331 per thousand (about 0.12%) on cadastral value for an owner-occupied home, 2.2997 per thousand (0.23%) for a home rented out, and 3.0663 per thousand (0.31%) for other uses (Ley de Hacienda para el Municipio de Los Cabos, art. 29).
  • Puerto Vallarta: a progressive bimonthly tariff on fiscal value with marginal rates of 0.025% to 0.045% per bimester (0.15% to 0.27% a year). A fiscal value of MXN 4,000,000 gives about MXN 7,030 a year (0.18%), roughly US$380 (Ley de Ingresos del Municipio de Puerto Vallarta 2026, art. 38).

Early-payment discounts for paying the full year up front, as set in each law: CDMX 8% in January and 5% in February; Playa del Carmen up to 25% by January 31 and up to 20% in February; Tulum up to 25% by January 31 and up to 15% in February; Puerto Vallarta 15% in January, 10% in February and 5% in March and April. US dollar figures on this page use the Banco de México FIX rate of MXN 18.5798 per dollar (October 26, 2026).

The Fideicomiso: The Real Annual Cost for Coastal Buyers

Mexico's constitution (article 27) bars foreigners from acquiring direct ownership of land in a strip 100 km along the borders and 50 km along the coast. If your property falls inside this restricted zone (Cancún, Playa del Carmen, Tulum, Los Cabos, Puerto Vallarta, etc.), you hold it through a fideicomiso, a bank trust that names you as beneficiary and requires a permit from the Secretaría de Relaciones Exteriores.

Under the Ley de Inversión Extranjera, the trust lasts up to 50 years and can be extended at the beneficiary's request (art. 13), and it gives you the right to use and enjoy the property, including renting it out and keeping the income (art. 12). The trustee bank charges a setup fee and an annual fee; these are set by each bank, not by law, so ask for the fees in writing before you choose a bank.

Outside the restricted zone (Mexico City, Guadalajara, San Miguel de Allende and most inland cities), foreigners can hold property directly in their own name with no trust, after filing the agreement with the Secretaría de Relaciones Exteriores that article 27 and article 10 A of the Ley de Inversión Extranjera require.

Acquisition Tax at Closing (ISAI)

Annual predial is separate from the one-time acquisition tax the buyer pays at closing. It is set locally and, as of October 2026, it is:

  • Playa del Carmen and Tulum: a flat 4% of the highest of the price and the official or expert appraisals.
  • Los Cabos: 3% of the property value, after a small deduction of five times the annual UMA.
  • Puerto Vallarta: a progressive tariff with marginal rates of 2.65% to 3.55%; about 2.9% on a MXN 3,000,000 property.
  • Mexico City: a progressive tariff (Código Fiscal CDMX art. 113): about 4.4% at MXN 1,000,000, 5.2% at MXN 3,000,000 and 6% at MXN 10,000,000.

Notary, registry and appraisal fees come on top and vary by notary and city, so ask the notary for a written closing-cost estimate. To compare Mexico with 10 other countries, see our Latin America closing costs table.

Taxes When You Rent Out or Sell

A non-resident who rents out Mexican property pays income tax (ISR) of 25% of the gross rent with no deductions (Ley del ISR art. 158). When a non-resident sells, the tax is 25% of the gross sale price, or, if the sale is made before a notary, the seller can opt to pay the top individual rate, 35%, on the net gain (art. 160). Mexican tax residents selling their own home are exempt on proceeds up to 700,000 UDIs (art. 93, fraction XIX), about MXN 6.2 million (roughly US$335,000) at Banco de México's UDI value of 8.893123 for November 8, 2026, provided they have not used the exemption in the previous three years.

Bottom Line

In the resort towns, annual predial is low: rates of roughly 0.12% to 0.31% of the taxable value. Mexico City's tariff is steeper, at roughly 0.5% to 1.25% of cadastral value before reductions. Coastal buyers also pay the fideicomiso bank's annual fee, which you should get in writing. The bigger budget item is the one-time acquisition tax of 3% to 6% at closing, plus notary fees, not the recurring tax.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do foreigners pay more property tax in Mexico?

No. Predial rates are set by each municipality's tax law by property type and value, not by nationality, so foreign owners pay the same predial as Mexicans. As of October 2026 the rate for built homes is about 0.12% to 0.31% a year in Los Cabos, Playa del Carmen, Tulum and Puerto Vallarta; Mexico City's progressive tariff works out to roughly 0.5% to 1.25% of cadastral value before reductions.

How much is the acquisition tax when buying property in Mexico?

It is set locally. As of October 2026 it is a flat 4% in Playa del Carmen and Tulum, 3% in Los Cabos, a progressive 2.65% to 3.55% in Puerto Vallarta, and a progressive tariff of about 4.4% to 6% in Mexico City for properties of MXN 1 million to 10 million. Notary and registry fees come on top.

What tax does a non-resident pay when selling or renting out property in Mexico?

Under the Ley del ISR, a non-resident seller pays 25% of the gross sale price, or can opt to pay 35% on the net gain when the sale is made before a notary. Rental income of a non-resident is taxed at 25% of the gross rent with no deductions. The home-sale exemption of up to 700,000 UDIs applies to Mexican tax residents.
