Property Tax in Mexico for Foreigners 2026

By Charles Brossy June 7, 2026 3 min read

Mexico charges foreigners the same annual property tax (predial) as Mexican citizens — typically 0.1% to 0.3% of cadastral value per year, which translates to a few hundred dollars annually on most properties. The real hidden cost for coastal buyers is the fideicomiso bank trust, which adds roughly $600 per year. Here is the full picture for 2026.

How Predial Works

Predial is set by each municipality and calculated on cadastral value — the government's assessed figure, which typically runs 20–40% below market price in major cities. Effective annual rates across Mexico's most popular expat markets:

  • Mexico City (CDMX): roughly 0.1% of cadastral value; a $250,000 USD condo typically generates an annual tax bill of $150–$400 USD
  • Playa del Carmen / Tulum: 0.15%–0.25% of cadastral value; expect $300–$800 USD/year on a mid-market condo
  • Puerto Vallarta / Cabo: similar range, often toward the lower end given cadastral assessments that lag behind resort-market price appreciation

Most municipalities offer an early-payment discount of 10–25% if you pay in January or February, making it worth scheduling that payment at the start of the year.

The Fideicomiso: The Real Annual Cost for Coastal Buyers

Mexico's constitution bars direct foreign ownership within the zona restringida — a strip running 50 km from any coastline and 100 km from any land border. If your property falls inside this zone (Cancún, Playa del Carmen, Los Cabos, Puerto Vallarta, etc.), you must hold it through a fideicomiso, a bank trust that names you as the beneficiary.

The fideicomiso itself is renewable every 50 years and does not limit what you can do with the property. The cost: a one-time setup fee of roughly $1,000–$1,500 USD, then an annual maintenance fee of $500–$700 USD paid to the trust bank. For many investors this fee is simply baked into operating costs alongside HOA and predial.

Properties outside the restricted zone — Mexico City, Guadalajara, San Miguel de Allende, and most inland cities — can be held directly in your name with no trust required.

Acquisition Tax at Closing (ISAI)

Annual predial is separate from the one-time Impuesto Sobre Adquisición de Inmuebles (ISAI) paid at closing. ISAI is set by each state and typically runs 2%–4% of the purchase price. Add notary fees, registration, and legal costs and total closing costs land around 5–7% of purchase price — higher than Panama or Uruguay, but comparable to Colombia.

Bottom Line

For ongoing annual costs, Mexico is one of the most tax-efficient markets in Latin America. Predial on a $250,000 property is rarely more than a few hundred dollars per year. Coastal buyers add a fideicomiso fee that typically runs $550–$650/year — annoying but manageable. The bigger budget line item is the one-time 5–7% closing cost, not the recurring tax.
