Chile Retirement Visa (Rentista) Requirements 2026: No Fixed Income Figure

By Charles Brossy October 29, 2026 3 min read

Chile's retirement visa requirements in 2026 come down to one test: a pension, or steady rental or investment income, that covers at least your basic needs in Chile, as measured by Ministry of Social Development and Family indicators. Neither Decree 177 of 2022 nor the Servicio Nacional de Migraciones (SERMIG) publishes a fixed minimum amount. You apply online from abroad for a temporary residence permit of up to two years.

Who Qualifies

The permit is the "jubilados y rentistas" subcategory of temporary residence (Decree 177, arts. 53 and 57). It covers:

  • Retirees: anyone who has obtained a retirement pension under the rules of their country of residence.
  • Rentistas: anyone with steady income from real estate or financial assets.

The money must arrive regularly. Monthly figures on relocation blogs are not official; SERMIG judges what is enough.

Documents and Where to Apply

Apply from outside Chile on SERMIG's Portal de Trámites Digitales. Upload a passport valid for a year or more, a criminal record certificate no older than 60 days, a photo and proof of income: retirees send a pension certificate stating amount and duration plus the latest payment slip; rentistas send proof of ownership, the lease where relevant, and proof of the income. Foreign documents must be apostilled or legalised, and translated unless in Spanish or English. Once approved, you have 90 days to enter Chile. The fee varies by nationality.

Renewal, Permanent Residence and Tax

The permit lasts up to two years (Exempt Decree 4,236 of 2022) and can be extended in two-year steps if you still prove the income. After 24 months you may apply for permanent residence (residencia definitiva); SERMIG can cut that to 12 months where you have pensions or rents, and raises it to 30–48 months if you spent over two months abroad. The definitiva fee is CLP 142,726 for most applicants. Chile's tax service (SII) confirms that a foreign resident is taxed only on Chilean-source income for the first three years, then on worldwide income.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much income do I need for Chile's retirement visa?

There is no published official minimum. Decree 177 of 2022 and SERMIG ask for a pension, or regular income from real estate or financial assets, that covers at least your basic needs during your stay, measured against Ministry of Social Development and Family indicators. Monthly figures quoted on relocation blogs are not official thresholds.

Can I apply for Chile's retiree visa from inside Chile?

As a rule, no. The jubilados y rentistas permit must be requested online from abroad through SERMIG's Portal de Trámites Digitales. The exception specific to this permit (Decree 177, art. 55) is a pensioner who already holds a temporary residence permit for paid work, who can switch from inside Chile.

How long until I can get permanent residence in Chile?

Usually after 24 months as a temporary resident. SERMIG can reduce that to 12 months where you have pensions or rents available, and raise it to 30–48 months if you spent more than two months outside Chile. The permanent residence fee is CLP 142,726 for most applicants.
