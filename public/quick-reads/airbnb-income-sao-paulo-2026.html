Airbnb Income in São Paulo 2026

By Charles Brossy May 18, 2026 3 min read

São Paulo is Latin America's largest city and its financial capital — yet it remains deeply undercovered in international real estate conversations. According to Airbtics and AirROI data, the median São Paulo Airbnb listing generates approximately $596/month in gross revenue, with top-quartile properties crossing $988 and the best 10% reaching $1,490 or more. Here's how to read those numbers.

Rates and Occupancy at a Glance

City-wide, São Paulo listings average $58 per night with an occupancy rate of roughly 58% — around 17–18 booked nights per month. Run the math and a typical well-managed apartment earns $580–$650/month at the median before platform fees and cleaning. Higher-end properties in prime neighborhoods push well above that.

The relatively low average ADR compared to coastal markets like Cartagena or Playa del Carmen reflects São Paulo's positioning as a business and culture hub rather than a leisure destination. But it also signals real upside: properties that serve corporate short-stays and design-week visitors consistently outperform the blended average.

Where the Income Is

Jardins / Jardim Paulista: São Paulo's most affluent bairro, walking distance from Paulista Avenue. Apartments here command premium ADRs — $80–$120/night — from business travelers and international visitors. Tight supply keeps occupancy high year-round.

Vila Madelena: The city's bohemian creative district draws younger travelers and conference attendees for SPFW (São Paulo Fashion Week) and SP-Arte. ADRs of $55–$85/night with strong occupancy spikes around major events.

Itaim Bibi: Corporate São Paulo's home base. Properties near the financial district capture a steady stream of business travelers, many of whom book stays of 5–14 nights — reducing turnover costs and vacancy risk.

What Foreign Investors Should Know

Brazil permits full foreign ownership of urban real estate. Acquisition costs — ITBI transfer tax, notary, and registration — typically run 4–6% of purchase price. São Paulo's short-term rental market is regulated but not restricted; hosts must register with the city, and HOA rules in some buildings prohibit sub-30-day lets, so due diligence on the condominium bylaws is essential before buying.

Prime Jardins apartments trade at $2,500–$4,000/m² in USD terms. At median rental income, gross yields run 5–7% — modest on paper, but São Paulo's dollar-priced assets have historically appreciated as the BRL weakens, offering currency-adjusted upside for USD-denominated buyers.
