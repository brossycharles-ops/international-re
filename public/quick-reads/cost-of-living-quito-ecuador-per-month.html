Cost of Living in Quito Ecuador Per Month (2026)

By Charles Brossy May 24, 2026 3 min read

Quito is one of the most underrated expat cities in Latin America. The Ecuadorian capital sits at 2,850 metres in the Andes, enjoys a year-round spring climate, and runs entirely on the US dollar — meaning zero currency risk. A single expat living well here in 2026 spends roughly $1,500–$1,800 per month all-in, according to current Numbeo and Expatistan data. A couple comfortable with the local pace can land around $2,200–$2,800. Here is what that budget looks like in practice.

Rent: The Biggest Variable

Housing drives most of the spread in expat budgets. In La Floresta — Quito's most walkable expat-friendly neighbourhood, known for cafés, galleries, and good restaurants — a furnished one-bedroom apartment runs $400–$600/month. A furnished two-bedroom in the same area goes for $550–$750.

Cumbayá, a leafy suburb east of the city popular with professionals and families, is priced higher: furnished two-bedrooms there typically run $600–$900. For retirees who prefer altitude and green space over city access, Cumbayá offers more space per dollar than any comparable neighbourhood in Colombia or Peru.

Food and Groceries

Ecuador grows an enormous variety of produce domestically, which keeps grocery bills low. Shopping at local mercados — Quito's Mercado Central or neighbourhood markets — a single person spends $80–$130/month on fresh food. Add imported goods and a full supermarket run and the number rises to $180–$260. Most expats blend both and land around $220–$350/month total for groceries and dining out combined.

A set lunch (almuerzo) at a local restaurant costs $2.50–$4 and includes soup, main, juice, and dessert. Dinner at a mid-range restaurant in La Floresta runs $10–$18 per person.

Transport

Quito's integrated bus network (Metrobús-Q) and new metro line cost $0.35 per ride. A monthly pass for unlimited travel is around $15. Uber operates throughout the city; a crosstown trip rarely exceeds $4–$6. A car is unnecessary in central Quito and adds $120–$200/month in running costs if you choose one.

Healthcare

Ecuador's private healthcare system is genuinely affordable. A GP consultation at a private clinic costs $25–$50; specialist visits run $50–$90. International-standard private hospitals include Hospital Metropolitano and Hospital de los Valles. A private health insurance policy for a healthy adult under 55 starts around $80–$120/month — roughly half the cost of equivalent coverage in Mexico City or Santiago.

Full Monthly Budget Snapshot

  • Rent (1-bed, La Floresta): $450–$600
  • Groceries + dining out: $250–$380
  • Transport: $15–$50
  • Health insurance: $80–$120
  • Utilities (electric, water, internet): $80–$130
  • Entertainment, misc: $150–$250
  • Total (single person): $1,025–$1,530

Add a comfortable buffer for travel, dining upgrades, and the occasional splurge and most expats settle into a $1,500–$1,800/month reality — well below comparable quality of life in Medellín or Lima.

The Dollar Advantage

Ecuador adopted the US dollar in 2000 and has used it ever since. For North American retirees and investors, this eliminates the exchange-rate risk that complicates budgeting in Colombia, Argentina, or Brazil. Rental agreements, property purchases, and everyday spending are all conducted in dollars — what you budget is what you pay.

Ecuador also offers one of the region's most accessible retirement visas: the Pensioner Visa requires just $800/month in guaranteed pension income, a lower threshold than Panama ($1,000) or Costa Rica ($1,000). For investors, Ecuador's capital gains rules and low property-acquisition costs make it an increasingly interesting entry-level market.
