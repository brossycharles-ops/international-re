Peru Retirement Visa (Rentista) Requirements 2026

By Charles Brossy October 27, 2026 3 min read

Peru's retirement visa, the rentista residente status, requires proof of a permanent net income of at least US$1,000 a month when the pension or income comes from abroad (Peruvian-source income has no published minimum), according to Migraciones' official procedure pages on gob.pe. The status gives an indefinite stay in Peru; you renew the foreigner ID card (carné de extranjería) every four years, not the status itself. Here is what the 2026 rules ask for.

Who Qualifies

Peru's Migration Law (Legislative Decree 1350, article 29.2) defines the rentista as a foreigner who receives a retirement pension or permanent income from a Peruvian or foreign source. The regulation (Supreme Decree 007-2017-IN, article 92) leaves the amount to Migraciones, so the US$1,000 figure appears on Migraciones' procedure pages rather than in the law itself; check gob.pe again before you apply. If the income is from abroad, you also sign a sworn statement that the money enters Peru through a bank or financial entity supervised by the SBS, Peru's banking regulator.

Two Ways to Apply

  1. From outside Peru (visa route): apply online through Migraciones' Agencia Digital while you are abroad. You need the application form, a copy of your valid passport, a certificate of no judicial, criminal or police record for the last five years, the income document and the bank sworn statement. The fee is S/ 58.80 (code 07567 at Banco de la Nación). Once approved, you collect the visa at a Peruvian consulate within 30 days, and it is valid for six months.
  2. Inside Peru (change of status): if you are already in Peru, you request a change of immigration status. The income certificate must be notarised or authenticated by Migraciones and legalised by the Peruvian consulate or apostilled, and Migraciones also asks for an Interpol record check (ficha de canje internacional) no older than six months. The fee is S/ 161.40 (code 07568).

Migraciones lists a processing time of 30 business days for both routes. Translation, apostille and notary costs are extra.

Rules After You Arrive

The stay is indefinite, but the carné de extranjería is valid for four years and must be renewed. Under article 33 of the Migration Law, residents other than permanent residents lose their status if they spend more than 183 consecutive days outside Peru within 365 days, unless Migraciones authorises the absence for a justified reason. Neither the law nor the regulation says whether a rentista may take paid work in Peru, so ask Migraciones before you plan on it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much income do I need for Peru's retirement visa?

Migraciones' procedure pages ask for proof of a permanent net income of at least US$1,000 a month when the pension or income comes from abroad; income from a Peruvian source has no published minimum. The figure is set by Migraciones, not written into the Migration Law, so check gob.pe before applying.

How long is Peru's rentista residence valid?

The stay is indefinite. The foreigner ID card (carné de extranjería) is valid for four years and must be renewed, and residents lose the status if they spend more than 183 consecutive days outside Peru within 365 days without authorisation.

How much does the Peru rentista visa cost?

Migraciones charges S/ 58.80 to apply from abroad and S/ 161.40 to change to rentista status inside Peru, paid at Banco de la Nación. Notary, translation and apostille costs are extra.
