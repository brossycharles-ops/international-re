Cost of Living in Medellín Per Month (2026)

By Charles Brossy May 22, 2026 3 min read

Medellín is routinely ranked among the world's top cities for digital nomads and retirees — and the numbers justify the hype. According to Numbeo's May 2026 data, the citywide average rent for an unfurnished one-bedroom apartment is approximately $634/month. A single expat living well in El Poblado or Laureles typically spends $1,000–$1,500/month all-in. Here is exactly where that money goes.

Rent: The Neighborhood Makes All the Difference

Medellín's rental market splits cleanly by barrio. El Poblado is the default expat landing zone — walkable, full of cafés and coworking spaces, and reliably safe. A furnished one-bedroom here runs $700–$1,200/month depending on building amenities and floor; unfurnished long-term leases are $500–$900. Expect to pay a premium for El Poblado's convenience.

Laureles offers the best value for expats willing to trade some walkability. Long-term unfurnished one-bedrooms go for $400–$600/month — roughly 30–40% less than El Poblado for comparable space. The neighbourhood has a strong local feel, excellent restaurants, and a growing coffee-shop scene around Avenida El Poblado and Parque Laureles.

Envigado, technically a separate municipality but effectively merged with the city, has become the quiet alternative: mid-rises, green streets, and rents 20–30% below El Poblado with a short Metro ride into the centre.

Food and Dining

Eating local is the biggest lever on your budget. A comida corriente — the set lunch of soup, protein, rice, and juice that is Colombia's daily staple — costs $3–$5 at a neighbourhood restaurant. Grocery shopping at Éxito or Jumbo for a week's worth of staples runs $30–$50 for one person. Total monthly food spend for a single expat who cooks some meals and eats out a few times per week typically lands at $250–$400.

International restaurants, rooftop bars, and the growing fine-dining scene in El Poblado and Laureles push costs higher — a dinner with drinks at a mid-range spot runs $20–$35 per person. Budget accordingly if that is part of your lifestyle.

Transport

Medellín's Metro system is one of the best-run urban transit networks in Latin America. A single Metro ride costs COP 3,200 (≈ $0.78); the integrated system includes the Metrocable gondolas connecting hillside comunas to the main line. A monthly transit card covering unlimited Metro, bus, and cable rides costs around $25–$30. Uber and InDriver operate across the city; a typical 15-minute ride costs $2–$4. Car ownership is rarely necessary and adds $150–$250/month in fuel and parking.

Healthcare

Private healthcare in Medellín is affordable and high-quality. A GP consultation at a private clinic costs $15–$40; specialist visits run $30–$70. Medellín is home to Clínica Las Américas and Clínica del Norte, two private hospitals with internationally trained staff. Monthly private health insurance for a healthy adult under 50 typically runs $50–$100 — a fraction of comparable coverage in North America or Europe.

The Full Monthly Picture

  • Rent (1-bed, Laureles): $450–$600
  • Rent (1-bed, El Poblado furnished): $700–$1,200
  • Groceries + dining: $250–$400
  • Transport (Metro + Uber): $30–$60
  • Health insurance: $50–$100
  • Utilities (electric, water, internet): $60–$100
  • Entertainment, misc: $150–$250
  • Total (single person, mid-tier): $1,000–$1,500

What the Numbers Don't Capture

Medellín's climate is the famous "City of Eternal Spring" — temperatures stay between 17°C and 28°C year-round, which means near-zero air conditioning or heating costs. That alone knocks $100–$200/month off utility bills compared to tropical coastal cities like Cartagena or Bogotá's cold altitude.

Colombia's peso has been volatile against the dollar, which cuts both ways: it compresses expat costs when the dollar strengthens, but adds currency risk for investors holding peso-denominated assets. For property investors, rental yields in Medellín's prime zones run 6–9%, with appreciation in El Poblado averaging 5–8% annually over the past three years.
