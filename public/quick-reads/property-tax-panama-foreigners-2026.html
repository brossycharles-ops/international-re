Property Tax in Panama for Foreigners 2026

By Charles Brossy May 13, 2026 · Updated October 17, 2026 3 min read

Panama treats foreign property owners exactly like citizens when it comes to annual tax — no surcharge and no separate rates for foreigners. Primary residences valued under $120,000 pay zero property tax, and the top rate on other property is 1% a year. Rates are set by Ley 66 of 2017 and have applied since January 1, 2019. Here is the full picture for 2026, checked against the law and the tax authority (DGI) on October 17, 2026.

The Rate Table

Panama's property tax is levied on the property's registered (cadastral) value, not its market price. Rates are marginal: each one applies only to the slice of value in its band. The standard rates for property that is not your registered primary residence (second homes, rentals) are:

  • Up to $30,000: 0% (exempt)
  • $30,001 – $250,000: 0.6% per year
  • $250,001 – $500,000: 0.8% per year
  • Above $500,000: 1% per year

Register the property as your primary residence (patrimonio familiar tributario / vivienda principal, by sworn declaration to the DGI) and the rates drop:

  • Up to $120,000: 0% (exempt)
  • $120,001 – $700,000: 0.5% per year
  • Above $700,000: 0.7% per year

Example: a condo with a registered value of $200,000 pays 0.5% on $80,000, about $400 a year, as a primary residence. As a rental it pays 0.6% on $170,000, about $1,020 a year. The tax is paid in three instalments, due by April 30, August 31 and December 31. An unpaid balance stays attached to the property, so have your attorney confirm the seller is up to date before closing.

What Changed for New Builds

The long property-tax holidays on new construction (20, 10 or 5 years on improvements under Ley 28 of 2012) only apply to buildings whose construction permit was issued before January 1, 2019. For later projects, Ley 66 of 2017 allows a narrower relief: a first home purchase registered as your primary residence, worth US$120,000 to US$300,000, is exempt for 3 years. Ley 468 of April 2025 did not change property tax; it repealed the first-sale exemption from the 2% transfer tax. Ley 472 of June 2025 postponed the repeal, so the exemption lapsed on January 1, 2026. Ley 546 of August 31, 2026 brought back partial relief: on the first sale of a new home priced up to US$200,000, the first US$120,000 of the taxable value is exempt and only the excess is taxed, at 0.5–1.8%. Homes above US$200,000 get no relief.

Transfer Tax and Closing Costs

Annual property tax is only part of the picture. When a property changes hands in Panama, the seller pays a 2% transfer tax on the higher of the purchase price or the registered value (cadastral value plus improvements, increased 5% for each full year the seller has owned it), plus attorney fees of about 2%, notary fees of about 0.1% and a registry fee of about 0.3%. The transfer tax is legally the seller's and a private agreement cannot shift it to the buyer, so buyer-side costs are about 2–3%, though sellers may build the tax into the price. The full breakdown is in our Panama legal guide.

Bottom Line

Panama's annual property tax stays low. Most new builds no longer come with long tax holidays, but ongoing annual rates are still a fraction of what US or European investors are used to. Combined with dollarization and straightforward foreign ownership rules, the fiscal case for Panama property is intact.

FAQ: Panama Property Tax for Foreigners

Do foreigners pay more property tax in Panama?

No. Foreign owners pay exactly the same rates as Panamanians. A registered primary residence pays 0% up to US$120,000 of registered value, 0.5% up to US$700,000 and 0.7% above. Other property pays 0% up to US$30,000, then 0.6%, 0.8% and 1% in bands.

When is Panama property tax due?

Panama's property tax is paid in three instalments each year, due by April 30, August 31 and December 31, according to Ley 66 of 2017 and the DGI. Unpaid tax stays attached to the property, so check the seller's account before you buy.

Do new homes in Panama still get a property tax exemption?

Only in limited cases. The 20-, 10- and 5-year exemptions apply only to buildings permitted before January 1, 2019. For newer projects, a first home registered as your primary residence with a value of US$120,000 to US$300,000 is exempt for 3 years.
