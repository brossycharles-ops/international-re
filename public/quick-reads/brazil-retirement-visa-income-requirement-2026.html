Brazil Retirement Visa Income Requirement 2026: US$2,000 a Month

By Charles Brossy October 28, 2026 3 min read

The Brazil retirement visa income requirement in 2026 is US$2,000 a month: retirees and survivor-pension holders must prove that at least that much in foreign currency is transferred to Brazil every month (CNIg Normative Resolution 40/2019, Art. 1). Other regular income can top up a smaller pension, and the first residence lasts up to two years.

The Rule in Brief

  • Income: a retirement pension or survivor's pension (pensão por morte) of at least US$2,000 a month, transferred to Brazil. Other regular income may complement it if needed (Art. 3).
  • Term: initial residence of up to two years, whether you apply at a consulate or from inside Brazil (Arts. 3 and 4). RN 40 leaves renewal to a separate resolution; the general rule in Decree 9,199/2017 allows renewal for up to two more years or a switch to indefinite residence, so confirm the current procedure with the Federal Police before your term ends.
  • Family: RN 40 has no dependants clause, so a spouse or children apply separately, usually for a family-reunion visa (Decree 9,199, Art. 45).
  • Status: the Ministry of Justice library lists no revocation of RN 40, and we found no 2026 change to the US$2,000 figure.

Documents and Where to Apply

At a Brazilian consulate you need a valid passport, health insurance valid in Brazil, a criminal record certificate from your country of origin, the visa form, proof of the consular fee and of travel to Brazil, plus a vaccination certificate where Anvisa requires one (Art. 2). If you are already in Brazil, the Ministry of Justice can grant the residence permit directly.

After you arrive on the visa, register with the Federal Police within 90 days to get your national migration card (CRNM). Missing that deadline is a fineable offence under the Migration Law.

Buying Property Before You Move

You do not need residency to buy. Receita Federal requires any foreigner who owns property in Brazil to hold a CPF taxpayer number, which non-residents can request through a consulate. Owning property does not, however, give you a right to a visa or residence (Law 13,445, Art. 35). The US State Department rates Brazil Level 2, "Exercise increased caution", and advises against travel to informal housing communities (favelas). Frequently Asked Questions

How much income do I need to retire in Brazil?

A retirement or survivor's pension of at least US$2,000 a month, transferred to Brazil in foreign currency, under CNIg Normative Resolution 40/2019. Other regular income can top up a smaller pension.

Does the Brazil retiree visa cover my spouse?

Not automatically. RN 40 has no dependants clause, so family members usually apply for a family-reunion visa under Decree 9,199/2017, Art. 45.

Can I buy property in Brazil without residency?

Yes. You need a CPF taxpayer number, which Receita Federal requires of any foreign property owner and which non-residents can get through a consulate. Buying property does not entitle you to a visa.
