Airbnb Rules in Medellín for Foreign Owners (2026)

By Charles Brossy October 17, 2026 3 min read

Yes, a foreign owner can legally rent a Medellín apartment on Airbnb, but three rules decide whether you can. Any rental of under 30 days counts as a vivienda turística, which must be registered in Colombia's Registro Nacional de Turismo (RNT). The building's condo bylaws must expressly allow tourist lodging. And as a non-resident you pay a 35% income tax on the rent. Medellín is now enforcing these rules.

1. Register in the RNT (Free, Renewed Every Year)

Under Ley 300 of 1996 and Ley 2068 of 2020, registering is mandatory before you operate. You apply online through Confecámaras (rnt.confecamaras.co), and individuals can register in their own name. Registration costs nothing, but it must be renewed between January 1 and March 31 each year or it is suspended, and reactivating it costs one monthly minimum wage. Platforms must show your RNT number and must remove listings without an active one. You must also keep a guest register, carry liability insurance and report foreign guests to Migración Colombia.

2. Check the Condo Bylaws Before You Buy

In a propiedad horizontal building, the RNT application requires you to declare that the bylaws (reglamento) authorise tourist lodging. MinCIT says the authorisation should be explicit, recorded in a public deed and registered, and that the building needs mixed residential and commercial land-use zoning. Building administrators must report unauthorised short-term rentals to the Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio (SIC), and the building can impose repeated fines. If you are buying to rent short-term, have your lawyer read the reglamento first.

3. Taxes and Enforcement

Non-residents pay a flat 35% on Colombian-source income, which includes rent. Hosts also owe a tourism contribution of 2.5 per thousand of operating income, and lodging services can be subject to 19% VAT, so get Colombian tax advice. Operating without an RNT can bring SIC fines of up to 2,000 monthly minimum wages. The city reports 408 inspections between 2024 and 2026, with 105 rentals suspended, while registered tourist homes rose from 521 in 2019 to 8,322 in 2026. A proposed zoning plan (POT) change would limit new short-term rentals to mixed-use areas, including zones near metro stations. It was still before the city council as of October 2026.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a foreigner rent out an apartment on Airbnb in Medellín?

Yes, if it is registered in the Registro Nacional de Turismo and the building's bylaws authorise tourist lodging. Registration is free, can be done in your own name, and must be renewed between January 1 and March 31 every year.

What tax does a non-resident pay on Airbnb income in Colombia?

Non-residents pay a flat 35% income tax on Colombian-source income, including rent from property in Colombia. Hosts also owe a 2.5-per-thousand tourism contribution, and VAT can apply to lodging, so take local tax advice.

Is Medellín banning Airbnb?

No. As of September 2026, a proposed change to Medellín's zoning plan would limit new short-term rentals to mixed-use areas such as zones near metro stations, while existing legal operators keep their rights. The planning director said the city intends to regulate, not prohibit. The proposal was still before the city council in October 2026.
