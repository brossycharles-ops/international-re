About Mexico City for Foreign Buyers

Mexico City (CDMX) draws foreign buyers with walkable, tree-lined neighbourhoods such as Roma Norte, Condesa, Polanco and Narvarte. In October 2026 the median asking price for apartments was about MXN 59,700/m² in Narvarte, MXN 64,000/m² in Polanco and MXN 80,400/m² in Roma Norte (roughly $3,200–$4,300 at Banxico's FIX of 18.67). Accumin data put the average new-build price at MXN 80,922/m² in Q2 2026, up 10.8% on a year earlier, and Global Property Guide puts average gross rental yields at about 6.8% (before vacancy, fees, maintenance and tax).

Unlike the coast, CDMX lies outside Mexico's restricted zone (100 km from borders, 50 km from beaches), so foreigners can hold title directly in their own name, with no bank trust (fideicomiso) needed. Under Article 27 of the Constitution and Article 10 A of the Ley de Inversión Extranjera, the buyer first files an agreement with the Secretaría de Relaciones Exteriores (SRE) to be treated as a Mexican national for that property and not to invoke their own government's protection (the Calvo clause), and gets the SRE permit. In a municipality wholly outside the zone, the permit counts as granted if no refusal is published within five business days. The sale is signed as an escritura before a notario público, who calculates the taxes and registers the deed with the Registro Público de la Propiedad de la CDMX, which can take three weeks to three months. The buyer pays the Impuesto sobre Adquisición de Inmuebles (ISAI), a sliding-scale tax under Article 113 of the CDMX Código Fiscal whose amounts were updated from 1 January 2026. It is charged on the highest of the price, the cadastral value and an appraisal, so ask the notario for the exact figure.

Now the risks. The US State Department's May 2026 reissue keeps Mexico City at Level 2, 'Exercise increased caution', citing terrorism, crime and kidnapping, while several other states are at Level 4. The city is seismic: the M7.1 earthquake of 19 September 2017 brought down buildings at 44 sites in the capital, so have your own structural engineer inspect any building, especially older ones and those repaired since. Short-term rental income is capped: under the 2024 reforms, hosts must register with the city's tourism ministry, and a listing occupied more than 50% of the year cannot renew its registration. Liquidity is softening: in Q2 2026 new-home sales fell 6.4% year on year while about 18,000 new units sat on the market, so price realistically and plan for a hold of several years.

Mexico City Real Estate FAQ

Can foreigners buy property in Mexico City?

Yes. Mexico City is outside the restricted zone along the borders and coast, so foreigners can own property directly in their own name, with no fideicomiso. Before buying you file an agreement with the Secretaría de Relaciones Exteriores (the Calvo clause) and get its permit, under Article 27 of the Constitution and Article 10 A of the Foreign Investment Law.

What taxes do you pay when buying in Mexico City?

The main one is the Impuesto sobre Adquisición de Inmuebles (ISAI), which the buyer pays. It is a sliding-scale tax set in Article 113 of the CDMX Código Fiscal, updated from 1 January 2026, and charged on the highest of the purchase price, the cadastral value and an appraisal. The notario calculates it with the registration fees and notary costs at closing.

Is Mexico City safe to buy property in?

The US State Department rates Mexico City Level 2, 'Exercise increased caution', in its May 2026 Mexico advisory, citing terrorism, crime and kidnapping. Beyond crime, check earthquake safety: the 2017 M7.1 quake brought down buildings at 44 sites in the capital, so get an independent structural inspection before you commit.

Can I rent out a Mexico City apartment on Airbnb?

Yes, with limits. Under the 2024 reforms, hosts and platforms must register with Mexico City's tourism ministry, and a listing occupied for more than half the year cannot renew its registration. Don't base a purchase on full-time short-term rental income.
