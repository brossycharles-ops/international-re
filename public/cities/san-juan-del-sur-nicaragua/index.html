About San Juan del Sur for Foreign Buyers

San Juan del Sur is a small Pacific bay town in Nicaragua's Rivas department, and its surf beaches and ocean-view hillsides are what draw most foreign buyers. Homes and condos are listed in US dollars: asking prices on Encuentra24 run from roughly US$1,000 per square metre for modest houses to about US$2,600 at the top end for beachfront condos. Foreigners can hold title in their own name: the US State Department reports no specific restrictions on foreign buyers outside border and national-security areas, and the 2025 constitution protects property in Article 40 while allowing expropriation 'with fair compensation'; the same report documents seizures of foreign-owned property, and in August 2026 the government took nine homes owned by US retirees in nearby Tola.

Coastal land has its own rules. Under Ley 690 of 2009, the Coastal Zones Development Law, the beach between low and high tide plus 50 metres inland from the average high-tide mark is state-owned public-use land where homes are prohibited, and the next 200 metres inland is a restricted zone where the state regulates use. Neither strip can be sold, only granted by concession, although rights legally acquired before the law and already-established urban centres are exempt. Purchases are recorded in the Registro Público de la Propiedad, part of the national registry system set up by Law 698 of 2009. The US Embassy says about 28,000 properties were expropriated between 1979 and 1990, many are still disputed, and it urges extreme caution with coastal property in particular.

The country risk is real. The US State Department rates Nicaragua Level 3, 'Reconsider travel', citing crime, health, wrongful detention and arbitrary enforcement of local laws. In August 2025 the National Assembly passed Law 1258, which declares a 15-kilometre strip along Nicaragua's borders state property, and the State Department called it a 'massive confiscation'. The town itself is roughly 25–30 km from the Costa Rican border, outside the strip, but the municipality's southern beaches (El Ostional, La Flor, Playa El Coco) lie within or close to it, so have a lawyer confirm where any lot sits. Nicaragua publishes no sales-price data and no reliable rental-yield figures, so treat agents' yield claims with caution and plan for a long hold.

San Juan del Sur Real Estate FAQ

Can foreigners buy beachfront property in San Juan del Sur?

Foreigners can own private land in their own name, but not the beach itself. Ley 690 makes the area between the tides plus 50 metres inland from the average high-tide mark public-use state land where homes are banned, and the next 200 metres inland is a restricted zone. Rights legally acquired before 2009 are respected, so your lawyer must check exactly where any lot sits.

How much does property cost in San Juan del Sur?

There is no official price index. Asking prices on Encuentra24 in 2026 range from about US$1,000 per square metre for modest houses to about US$2,600 at the top end for beachfront condos. Treat these as asking prices, not sale prices.

What are the biggest risks of buying in San Juan del Sur?

Title and politics. The US Embassy says about 28,000 properties were expropriated in 1979-1990, many are still disputed, and it urges extreme caution with coastal property. Law 1258 of 2025 also made a 15 km strip along Nicaragua's borders state property: the town is outside it, but the southern beaches such as El Ostional, La Flor and Playa El Coco are within or close to it. Order a full title study at the Registro Público before paying any deposit.

Is it safe to buy property in Nicaragua right now?

The US State Department rates Nicaragua Level 3, 'Reconsider travel', citing crime, health, wrongful detention and arbitrary enforcement of local laws, and the US Embassy urges extreme caution when buying coastal property. Weigh that risk, and the slow resale market, before committing.
