Buying Off-Plan in Chile? The Law Makes Developers Guarantee Your Deposit

By Charles Brossy October 16, 2026 2 min read

Buying off-plan in Chile? Under Article 138 bis of the Ley General de Urbanismo y Construcciones (DFL 458), a developer that takes money under a promesa de compraventa for a home, shop or office without final municipal sign-off (recepción definitiva) must back it with an insurance policy or bank guarantee in your favour, equal to what you paid.

What the Rule Says

  • The promise must be notarised, and notaries may not authorise it unless the guarantee exists.
  • The guarantee covers the case where the promise isn't fulfilled within the seller's stated deadline or condition.
  • It stays in force until the title is registered in your name at the Conservador de Bienes Raíces.
  • The one alternative: your money sits in a savings account you can't draw on, or a time deposit in the seller's favour held by the notary, released only once the sale is signed and registered in your name.
  • Housing cooperatives and rent-to-own contracts under Law 19,281 are excluded.

The Takeaway

Before you pay a peso, ask for the póliza or boleta, check that the amount matches your payments and that you are the beneficiary. Chile's consumer agency SERNAC says buyers can demand its execution if the developer misses its promised dates.
