Cartagena Beachfront vs Miami Beach: Bocagrande Costs About 40% Less per m²

By Charles Brossy May 14, 2026 · Updated October 23, 2026 3 min read

Apartments for sale in Bocagrande, Cartagena's main beachfront strip, were listed at an average of about COP 13 million per square meter (roughly US$4,080/m² at the October 23, 2026 exchange rate), according to the city's Catastro observatory. In Miami Beach, Redfin's median sale price was US$632 per square foot (about US$6,800/m²) in the three months to September 2026. That makes Bocagrande about 40% cheaper per m², not the "68% less" this tip used to claim.

Correction, October 23, 2026: this tip previously put Bocagrande at $3,500/m², Miami Beach at $11,000/m² and short-term rental yields at 8–12%. The Bocagrande figure predates the peso's rise against the dollar, $11,000/m² (about $1,020/sq ft) is luxury Miami Beach pricing rather than the median, and we found no reliable source for 8–12% yields. A "January 2026 TheLatinvestor" range we cited could not be found and has been removed.

The Cartagena coastal figures are average asking prices from the Catastro observatory, reported by El Universal in October 2025; the citywide average is Ciencuadras' May 2026 asking price. Miami Beach is a median sale price, so the comparison sets Cartagena listings against closed Miami sales. Pesos are converted at the official rate (TRM) of COP 3,185.01 per dollar on October 23, 2026. The Miami Beach median covers all home types; premium oceanfront buildings in both cities sell for far more than these averages.

Yields, Prices and the Peso

  • Rental yields: Global Property Guide puts Cartagena's average long-term gross rental yield at 5.22% (Q3 2026), below Colombia's 7.06% average. Net yields are lower after fees, administration and taxes.
  • Short-term rentals: one data provider, AirROI, reports Cartagena Airbnb listings booked on about 39% of available nights in the 12 months to September 2026, with average revenue down 10% on the year. Treat any 8–12% short-term yield claim with caution.
  • Price growth: DANE's new-housing price index for the Cartagena urban area rose 8.73% in the year to Q4 2025 and was up 6.28% year on year in Q2 2026. It covers new homes, not resales.
  • Rules for tourist rentals: listings need a National Tourism Registry (RNT) number, and the building's condominium rules must allow tourist use.
  • Foreign ownership: foreigners can buy in their own name with the same rights as Colombians. Bring the money through the formal exchange market so the investment can be registered with Banco de la República.

The peso has strengthened, not weakened: the official rate fell from a peak of about COP 5,061 per dollar in November 2022 to COP 3,185 on October 23, 2026. That is why Cartagena now costs more in dollars than older articles suggest. A currency that moved that far can move back, so treat the exchange rate as a risk in both directions.

The Bottom Line

Caribbean beachfront in Bocagrande still costs well under Miami Beach, but the honest gap is about 40% per m², and long-term yields average about 5%. If you are drawn to Cartagena, compare like with like, check the building's rules before counting on rental income, and read the US State Department's Colombia advisory (Level 3, "Reconsider Travel"; Bolívar, where Cartagena sits, is not a Level 4 area).
