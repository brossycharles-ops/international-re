Uruguay’s 10-Year Tax Holiday for New Residents: The 2026 Rules

By Charles Brossy May 12, 2026 Updated October 27, 2026 4 min read

As of October 2026, Uruguay’s 10-year tax holiday works like this: people who become Uruguayan tax residents on or after January 1, 2026 can opt to pay the non-resident tax (IRNR) instead of personal income tax (IRPF) on their foreign dividends, interest and capital gains for the year they become resident plus the ten following years. Because IRNR only taxes Uruguayan-source income, that foreign investment income is in practice untaxed in Uruguay during the holiday. The rules come from Law 20.446 (the 2025–2029 budget law), which replaced the earlier regime, and Decree 188/026 of August 2026 regulates them.

This page was rewritten on October 27, 2026. Earlier versions described the pre-2026 rules, said qualifying required about US$2,000,000 in real estate, called the holiday “zero tax on all foreign income” and said Uruguay was the only South American country with such a regime; those statements were out of date, overstated or unsourced.

What the Holiday Covers

Uruguay’s IRPF taxes residents on Uruguayan-source income, and on a defined list of foreign income: returns on capital (such as dividends and interest) from non-resident entities, capital gains on those assets, and income from financial derivatives. Foreign capital income of this kind is taxed at the general 12% IRPF rate. The holiday election applies exclusively to the first two items — foreign capital income from non-resident entities and the gains on those assets.

  • Covered: dividends from foreign companies, interest from foreign banks and bonds, and gains on selling foreign shares and similar assets.
  • Not covered: income from Uruguay, such as rent from a Uruguayan property (taxed at 12%) or work done in Uruguay.
  • Rent from a property abroad: rent you receive directly from a property you own abroad is not on the IRPF list of taxable foreign income; if the property is held through a foreign company, the dividends that company pays you are. Check your own structure with an adviser.

Who Qualifies

The election can be made once, by people who become tax residents from January 1, 2026 and who meet one of these conditions:

  • Physical presence: more than 183 days in Uruguay in each fiscal year. People who qualify this way do not need to make any investment.
  • Real estate: an investment in property worth more than UI 12,500,000 (indexed units), about US$2.07 million in October 2026.
  • Investment funds: at least UI 625,000 a year, about US$104,000, into investment funds that finance productive projects, research or innovation, as set out in the regulations.

In every case the person must not have been a Uruguayan tax resident in the two previous fiscal years, and must not have used the previous regime. The dollar figures are approximate: they use the INE value of the indexed unit on October 27, 2026 (UYU 6.6792) and the DGI interbank dollar rate of September 30, 2026 (UYU 40.286), and they move as the UI is adjusted for inflation and as the exchange rate changes.

After the Ten Years

When the holiday ends, a resident can choose, year by year, for up to twenty fiscal years after the first choice:

  • Half-rate IRPF for five years on the same foreign capital income, if they keep meeting the investment-fund condition each year or invest more than UI 6,250,000 (about US$1.04 million) in real estate; or
  • A fixed annual IRPF amount covering all that foreign capital income: UI 1,875,000 a year (about US$311,000), reduced to UI 1,250,000 (about US$207,000) in years they spend more than 183 days in Uruguay or if they make a direct investment of more than UI 45,000,000 in a business. A spouse who also opts in pays 15% of the fixed amount.

If You Moved Before 2026

The option under the previous rules (article 24 of Title 7) could only be exercised until December 31, 2025. People who opted in by then keep their regime for its term; when it ends, they can use the half-rate or fixed-amount options above. Anyone becoming resident now is under the new article 24-BIS rules.

Buying Property and Local Taxes

Uruguay’s investment law gives foreign investors the same treatment as nationals, and you do not need residency to buy. Punta del Este, the main beach resort, is a summer market: AirROI’s data shows occupancy at about 20% in August 2026 and 69% in January 2026, and InfoCasas puts the median asking price for a one-bedroom on Playa Brava at US$200,000 for 61 m² (September 2026). The Fine Print

US citizens and resident aliens are taxed by the United States on their worldwide income wherever they live, so the holiday lowers Uruguayan tax but not US tax. Tax residency for the holiday is not the same as legal residence from Migración, which is a separate process. The regulations in Decree 188/026 set the detailed conditions for the real-estate and fund investments; get advice from a Uruguayan tax adviser before relying on the election.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who qualifies for Uruguay’s 10-year tax holiday in 2026?

People who become Uruguayan tax residents on or after January 1, 2026 and were not tax residents in the two previous fiscal years. They qualify by spending more than 183 days in Uruguay in each year, or by investing more than UI 12.5 million (about US$2.07 million in October 2026) in real estate, or by putting at least UI 625,000 (about US$104,000) a year into qualifying investment funds.

Does the Uruguay tax holiday cover all foreign income?

No. The election covers only the foreign-source capital income that Uruguay’s personal income tax (IRPF) reaches: returns such as dividends and interest from non-resident entities, and capital gains on those assets. Under the election that income is taxed under the non-resident tax (IRNR), which only taxes Uruguayan-source income. Income from Uruguay, such as local rent or work, is still taxed.

What happens if I chose the old Uruguay tax holiday before 2026?

The option under the previous rules closed on December 31, 2025. People who used it keep it for the rest of their term, and when it ends they can choose the new post-holiday options: IRPF at 50% of the normal rate for five years if they meet investment conditions, or a fixed annual amount.
