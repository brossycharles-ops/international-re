Tax Fact: Uruguay Gives New Residents a 10-Year Zero-Tax Holiday on All Foreign Income

By International RE May 12, 2026 2 min read

Uruguay is the only country in South America that lets new tax residents elect to pay zero tax on foreign-source income — dividends, offshore rental income, interest, and capital gains — for a full 10 fiscal years after arriving. No other country on the continent bakes this into its standard tax code.

How the Holiday Works

Under Uruguay's territorial tax system, income earned outside the country is normally subject to a flat 12% personal income tax (IRPF) for residents. But new residents can make a one-time election to be taxed instead under the non-resident rate (IRNR) for foreign-source passive income during the year they establish residency and the following 10 fiscal years. For most categories of passive income — foreign dividends, foreign rental earnings, offshore capital gains — the effective IRNR rate is 0%.

Qualifying is straightforward: spend more than 183 days per year in Uruguay, or invest in Uruguayan real estate valued above approximately USD $2,000,000. That's it.

What This Means for Investors

  • Foreign rental income: Rent from a U.S., European, or other overseas property? Uruguay takes nothing on it for 10 years.
  • Dividends & interest: Portfolio income from foreign brokerage accounts is untaxed during the holiday window.
  • Capital gains abroad: Profits from selling foreign assets are sheltered for the full decade.
  • Local real estate: No restrictions on foreigners buying property — full freehold title in your own name.

Punta del Este, Uruguay's premier coastal resort, delivers 7–10% gross short-term rental yields during peak season (December–March), with occupancy rates hitting 90–95%. Entry-level investor apartments in solid neighborhoods start around $2,200/sqm — roughly 80% less than comparable beachfront in Miami Beach.

The Fine Print

US citizens are subject to US taxation on worldwide income regardless of where they live — the holiday reduces local Uruguayan tax but does not eliminate US obligations. Non-US nationals benefit most. Consult a cross-border tax adviser before restructuring.
