Buying in Brazil? You Need a CPF First, and It's Free at the Consulate

By Charles Brossy October 19, 2026 1 min read

Before you can buy a home in Brazil, you need a CPF, the individual taxpayer number issued by the Receita Federal. Brazil’s tax agency says non-residents, Brazilian or foreign, who hold assets subject to public registry in Brazil must register, and real estate (imóveis) heads its list.

How to Get One From Abroad

  • Where: foreigners living abroad apply in person at a Brazilian consulate or embassy, booked through the e-consular system.
  • What to bring: the signed FCPF registration form from the Receita Federal website, an identity document, and a birth or marriage certificate.
  • Cost: free, according to the federal services portal.
  • Proof: there is no plastic card. You print the Comprovante de Inscrição e de Situação Cadastral no CPF from the Receita Federal website and show it with your ID.
  • Keep it active: since 2025, foreigners with an address abroad aged 16 or over must update their CPF data once a year in the Receita Federal app. Skip it and the number can be suspended, which would hold up a purchase.

Do it early: the federal portal gives no processing estimate, and the same rule applies if you hold Brazilian bank accounts or investments. Our guide to buying property in Brazil as a foreigner explains what comes next, and the São Paulo city guide covers the largest market.

Sources

  • Receita Federal — CPF for individuals abroad (non-residents with registrable assets such as real estate must register; proof via printed comprovante)
  • gov.br — Inscrever no CPF no exterior (foreigners apply in person at a consulate via e-consular; documents; free; no time estimate)
  • LegisWeb — Instrução Normativa RFB nº 2.236/2024 (art. 23-A annual CPF update for foreigners abroad, optional in 2024; suspension under art. 12)
