São Paulo Charges a 3% ITBI Transfer Tax on Property Purchases

By Charles Brossy October 29, 2026 1 min read

São Paulo charges a 3% ITBI transfer tax on property purchases, and the buyer pays it, foreigner or not (municipal Law 11.154/1991, Arts. 6 and 10). The city's tax portal calculates it on the greater of the transaction price or the city's “valor venal de referência”, and when the sale is made by public deed, the tax must be paid before the deed is signed (Art. 12).

On a R$1,000,000 apartment, 3% is R$30,000. Watch the reference value: in February 2022, Brazil's Superior Court of Justice (STJ, Tema 1113) ruled that the ITBI base is the property's market value, that the price you declare is presumed to match it, and that a city cannot impose a reference value it set on its own in advance. São Paulo's portal still states the greater-of rule, so if the city's figure is above your price, ask a Brazilian lawyer before you pay.

Notary and registry fees come on top: São Paulo state law fixes notary fees for every notary in the state (the current table took effect on January 8, 2026), and registering the deed is charged separately. You also need a CPF tax number first, because Receita Federal requires one for anyone who holds real estate in Brazil (see No CPF, No Property Purchase). Sources

  • Prefeitura de São Paulo — ITBI-IV: alíquota e base de cálculo (3% rate; base is the greater of the transaction value or the valor venal de referência)
  • Prefeitura de São Paulo — Lei 11.154/1991 (ITBI), as amended (buyer is the taxpayer (Art. 6), 3% rate (Art. 10), paid before a public deed is executed (Art. 12))
  • STJ — ITBI e IPTU: o STJ e os impostos municipais (2022) (Tema 1113, February 2022: market-value base, declared price presumed correct, no unilateral pre-set reference value)

  • Receita Federal — CPF para estrangeiros (CPF required for non-residents holding real estate in Brazil)
