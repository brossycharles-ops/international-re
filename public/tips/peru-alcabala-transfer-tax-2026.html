Peru's Alcabala Tax in 2026: Buyers Pay 3%, but the First S/55,000 Is Exempt

By Charles Brossy October 17, 2026 1 min read

If you buy a resale home in Peru, the alcabala tax is the main transfer tax, and the buyer pays it, foreigner or not. The rate is 3%, but the first 10 UIT of the value are exempt. With the 2026 UIT set at S/ 5,500, that is the first S/ 55,000.

The Math on a S/ 1,000,000 Apartment

  • Exempt slice: 10 × S/ 5,500 = S/ 55,000
  • Taxable: S/ 1,000,000 − S/ 55,000 = S/ 945,000
  • Alcabala: 3% × S/ 945,000 = S/ 28,350, about 2.8% of the price

The base is the sale price, but it can never be lower than the municipal assessed value (autovalúo) adjusted for inflation. The law says the buyer pays and the contract cannot shift the tax to the seller. It is due in one payment by the last business day of the month after the transfer. In the province of Lima you pay SAT Lima; elsewhere you pay the local municipality.

One exception: when a construction company sells a new unit for the first time, alcabala applies only to the land value, and the sale itself is taxed with IGV (Peru's VAT). Foreigners can own property in Peru on the same terms as Peruvians, except within 50 km of the borders, where foreigners need a supreme decree to acquire land. Sources

  • SAT Lima — TUO de la Ley de Tributación Municipal (DS 156-2004-EF) (Arts. 22–26 and 29: 3% rate paid by the buyer, first 10 UIT exempt, tax base, due date, new-build rule, collecting municipality)
  • El Peruano — Decreto Supremo Nº 301-2025-EF (2026 UIT of S/ 5,500)
  • SAT Lima — Directiva Nº 001-006-00000012 on the Impuesto de Alcabala (SAT collects alcabala for transfers in the province of Lima)
  • SUNAT — Informe N.° 006-2014-SUNAT/5D0000 (IGV applies to a builder's first sale of a property)
  • Constitución Política del Perú 1993 (SENASA copy) (Art. 71: foreigners have the same property rights as Peruvians, except within 50 km of the borders)
