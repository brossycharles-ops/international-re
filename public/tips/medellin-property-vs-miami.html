Medellín vs Miami: Apartments Cost About 60% Less per m²

By Charles Brossy April 15, 2026 · Updated October 21, 2026 2 min read

New apartments in Medellín, Colombia had an average asking price of about US$2,400 per square meter in the third quarter of 2026, according to Global Property Guide. In Miami, Redfin's median sale price was US$532 per square foot (roughly US$5,700 per m²) in September 2026, across all home types. That makes Medellín about 60% cheaper per m², not the "93% below Miami" this tip claimed before.

Correction, October 21, 2026: this tip used to put Medellín at $1,200/m² and Miami at over $16,000/m². Both figures were wrong. The Medellín figure was out of date, reflecting older prices and a much weaker peso. And $16,000/m² (about $1,490/sq ft) is ultra-luxury Miami pricing, not the average.

What the Gap Looks Like in Practice

  • Laureles: median asking prices of about US$155,500 for a 1-bedroom and US$201,900 for a 2-bedroom (Global Property Guide, Q3 2026).
  • El Poblado: about US$250,000 for a 1-bedroom and US$336,600 for a 2-bedroom, so the old "$80,000–$200,000" range does not apply there.
  • Rental yields: long-term gross yields average 6.88% in Medellín. Net yields are typically 1.5–2 points lower. Short-term rentals need Registro Nacional de Turismo (RNT) registration, and the building's rules must allow tourist use.
  • Cost of living: Numbeo puts Medellín about 43% cheaper than Miami excluding rent, and rents about 69% lower.

Ownership, Costs and Currency

Foreigners can buy in their own name with the same rights as Colombians. Bring the money in through the Colombian exchange market (a bank or authorised intermediary) so the purchase can be registered as foreign investment with Banco de la República. Budget roughly 1.5–2.5% for the buyer's usual share of notary fees (half of 0.3% plus VAT), registry fees (about 0.9–1.3%) and Antioquia's 1% registration tax (normally split with the seller). That rises to about 3% if you pay the whole registration tax.

One thing has changed against dollar buyers: the peso has strengthened. The official rate fell from a peak of about COP 5,061 per dollar in November 2022 to about COP 3,164 on October 21, 2026. That is a big part of why Medellín costs more in dollars than it used to. A peso that moved that far can move back, so treat the exchange rate as a risk in both directions.

The Bottom Line

Medellín is still far cheaper per square meter than Miami, and at around 60% the gap is large enough to matter. It is no longer the "fraction of the price" bargain that a weak peso once made it. Compare like with like: new-build against new-build, neighbourhood against neighbourhood.
