Market Stat: In Mexico City, Foreigners Own Property in Their Name — No Bank Trust — at $4,200/sqm

By International RE June 4, 2026 2 min read

Most people know Mexico requires a bank trust (fideicomiso) for foreigners buying near the coast. What most people don't know: Mexico City is entirely outside the restricted zone. Foreigners buy condos in Roma Norte and Condesa directly in their own name — no trust, no annual fees — at around $4,200 per square meter.

The Ownership Advantage Almost Nobody Talks About

Mexico's "restricted zone" covers land within 50 km of a coastline or 100 km of a border. Mexico City, sitting at 2,240 meters above sea level in the country's interior, doesn't come close. That means a foreign buyer in Roma Norte has the same clean title a Mexican citizen would — no intermediary bank, no setup costs, no annual trust renewal fees that coastal purchases carry.

The numbers stack up:

  • Price per sqm: ~$4,200 USD in Roma Norte / Condesa as of 2026
  • Comparable NYC neighborhood (SoHo, West Village): $22,000–$30,000/sqm
  • Annual appreciation: ~10% in Roma Norte and Condesa in 2025–2026
  • Gross rental yield: 6–9% in central colonias with strong expat demand
  • Typical 1BR entry price: ~$250,000–$400,000 USD

Why Roma Norte and Condesa?

These two neighborhoods have become the cultural heart of Latin America's largest city — dense with restaurants, independent cafés, and a booming short-term rental market driven by digital nomads and business travelers. Demand has pushed prices up steadily, but the market remains a fraction of comparable global cities.

For investors who want clean title, a globally connected city, and appreciation momentum, Mexico City interior is one of the most overlooked plays in Latin America right now.
