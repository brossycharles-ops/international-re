Market Tip: Costa Rica Gives Foreigners the Same Property Rights as Citizens — Full Title in Your Own Name

By Charles Brossy May 19, 2026 2 min read

In Costa Rica, foreigners purchase property with the exact same legal rights as citizens — full freehold title, your name on the deed, no bank trust required. No fideicomiso. No middleman. No annual trust fees.

What Makes This Unusual

Mexico's restricted zones — within 50 km of any coastline or 100 km of any border — require foreign buyers to hold property through a bank trust called a fideicomiso. It works, but it adds $500–$1,000/year in fees, a layer of bureaucracy, and a trust that must be renewed every 50 years.

Costa Rica has no equivalent restriction. A foreign buyer can purchase titled residential property anywhere in the country, in their own name, with the same legal protections as any Costa Rican citizen. The only caveat is the Maritime Zone: the first 50 meters from the high tide line is public land (off-limits entirely), and the next 150 meters operates on a municipal concession system — but everything beyond that 200-meter band is yours to own outright.

The Yield Story in Guanacaste

Clean title and strong legal footing are the foundation. The returns are the reason investors are moving fast:

  • Gross rental yields: Guanacaste vacation rentals average 7–8% annually — with peak-season short-term rentals in Tamarindo and Nosara reaching 10–12%
  • Entry prices: Quality condos in Tamarindo and Flamingo from $150,000–$350,000 USD
  • No foreign ownership quotas: No cap on how many units in a building foreigners can own
  • Dollar-denominated market: Transactions priced in USD, reducing currency conversion friction for US buyers

US retiree and expat demand has pushed Guanacaste into one of Central America's most liquid second-home markets — which also means resale is easier than in more frontier destinations.

The Bottom Line

Costa Rica is one of the few countries in Latin America where a foreign buyer gets exactly what a local buyer gets: clean title, full rights, no workarounds. Paired with Guanacaste's 7–8% vacation rental yields, the legal simplicity isn't just reassuring — it's a competitive advantage.
