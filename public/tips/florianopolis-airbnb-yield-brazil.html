Florianópolis Airbnb Yield 2026: About 7% Gross, Not 8–12%

By Charles Brossy April 28, 2026 · Updated October 20, 2026 2 min read

Florianópolis is one of Brazil's three biggest Airbnb markets, but the oft-quoted "8–12% Airbnb yield" does not hold up. FipeZAP puts the average asking price at R$13,607/m² (about US$2,690) in September 2026 and the long-term rental yield at 5.42%. AirDNA's short-term revenue data points to roughly 7% gross on an average flat, before fees and tax.

The Numbers (September–October 2026)

  • Average asking price: R$13,607/m², about US$2,690 at the Banco Central PTAX rate of R$5.06 per dollar (19 October 2026). Florianópolis is Brazil's second-most expensive capital after Vitória, and prices rose 8.31% in the 12 months to September.
  • Neighbourhoods: FipeZAP's ten sampled areas range from Capoeiras at about R$8,763/m² (US$1,730) to Agronômica at R$16,517/m² (US$3,270); beach-side Ingleses do Rio Vermelho averages R$10,455/m² (US$2,070). It publishes no figure for Jurerê Internacional.
  • Long-term rental yield: 5.42% gross a year (FipeZAP rental index), with average rents of R$61.81/m² a month, up 3.56% in a year.
  • Short-term rentals: AirDNA counts about 10,725 active listings, averaging 56% occupancy, a US$84 nightly rate and US$13,200 a year in revenue. That ranks Florianópolis third in Brazil by listings, behind São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro.
  • Entry point: at the city average, a 50–90 m² apartment costs about US$135,000–240,000.

What the Airbnb Yield Really Looks Like

Take a 70 m² flat at the city average: about R$952,000, or US$188,000. AirDNA's average revenue of US$13,200 a year gives a gross return of about 7% (AirDNA's revenue includes guest fees, so this is slightly flattering). Platform commission, cleaning, management, condominium fees and income tax all come out of that. Other trackers report lower figures: AirROI estimates average revenue of about US$8,400 a year at about 37% occupancy. Revenue is seasonal, peaking in the southern summer, so treat a net short-term yield of about 4–5% as a realistic planning figure and the 5.42% long-term yield as the benchmark to beat.

What Foreigners Need to Know

Ownership: Brazilian law restricts foreigners only on rural land (Law 5,709/1971) and within the 150 km border strip (Law 6,634/1979), so urban apartments in Florianópolis can be bought in your own name. One coastal caveat: federal "terrenos de marinha" land near the shore has its own rules, though condominium units in urban areas are exempt from the authorisation requirement when foreign holdings do not exceed one-third of the building.

CPF: you need a Brazilian tax number (CPF). Foreigners living abroad apply in person at a Brazilian consulate, bringing the completed CPF registration form (FCPF), ID and a birth or marriage certificate.

Tax: non-residents pay a flat 15% on Brazilian rental income (25% if resident in a listed tax haven), with no deductions, according to PwC's tax summary.

Condominium rules: the Superior Court of Justice (STJ) has held that owners cannot run Airbnb-style lettings where the condominium convention sets residential use (REsp 1.819.075, 2021), and in mid-2026 made the question a binding "Tema 1.443" case. Related lawsuits are suspended nationwide until it rules, so check the building's convention before buying for short-term rental.

The Bottom Line

Florianópolis is a large, liquid holiday-rental market with prices still rising faster than inflation, but it is not a double-digit-yield play. Budget about 7% gross and 4–5% net on short lets, compare that with the 5.42% long-term yield, and confirm the building allows short stays before you sign.
