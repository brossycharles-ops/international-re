Ecuador Property Transfer Tax: 1% Alcabala, Shared Unless the Contract Says Otherwise

By Charles Brossy October 26, 2026 1 min read

Ecuador’s property transfer tax, the alcabala, is 1% of the price in the deed, or of the municipal cadastral value if that is higher (COOTAD Articles 532 and 535). It is paid to the municipality where the property sits, before the notary can execute the deed.

Who pays is not fixed by law. Article 531 makes the taxpayers the parties who benefit from the contract and presumes the benefit is mutual and proportional unless the contract says otherwise, so agree the split in writing. Surcharges created by special laws are collected with it, capped together at another 100% of the 1% rate. On a US$150,000 apartment priced at or above its cadastral value, that is US$1,500 before surcharges. If the same property changes hands again, with one of the same parties, within one, two or three years, the alcabala is cut by 40%, 30% or 20%. The seller’s tax on the gain (plusvalía, 10% unless a municipal ordinance changes it) is a separate bill, though the buyer can be held liable if it goes unpaid.

  • Gob.ec — Código Orgánico de Organización Territorial (COOTAD) (consolidated text, last amended 16 January 2015; Arts. 530–533, 535–537, 556 and 558: where it is paid, taxpayers and presumed split, base, resale rebates, 1% rate, surcharge cap, notary rule, plusvalía)
