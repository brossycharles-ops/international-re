Panama Capital Gains Tax on Property Sales (2026)

By Charles Brossy April 17, 2026 2 min read

Does Panama charge capital gains tax on property? Not under that name, but sellers are taxed on the sale. An individual seller pays an advance income tax of 3% of the higher of the sale price or the registered value, and can either accept it as final or pay 10% of the actual gain and credit the 3% against it. The seller also pays the 2% transfer tax (ITBI). (Corrected October 2026: an earlier version of this page said Panama has no capital gains tax on property.)

The Taxes on a Sale in Plain English

  • Advance income tax (seller): 3% of the higher of the price or the registered value, paid at the sale. If the actual gain is small, the seller can file instead and pay 10% of the gain, crediting the 3%.
  • Transfer tax, ITBI (seller): 2% of the higher of the price or the registered value. Ley 546 of 2026 brought back partial relief on the first sale of some new homes priced up to US$200,000.
  • Buyer's costs: attorney, notary and registry fees of roughly 2–3% of the price.

These taxes are the seller's by law, but sellers often build them into the price, so compare offers on an all-in basis. For the full rules, see our sourced guide: Can foreigners buy property in Panama?

Other Points for Foreign Buyers

  • US dollar economy: Panama uses the US dollar, so American buyers face no exchange-rate risk.
  • Foreign buyer rights: foreigners can buy titled property in their own name with almost the same rights as citizens; limits apply near borders and on some islands and beaches.
  • Yields: Panama City's average gross rental yield was 7.57% in April 2026, according to Global Property Guide (October 2026 market update).
