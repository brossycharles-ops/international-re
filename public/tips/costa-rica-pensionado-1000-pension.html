Costa Rica Pensionado Visa Requirements 2026: A $1,000 Monthly Pension

By Charles Brossy October 20, 2026 1 min read

The core of Costa Rica's pensionado visa requirements in 2026 is one number: you must prove a monthly, permanent and stable pension from abroad of at least US$1,000 or its equivalent. That floor comes from Article 81 of the Ley General de Migración y Extranjería (Law 8764).

What Else the Law Says

  • Term: temporary residence for up to two years, renewable for the same period (Art. 79).
  • Dependents: Article 82 lets applicants in “these subcategories” include a spouse and children under 25 (or adult children with disabilities) on the same income. Confirm with DGME how it applies to you.
  • Caja (CCSS): to renew, you must show CCSS enrolment with no gaps from the day residency was granted (Art. 80).
  • Time in the country: the law sets no minimum number of days per year. DGME cancels temporary residence after an absence of more than two consecutive years, barring proven health, study, family or similar reasons (Art. 129).
  • Permanent residence: after three consecutive years of temporary residence, you can apply (Art. 78).

Buying a home needs no residency or visa: a foreigner can buy titled property with just a passport (beachfront in the 200 m maritime zone is a concession with extra limits). See our Costa Rica buying guide, the Costa Rica retirement guide, and the San José city guide. Unsure? Try our market quiz.

Sources

  • Poder Judicial de Costa Rica — Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, Ley 8764 (Arts. 78–82 and 129: US$1,000 pension floor, two-year renewable term, dependents, CCSS enrolment for renewal, absence rule, permanent residence after three years)
  • Defensoría de los Habitantes — Ley 8764, Título VI: Categorías migratorias (same text of Arts. 78–82)
  • The Tico Times — Buying Property in Costa Rica: What Foreign Buyers Need to Know (Sept. 11, 2026) (foreigners can buy titled property with a passport; no residency or visa needed; 200 m maritime zone held by concession)
  • Tribunal Supremo de Elecciones — Constitución Política de Costa Rica (Art. 19: foreigners have the same individual and social rights as Costa Ricans, with legal exceptions)
