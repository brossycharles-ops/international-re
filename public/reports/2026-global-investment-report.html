1. Executive summary

Foreigners can legally buy in their own name in most of these markets, but Nicaragua is now high-risk: the US State Department reports seizures of foreign-owned property there (sources). This report deliberately shows no price-growth forecasts or "appreciation" picks: past price changes are not a reliable guide to future returns, and we only quote figures we can source. Where we have no reliable figure for a market, the table says so.

Three things to know for 2026

Highest sourced average yield: Costa Rica — average gross yields of 7.63% in Q2 2026 (Global Property Guide), before costs and taxes. Panama vs Costa Rica, sourced.

Highest risk: Nicaragua — documented seizures of foreign-owned homes and no reliable price or yield data.

Gross is not net: every yield below is asking rent over asking price, before vacancy, management, building fees and tax. Global Property Guide notes net yields typically run 1.5 to 2 percentage points lower.

3. Market-by-market detail

🇨🇷 Costa Rica

San José apartments run about $2,100–$2,950/m² with gross yields of 6.3–8.1% (Global Property Guide). For Guanacaste's beach towns we have found no reliable published yield data. Foreign buyers have the same ownership rights as citizens. The municipal property tax is 0.25% of the appraised value; homes whose construction value is above ¢143 million in 2026 also pay the solidarity (luxury home) tax at 0.25% to 0.55% by band. How to buy in Costa Rica.

🇵🇦 Panama

Dollarized economy, so no exchange-rate risk for dollar buyers. Global Property Guide puts Panama's average gross yield at 6.94% in Q2 2026, with a wide spread by neighborhood (for example 8.47% for a studio or one-bedroom in El Cangrejo). Neither residency route is a tax-residency program: the Qualified Investor program now requires $300,000 in first-sale property or $500,000 in resale property (Executive Decree 17 of September 8, 2026), and the Friendly Nations route has required a real economic tie since 2021, with a three-year fixed deposit of at least $200,000 as one option. Sourced details.

🇨🇴 Colombia

Medellín asks about $1,600–$2,800/m² (Metrocuadrado, August 2026), with gross yields around 6.9% (Global Property Guide). Prices are in pesos, so dollar buyers carry currency risk. Medellín city guide.

🇲🇽 Mexico

Mexico City apartments ask a median of about $3,200–$4,300/m² from Narvarte to Roma Norte (Mudafy listings, October 2026, at the Banxico FIX), with gross yields of 6.5–7%. In the restricted zone (within 50 km of the coast and 100 km of the borders) foreigners hold residential property through a fideicomiso (bank trust) with an SRE permit. For Playa del Carmen and Tulum we have found no reliable published yield data. Mexico City guide · Tulum guide.

🇦🇷 Argentina

Listings are priced in US dollars. Buenos Aires averages US$2,484/m² (Palermo US$3,454), 11.3% below its peak (Zonaprop, Sept 2026), with a 5.70% city-wide gross yield. City stamp duty on the transfer is 2.7% up to ARS 226.1 million and 3.5% above that. Argentina has a long history of currency controls. Buenos Aires guide.

🇨🇱 Chile

New apartments across Gran Santiago averaged 83 UF/m² (about CLP 3.4 million) in the three months to July 2026, and 103–117 UF in Providencia, Las Condes and Vitacura. Prices are quoted in UF, an inflation-indexed unit. Global Property Guide puts Santiago's gross apartment yield at 4.91% in Q1 2026. Santiago guide.

🇳🇮 Nicaragua

Low prices, but the highest risk in the region. The US State Department reports seizures of foreign-owned property, the constitution was rewritten in 2025, and in August 2026 the government took nine homes owned by US retirees in Tola, Rivas. There is no reliable price or yield data, and tourist arrivals are well below their 2017 peak. Sourced 2026 risk briefing.

🇺🇾 Uruguay

Foreigners buy on the same terms as Uruguayans. Montevideo's median asking price was about $2,840/m² in September 2026 (Índice INGAR), with gross yields of about 5–6%. Gains on a sale and rental income are taxed at 12%. The new-resident tax holiday changed under Law 20.446 from January 1, 2026 and now has stricter tests. Montevideo guide · Uruguay property tax.

🇪🇨 Ecuador

Dollarized, so no exchange-rate risk for dollar buyers; Cuenca is the best-known expat destination. We have not yet sourced a reliable price-per-m² or yield figure, so the table shows n/a. Can foreigners buy property in Ecuador?

🇵🇪 Peru

Central bank (BCRP) data put Q1 2026 median asking prices at about $2,460/m² in Miraflores, $2,560 in Barranco and $2,600 in San Isidro; gross yields are about 5.5–6%. The buyer pays the 3% alcabala transfer tax on the value above 10 UIT (S/ 55,000 in 2026). Lima guide.

🇧🇷 Brazil

São Paulo's average asking price was R$12,206/m² (about US$2,420) in September 2026, with a 6.43% average gross yield (FipeZAP). Foreigners can buy urban property on the same terms as Brazilians once they have a CPF tax number; the city charges 3% ITBI transfer tax. São Paulo guide.

4. Tax & cost-of-ownership notes

Annual property taxes are set very differently across these countries, so we do not reduce them to one number per market. Examples we have sourced: Costa Rica charges 0.25% of appraised value (plus the solidarity tax on high-value homes); Panama charges 0% on the first $120,000 of a registered primary home and 0.6–1% in bands on rentals above $30,000; Chile exempts the first CLP 61.7 million of a home's fiscal appraisal and charges 0.893%, then 1.042% above CLP 220.4 million. On a sale, the gain is taxed at 10% in Panama, 15% in Costa Rica and 12% in Uruguay. Check the city guide for each market's transfer taxes and closing costs before you budget.

5. Methodology & sources

Every figure in this report comes from one of our sourced city guides or articles, linked above, and from the primary data sources below. Prices are asking prices; yields are gross (asking rent over asking price) and exclude vacancy, management, building fees and tax. Where no reliable figure exists we show n/a rather than an estimate. We do not publish price-growth projections. This report is provided for informational purposes only and does not constitute financial or legal advice. Always engage local counsel for due diligence before purchase.
