3. The 47-item relocation checklist

Six months before

  1. Pick two or three target markets using the Market Quiz and our city guides.
  2. Set an all-in budget: purchase price plus 5–8% for closing costs and a 3–6 month cash reserve.
  3. Check your passport has at least 12 months of validity left, and renew it if not.
  4. List the residency routes for each target country and note their income or savings requirements.
  5. Ask a tax adviser how foreign property and rental income are taxed in your home country.
  6. Decide whether to buy straight away or rent for 3–6 months first (we recommend renting first).
  7. Start tracking listings and prices in your target neighborhoods.
  8. Join local expat forums to hear about current, on-the-ground issues.
  9. Read the official travel advisory for each country you're considering.
  10. Plan a 2–3 week exploratory trip that covers several neighborhoods.

Three months before

  1. Shortlist a bilingual real estate attorney who represents only you, never the seller.
  2. Get apostilled copies of your birth certificate, marriage certificate and police background check — most residency applications ask for them.
  3. Confirm a bank account that handles international wires, and check its fees and limits.
  4. Buy international health insurance that covers you until you can enroll locally.
  5. Plan your currency conversion so you avoid retail airport or bank rates.
  6. Revisit shortlisted neighborhoods at different times, including a weekday night.
  7. Ask agents for recent comparable sale prices, not just asking prices.
  8. Find out the property tax, HOA fees and typical utility costs for each property.
  9. If you work remotely, test internet speed and reliability at the actual property.
  10. If you plan to rent the property out, get quotes from 2–3 property managers.

The purchase

  1. Have your attorney run a full title search and lien check.
  2. Confirm zoning, permits, and that the seller has the legal right to sell.
  3. Get an independent inspection covering structure, water, electrics and roof.
  4. Negotiate with comparables in hand, and get every term in writing.
  5. Pay deposits through escrow or your attorney's trust account — never directly to the seller.
  6. Get closing costs, transfer taxes and notary fees confirmed in writing.
  7. Register the deed at the public registry and keep proof of registration.
  8. Put utilities, the property tax account and insurance in your name.
  9. Keep certified copies of every closing document.

Travel week

  1. Carry your apostilled documents in original and copy.
  2. Book temporary accommodation for your first 2–4 weeks.
  3. Tell your bank you're traveling, and carry cards from two different banks.
  4. Set up a local SIM or eSIM.
  5. Back up all your documents to secure cloud storage.
  6. Check entry rules, including proof of onward travel.

First 30 days

  1. Open a local bank account.
  2. Start your residency application with a licensed immigration lawyer.
  3. Register with your embassy's citizen program.
  4. Find a doctor, a pharmacy and the nearest hospital.
  5. Learn the local tenancy rules if you will rent the property out.
  6. Meet your property manager and agree how they will report to you.
  7. Track every expense for your tax records.

First 90 days

  1. Compare your real costs with your budget and adjust.
  2. Confirm property tax and HOA payments are up to date.
  3. File any home-country disclosures required for foreign accounts or property.
  4. Build a local network: neighbors, other owners and trusted tradespeople.
  5. Put an annual review of your property's value, rent and costs in your calendar.

4. A realistic six-month buying timeline

WhenWhat to get done
Month 1Choose 2–3 markets, set your budget, and read the city guides and legal guides for each.
Month 2Exploratory trip. Visit neighborhoods at different times and meet two or three attorneys.
Month 3Narrow to one market. Order apostilled documents and line up banking and insurance.
Month 4Shortlist properties, get comparables, and arrange inspections.
Month 5Make an offer, run the title search, and pay the deposit into escrow.
Month 6Close, register the deed, put accounts in your name, and start residency if you're moving.
