Santiago vs. The Lake District: Where Should You Buy in Chile?

By Charles Brossy April 6, 2026 · Updated October 19, 2026 8 min read

Santiago or the Lake District? For most foreign buyers, Santiago is the income and liquidity play: gross yields of about 5% and a deep resale market, at new-build prices of roughly US$4,300–4,900/m² in the prime eastern comunas. The Lake District (Puerto Varas, Pucón, Villarrica) is a lifestyle purchase with seasonal rental demand and far thinner data. Either way, plan to pay cash: Chilean banks generally lend only to residents. Every figure below is sourced at the end.

The Case for Santiago

The Santiago Metropolitan Region had 7.4 million residents at the 2024 census. The economy is soft right now: in September 2026 the Banco Central cut its 2026 growth forecast to 0.25–0.75%, and the IMF's October 2026 outlook expects 0.6% this year and 2.6% in 2027.

Pricing

New apartments in the prime eastern comunas now cost more than 100 UF per square metre, according to TOCTOC data reported by CNN Chile (data to July 2026). At about US$42 per UF:

  • Vitacura: 117 UF/m², about $4,900/m², the most expensive
  • Las Condes: 114 UF/m², about $4,800/m²
  • Providencia: 103 UF/m², about $4,300/m²
  • Metropolitan Region average: 83 UF/m², about $3,500/m², up 5.4% in a year in UF terms

Across the city as a whole, Global Property Guide puts new (primary-market) apartments at about $2,810/m² in Q3 2026, down 16% in dollar terms in a year, so the eastern comunas sit well above the city average. For comparison, Global Property Guide's averages are about $2,950/m² for Mexico City and about $5,480/m² for Miami.

Rental Yields

Global Property Guide puts Santiago's average gross rental yield at 5.01% (Chile 4.80%, Q3 2026), with its samples ranging from about 3.6% to 5.5%. Net yields typically run 1.5–2 points lower, so about 3–3.5% net. Non-resident landlords also pay Chile's 35% Additional Tax on rental income, with a credit for first-category tax. It is steady income, not high income.

The Advantage: Liquidity and Leverage

Santiago has an active resale market with established agencies and a deep pool of local buyers. Buyers also have bargaining power: the president of the Chilean Chamber of Construction (CChC) said in August 2026 there are about 100,000 unsold homes nationally, and Banco Central data reported by El Desconcierto suggest clearing the stock would take about 19 months, against 7 months in 2011–2019. Unsold new-apartment stock in the Metropolitan Region was 49,834 units in July 2026, down 18% in a year.

The Case for the Lake District

Chile's southern Lake District — Puerto Varas on Lago Llanquihue, and Pucón and Villarrica on Lago Villarrica — offers volcanoes, lakes, forests and hot springs, and draws domestic and foreign second-home buyers.

Pricing and appreciation: thin data

Reliable price data for these towns is scarce. The CChC data reported by Global Property Guide shows new-apartment prices in Chile's broad "South" macro-zone rose 14.63% year on year in Q3 2025 (9.8% after inflation), the fastest of any zone. But that zone is far bigger than the Lake District, and town-level data has pointed the other way: a USS-Tinsa study found the average price in Puerto Varas fell from 99.88 to 88.43 UF/m² between Q2 2023 and Q2 2024, about 11.5%. Even at 88 UF/m², about $3,700/m² at today's rates, Puerto Varas is above the Metropolitan Region's new-apartment average of about $3,500/m². Get local comparable sales before you trust any headline growth figure.

The public works under way are modest. The Ministry of Public Works began a lakefront park, the Parque Costanera, in Puerto Varas in July 2026, with an investment of about CLP 9.7 billion (roughly US$10 million).

The Tradeoff: Seasonality

Tourist demand in southern Chile peaks in the summer high season, December to March (Sernatur's regional barometer for Aysén, for example, uses that window). Outside it occupancy is lower, so a holiday rental earns most of its income in a few months. Santiago has year-round tenant demand.

Buying as a Foreigner in Chile

  • Ownership: the Civil Code gives foreigners the same civil rights as Chileans, so they can buy in their own name. You need a RUT (tax ID) from the SII first, and a non-resident investor must name a representative domiciled in Chile.
  • Border and coastal land: under Decreto Ley 1939, state land within 10 km of the border or 5 km of the coast is restricted for foreigners, and nationals of neighbouring countries cannot buy in declared border zones without an exemption. Your lawyer should check whether a property lies in a declared border zone.
  • Mortgages: the average Chilean mortgage rate was 4.07% in September 2026 (UF loans, Banco Central). But banks such as Bci and Banco Consorcio lend only to Chileans or foreigners with permanent residency (Consorcio asks for at least two years), with Chilean income, up to 80–90% of the value over up to 30 years. Non-resident buyers should plan on cash.
  • Buying costs: about 2.3–3.4% for the buyer (notary, legal fees, registry and agent), per Global Property Guide, plus a 0.8% stamp tax on any mortgage.
  • Property tax (contribuciones): homes are exempt up to an assessed value of CLP 61.7 million; above that the rate is 0.893%, and 1.042% on the portion above CLP 220.4 million (second half of 2026). Our Chile property tax guide for foreigners has a worked example.
  • VAT relief from 1 December: the Ley de Reconstrucción (Ley 21.850), published in the Diario Oficial on 17 October 2026, exempts the first sale of finished new homes from VAT for one year from 1 December, with no cap on price or size.

For a full legal walkthrough, see our guide to buying property in Chile as a foreigner.

Income-focused investors should lean Santiago: steady yields, year-round demand and an easier exit, with buyers' leverage while unsold stock stays high.

Lifestyle buyers who want a home they will use for part of the year should look south, but treat it as a personal purchase first. Don't count on price growth: the data is thin and contradictory.

Splitting capital between both is possible, but because foreign non-residents generally can't get a Chilean mortgage, it means paying cash twice.

2026 Update: Tourism Is Recovering

Chile received 6,004,567 international tourists in 2025, according to the Subsecretaría de Turismo, up from 5.24 million in 2024. That was the best year since 2017, though still below the 6.45 million of 2017. Nationals of about 95 countries and territories, including the United States, Japan and the United Arab Emirates, need no visa for stays of up to 90 days, according to the Chilean consular fee table. The Route of Parks of Patagonia links 17 national parks over about 2,800 km between Puerto Montt and Cape Horn, with the Lake District as its northern gateway.

Tourism supports short-term rental demand in the south, but it is seasonal, and arrivals are only now regaining their 2017 level. The US State Department rates Chile Level 2, "Exercise increased caution", because of crime, civil unrest and health risks. Compare the dynamic with Guanacaste, Costa Rica, another tourism-led market.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an apartment cost in Santiago's best neighbourhoods?

New apartments cost about 117 UF/m² in Vitacura, 114 in Las Condes and 103 in Providencia (TOCTOC, 2026), roughly US$4,300–4,900/m². Global Property Guide puts the city-wide average for new apartments at about $2,810/m².

Can foreigners get a mortgage in Chile?

Usually only with residency. Banks such as Bci and Banco Consorcio lend to Chileans or foreigners with permanent residency and Chilean income. The average mortgage rate was 4.07% in September 2026, but non-resident buyers should plan to pay cash.

What rental yield can you get in Santiago?

Global Property Guide puts Santiago's average gross yield at 5.01% in Q3 2026 (Chile 4.80%). Net yields typically run 1.5–2 points lower, so about 3–3.5%.
