Medellín in 2026: Latin America's Most Transformed City Is Also Its Smartest Real Estate Play

By Charles Brossy April 12, 2026 · Updated October 19, 2026 8 min read

Buying property in Medellín in 2026 costs roughly US$1,400–2,500 per square metre on average depending on the source and segment, and up to about $3,800 in El Poblado, and gross rental yields average about 6.9% (Global Property Guide, Q3 2026). Foreigners can buy in their own name with no restrictions, but every peso has to come through a Colombian bank, and short-term rentals need both a tourism registration and building approval. Here is what the current data actually shows, with every figure sourced below.

El Poblado and neighbouring Envigado have the highest averages in the valley, COP 8.5–12 million/m², about $2,700–3,800, according to El Colombiano. Laureles-Estadio, the flat, leafy district west of the river, averages close to $2,000/m². These are asking prices, not closed sales, so expect room to negotiate.

Prices have been rising. DANE's new-housing price index (IPVN) put Medellín municipality up 8.29% year on year in Q4 2025 (8.77% for the metro area), and Metrocuadrado measured about 9% growth for used homes in 2025. The latest DANE bulletin (Q2 2026) shows the metro area up 9.14% and the municipality up 5.58%. Because the peso has also strengthened sharply (see below), the rise in dollar terms has been bigger still.

Global Property Guide's Q3 2026 survey puts Medellín's average gross yield at 6.88%, against 7.06% for Colombia. Its neighbourhood samples:

Global Property Guide notes that net yields typically run 1.5–2 percentage points lower after administration fees, property tax, maintenance and vacancy, so plan on roughly 4.5–5.5% net for a well-bought long-term rental. Lower-priced comunas such as Castilla or Buenos Aires may yield more, but we found no reliable published data for them.

Short-term rentals: check the rules first

Furnished stays of up to 30 days are legal in Medellín only if the unit is entered in the national tourism register (RNT) before it starts operating, and, in a building under propiedad horizontal, the building's bylaws (reglamento) expressly allow tourist use. If they don't, the co-owners' assembly must approve it and the change must be recorded in an escritura pública, according to the Alcaldía de Medellín; the registration rules come from Ley 2068 de 2020 and Decreto 1836 de 2021. Fines for unregistered operation can reach 2,000 monthly minimum wages. Demand is not guaranteed either: city tourism data showed tourist-dwelling occupancy of just 48.7% in November 2024, against 78.1% for hotels. Who pays the notary and registry charges is negotiable, so agree it in the promesa de compraventa. Cadastral value (avalúo catastral) is usually below market value, so the real predial bill is often lower than the headline rate suggests. Sellers pay the agent's commission, typically 3–4% plus VAT, according to Global Property Guide.

The Currency Equation

The Colombian peso has had a strong run. The official TRM was COP 3,174.67 per dollar on 17–19 October 2026, against 3,808.12 a year earlier: the dollar buys about 17% fewer pesos than it did in October 2025, and about 37% fewer than at the November 2022 peak of 5,061. The rate last stood above 4,300 in May 2025.

For dollar buyers that cuts both ways: Medellín is noticeably more expensive in dollars than a year ago, but anyone who bought in 2022–2024 has gained on the currency as well as on the price. Prices are sometimes quoted in dollars, but under Banco de la República rules a foreign-currency obligation that is not an exchange operation is paid in Colombian pesos at the TRM, and a non-resident's purchase counts as foreign investment that must be channelled through a Colombian bank or exchange intermediary.

What Foreigners Can Own — and How

Colombia gives foreigners the same property rights as residents. A non-resident can buy titled property 100% in their own name, with no local partner and no special permit. The usual sequence:

  1. Get a Colombian tax ID (NIT/RUT) and open a route for the funds through a Colombian bank.
  2. Sign a promesa de compraventa (promise-to-buy contract) and pay the deposit.
  3. Have your lawyer review the certificado de tradición y libertad (title history) and any liens or unpaid predial.
  4. Transfer the funds through the exchange market so the investment is documented with Banco de la República.
  5. Sign the escritura pública at a notary, then register it at the Oficina de Registro de Instrumentos Públicos (typically 1–2 weeks).

Failing to register the foreign investment with Banco de la República does not affect your ownership, according to the bank, but registration is the official record of the money you brought in, and the investor visa requires it. Keep every exchange declaration, and ask your lawyer how they support moving sale proceeds abroad later.

For residency, Colombia's Visa M Inversionista is open to foreigners who own Colombian real estate worth at least 350 monthly minimum wages, registered with Banco de la República. With the 2026 minimum wage at COP 1,750,905, that is COP 612.8 million, about US$193,000 at October 2026 rates.

The Wider Picture: Economy and Safety

Medellín hosted the 2026 Pan-American Athletics Championships on 26–28 June at the renovated Estadio Alfonso Galvis Duque. Antioquia's economy grew 3.0% in 2025 according to DANE's preliminary departmental accounts, ahead of the national 2.6%. The city's reputation for reinvention is long-standing: it was named the world's most innovative city in the 2013 Citi/Wall Street Journal "City of the Year" competition run with the Urban Land Institute.

Safety still needs weighing. The US State Department rates Colombia Level 3, "Reconsider travel" (March 2026). Medellín is not on its do-not-travel list, but the advisory warns about crime in Colombian cities generally. Choose a building with good security and research the street, not just the neighbourhood.

The Bottom Line

Medellín still offers gross yields near 7% in established neighbourhoods like El Poblado and Laureles, at average prices of roughly $2,000–2,500/m², with clear rules that let foreigners own outright. But the easy currency gains are behind it: new-home prices rose about 8–9% in pesos in 2025 (5.6% in the municipality in the year to mid-2026) and the peso is up about 20% against the dollar in a year, so new buyers pay more in dollars than 2024 buyers did. Run the numbers on net yield (about 4.5–5.5%), budget 2–3.5% for buying costs, and treat Airbnb income as possible only once the building's rules and the RNT are confirmed.

For investors weighing other markets, compare Medellín with Guanacaste's vacation rental market or Buenos Aires, where yields are lower but prices are still below their peak.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an apartment cost per square metre in Medellín in 2026?

Roughly US$1,400–2,500/m² on average, depending on the source and segment. Ciencuadras puts El Poblado at about COP 8.0 million/m² (about $2,530) and Laureles at about COP 6.3 million/m² (about $1,980) in Q1 2026, and premium Poblado micro-areas reach about $3,800/m².

What rental yield can you get in Medellín?

Global Property Guide puts the average gross yield at 6.88% in Q3 2026, with samples in El Poblado and Laureles between about 6.2% and 7.8%. Net yields are typically 1.5–2 percentage points lower after costs and vacancy.

How much property do you need for a Colombian investor visa?

At least 350 monthly minimum wages in Colombian real estate, registered with Banco de la República. With the 2026 minimum wage of COP 1,750,905 that is COP 612.8 million, about US$193,000 at October 2026 exchange rates.
