Latin America Real Estate — June 2026 Market Update

By Charles Brossy June 1, 2026 7 min read

Newer data available: see the October 2026 Market Update for what changed since summer. This June post was re-checked against current sources on October 20, 2026; corrections and dated updates are marked in the text.

Our June 2026 Latin America real estate market update covers seven markets: Costa Rica, Colombia, Panama, Mexico, Argentina, Chile and Uruguay. For each one you get a price benchmark, the latest published gross rental yield and the development buyers most needed to know about. The short version: Costa Rica, Panama City and Colombia posted gross yields above 7%. Buenos Aires prices were rising again in dollars, but slowly. And several big stories, including Costa Rica's electric train and Colombia's short-term rental rules, were further from finished than the headlines suggested.

Costa Rica — Train Financing Clears, Yields Above 7%

The Greater Metropolitan Area electric passenger train took its biggest step in May. On May 11, 2026, the Legislative Assembly approved the US$800 million financing in second debate, 56 votes to none: US$550 million from the Central American Bank for Economic Integration and US$250 million from the European Investment Bank. The project covers 52 km of electrified double track in two lines, Paraíso de Cartago to San José's Atlantic Station and from there to central Alajuela, with 26 new stations. Land acquisition has not actually started, though. In February the rail institute Incofer said about 145 expropriations were needed, paid from roughly US$24 million of national funds because the loans can't be used for them, and that the process could finish around 2028. Buyers paying a premium for "future station" locations are betting on a timeline measured in years.

Benchmarks: Global Property Guide puts San José-area apartments at about US$2,902 per square meter (its March 2026 headline figure). In its July 2025 breakdown, Guanacaste province had the highest average apartment price at US$2,990/sqm. Average gross rental yields were 7.63% in Q2 2026, down from 7.80% in Q4 2025, and Heredia was the strongest city at 8.69%.

Colombia — The Airbnb Crackdown That Hasn't Happened Yet

Registering in Colombia's National Tourism Registry (RNT) is already mandatory before offering tourist services, including short-term rentals. The requirement comes from article 61 of Law 300 of 1996 as amended by Law 2068 of 2020, and article 38 of that law puts obligations on digital platforms. What made headlines in December 2025 was a draft decree from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Tourism. It would have required platforms to hold a valid RNT of their own from December 18 and to take down listings without one. It would also have required a separate RNT for each unit, in the owner's name, and proof that the building's rules allow tourist use. The decree was not issued. In February 2026 the ministry paused it to talk with platforms and hotels, and its justification document is dated March 31, 2026, so the rules were still pending in June. (Update, October 2026: the ministry has since published a new draft that would take effect on January 1, 2028, require platforms to remove listings whose hosts cannot prove registration, and run verification through the chambers of commerce. It is still a draft.) We found no published data showing a drop in active listings.

For investors, that means checking that the building's reglamento de propiedad horizontal allows short stays before you buy, because that is the condition most likely to tighten. On yields, Global Property Guide's Q1 2026 survey put Colombia's average at 7.01%, with Bogotá at 7.71% and Medellín at 7.25% (El Poblado one-bedrooms 7.15%). (Update, October 2026: Banco de la República raised its policy rate by 75 basis points to 12% on June 30, 2026, held it at 12% on July 31, and raised it again to 12.25% on September 30, which makes local mortgage credit more expensive.)

Panama — Rents Up Double Digits as Supply Tightens

Panama City's rental market is running hot. Global Property Guide's analysis of Encuentra24 listings put average apartment asking rents at about US$14.70 per square meter in June 2026, up 13.5% year on year. Rents on leases actually closed through the ACOBIR MLS rose 8.9%, to US$12.57/sqm, in the first half of 2026. Supply is shrinking: resale listings fell about 40% in Costa del Este and more than 50% in Santa María, according to brokerage data compiled by TheLatinvestor. Panama's territorial tax system, which taxes residents only on Panamanian-source income (PwC), remains part of its appeal for foreign residents.

Prices: a Torcuato Di Tella University/Zonaprop survey put the average Panama City asking price at US$1,881/sqm in September 2025, 6.81% higher than a year earlier. Average closing prices on resale deals were lower, about US$1,726/sqm in the first half of 2026. In premium districts, Panama Equity estimated early-2026 resale prices at about US$2,500/sqm in Costa del Este and US$2,200 in Punta Pacífica, with pre-construction prices of US$3,800 and US$4,300. Global Property Guide estimated Panama City's gross yield at 7.57% in April 2026 (7.83% a year earlier) and the national average at 6.94%. Credit is the weak spot: new mortgage lending in January to May 2026 was 19.9% lower than a year earlier.

Mexico — Prices Still Rising Fastest Outside the Capital

The Sociedad Hipotecaria Federal (SHF) house price index rose 8.74% year on year nationally in Q1 2026. Growth was uneven. Quintana Roo led the states at 13.42% and the Guadalajara metro area rose 12.46%, while Mexico City had the slowest growth of any state at 4.47% (the Valle de México metro area rose 5.05%). Mexico City is still the most expensive major market. Banorte's indicator put asking prices at MXN 57,921 (about US$3,283) per square meter in January 2026, according to Global Property Guide. Fitch Ratings' mid-year 2026 housing outlook projects national home prices rising 7% to 8% by the end of 2026, citing high construction costs and tight supply.

Global Property Guide puts Mexico's average gross yield at 5.79% in Q2 2026, with Mexico City apartments averaging 6.77%. That is lower than in Central America, but the market is much deeper and more liquid. (Update, October 2026: the SHF index rose about 7.3% year on year in Q2 2026, a slower pace than Q1.)

Argentina — The Dollar Recovery Is Real but Slowing

Buenos Aires apartment asking prices were rising in dollars again, but slowly. Zonaprop's May 2026 report put the city average at US$2,462 per square meter, up 1.9% year on year. That was the smallest annual gain in 26 months, and prices remained 12% below their historical peak. Most of the old currency distortion has gone. Argentina lifted most of its cepo currency controls on April 14, 2025, allowing individuals to buy dollars without the old limits, and the gap between the informal "blue" dollar and the official rate was no more than about 5% through July 2026.

Benchmarks: Puerto Madero is the most expensive barrio at US$6,140/sqm and Lugano the cheapest at US$1,041/sqm (Zonaprop, May 2026). Gross yields are moderate, not the region's highest. Zonaprop puts the city-wide figure at 5.94%, or about 16.8 years of rent to recover the price, and Global Property Guide puts Argentina's national average at 5.46% in Q2 2026. Peso rents for a two-room apartment were up 32.5% year on year in May. For deeper coverage, see our Argentina buying guide for foreigners.

Chile — Metro Expansion Is a Long-Dated Bet

Santiago's next big transit addition is Metro Line 7: 26 km between Renca and Vitacura through Renca, Cerro Navia, Quinta Normal, Santiago, Recoleta, Providencia, Las Condes and Vitacura. It was about 40% complete in February 2026 and is expected to open in 2028. Anyone buying near future stations should model at least two more years of construction disruption before the connectivity premium shows up in rents.

Yields are the lowest of the seven markets. Global Property Guide put Chile's average gross yield at 4.82% in Q1 2026 and Santiago's at 4.91%. That makes Chile a market for capital preservation and liquidity, not income. (Update, October 2026: TOCTOC's InfoInmobiliario report put new Gran Santiago apartments at an average of 83 UF/sqm in the three months to July 2026, up 5.4% year on year, ranging from 41 UF in Lampa to 117 UF in Vitacura. Quinta Normal, on the Line 7 route, posted the largest annual rise at 37.2%, to 85 UF/sqm.)

Uruguay — Stable, Taxed and Moderately Yielding

Global Property Guide's Q2 2026 survey put Montevideo's average gross yield at 5.40%. The national average was 6.47%, but that figure now includes Maldonado, home to Punta del Este. One correction to a common claim, which our original June text repeated: Uruguay does tax real-estate gains. For non-residents, gains on Uruguayan property count as taxable income under the non-resident income tax (IRNR), taxed at 12%. Get advice from a local escribano or accountant on how your gain will be calculated.

New-build supply is supported by the "vivienda promovida" regime under Law 18.795 of 2011. The Executive can grant declared projects exemptions from income tax (IRAE), wealth tax (IP), VAT and, on the first sale, the property transfer tax (ITP). For a head-to-head comparison of Uruguay and Chile, see our Uruguay vs. Chile for Retirees 2026 guide.

The June 2026 Takeaway

Two themes ran through June. Rental income is strongest in Central America and Colombia: Costa Rica at 7.63%, Panama City at 7.57% and Colombia at 7.01%, with Panama's asking rents up 13.5% in a year. Infrastructure stories are long-dated: Costa Rica's train has its financing but not its land, and Santiago's Line 7 is not due until 2028. Argentina is a slow price recovery, not a yield play, and Chile and Uruguay offer stability at lower yields. Frequently Asked Questions

Which Latin American market had the highest gross rental yield in mid-2026?

Among the seven markets covered, Costa Rica led with an average gross yield of 7.63% in Q2 2026 (Heredia 8.69%), followed by Panama City at 7.57% (April 2026) and Colombia at 7.01% (Q1 2026), according to Global Property Guide. Buenos Aires was lower, at 5.94% (Zonaprop, May 2026).

Are Buenos Aires property prices still rising in dollars?

Yes, but slowly. Zonaprop's May 2026 report put the average apartment asking price at US$2,462 per square meter, up 1.9% year on year. That was the smallest annual gain in 26 months, and prices were still 12% below their historical peak.

Does Uruguay charge capital gains tax on property sales?

Yes. For non-residents, gains from selling Uruguayan real estate count as taxable income under the non-resident income tax (IRNR), taxed at 12%. Residents fall under the personal income tax (IRPF). Uruguay is not a zero capital gains tax jurisdiction for property.
