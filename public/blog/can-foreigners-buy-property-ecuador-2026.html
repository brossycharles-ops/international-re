Can Foreigners Buy Property in Ecuador? Yes: 2026 Rules and Costs

By Charles Brossy October 26, 2026 8 min read

Yes, foreigners can buy property in Ecuador, in their own name. Article 9 of the 2008 Constitution says foreigners in Ecuador “have the same rights and duties as Ecuadorians”, and the Constitution guarantees the right to property in all its forms. The main restriction is geographic: under the Ley de Seguridad Pública y del Estado, foreign individuals and companies may not acquire land within 40 kilometers of Ecuador’s land borders or in reserved security areas, except in populated and urban spaces. You buy through a public deed signed before a notary, pay a 1% transfer tax (alcabala) to the municipality, and register the deed in the canton’s Registro de la Propiedad.

What the Constitution Says, and the Border-Zone Exception

Article 9 is short: foreigners who are in Ecuadorian territory have the same rights and duties as Ecuadorians, in accordance with the Constitution. Article 66 (item 26) recognizes “the right to property in all its forms”, with a social and environmental function, and Article 321 guarantees public, private, community and other forms of property. Nothing in these articles requires you to be resident, or to hold the property through a local company.

The exception is in Title VII of the Ley de Seguridad Pública y del Estado. Article 39, as replaced by a reform published in Registro Oficial Supplement 279 on 29 March 2023, defines the border security zone as the land strip of 40 kilometers inland from the borders, plus 10 nautical miles of sea and the corresponding airspace. Article 40 then prohibits foreign individuals and companies, and Ecuadorian companies with one or more foreign partners, from possessing, acquiring or holding concessions over land in those zones and in reserved security areas declared by the President, “with the exception of populated and urban spaces” in them. The rule applies to individuals, so buying in your own name does not avoid it. Two further exceptions apply: a foreigner married to, or in a recognized de facto union with, an Ecuadorian for at least 5 years, and an Ecuadorian company whose foreign partners have been domiciled in Ecuador for at least 5 continuous years. If you are looking at a farm or rural lot anywhere near the Colombian or Peruvian border, have a lawyer confirm the distance and the land’s urban or rural status before you sign. Some older guides cite a 20 km zone; that was the rule before the 29 March 2023 reform, and the consolidated law in force today says 40 km.

How the Purchase Works, Step by Step

  1. Check the title and the cadastre. Ask the Registro de la Propiedad of the canton where the property sits for a current certificado de gravámenes (lien certificate) or certificado de bienes. Cuenca’s registry lists it as a requirement for registering any sale, and the city’s alcabala filing asks for an updated one. Confirm with the municipality that the property tax (impuesto predial) is paid, since Cuenca requires proof of payment for the transfer, and compare the price with the municipal cadastral value (avalúo catastral), which sets the minimum tax base.
  2. Agree terms, and use a preliminary contract only with your own lawyer. If you sign a promesa de compraventa (promise to sell) before the final deed, have a lawyer who represents only you draft it, with a deadline and the conditions for completion, and ask the notary what formalities it needs to be enforceable.
  3. Sign the deed (escritura pública) before a notary. Cuenca’s alcabala procedure asks for the legalized minuta (the contract text prepared by a lawyer) and the IDs of buyer and seller, and the page lists foreigners “with regularized immigration status” among those who can file it, so if you are not yet resident ask your lawyer who will file for you. Under COOTAD Article 537, the notary must first obtain a municipal certificate showing the property’s cadastral value and the tax due, and may not execute the deed until the alcabala and surcharges are paid; the receipts are bound into the deed.
  4. Pay the alcabala, and the seller’s gains tax, at the municipality. The tax belongs to the municipality where the property is located (COOTAD Article 530). Article 560 also bars notaries from executing a sale of urban property without the receipt for the impuesto a las utilidades y plusvalía, or the municipal treasury’s authorization.
  5. Register the deed in the Registro de la Propiedad. Cuenca’s registry asks for the public deed, the lien certificate and proof that the alcabala and/or plusvalía were paid. Until that registration is done, do not treat the purchase as finished. Afterwards, make sure the municipal cadastre shows you as owner, because the annual property tax is billed from it.

Closing Costs and Annual Taxes

Ecuador’s property transfer taxes are municipal and are set by the Organic Code of Territorial Organization (COOTAD). Several thresholds below are written in unified basic salaries (SBU); Ecuador’s Ministry of Labour reports that the SBU is US$482 a month from 1 January 2026, and the tax authority SRI uses the same figure (three SBU = US$1,446 in 2026).

Worked example: on a US$150,000 apartment with a cadastral value of US$110,000, the base is the contract price, so the alcabala is 1% × US$150,000 = US$1,500 before surcharges, and at most US$3,000 with the maximum surcharges. Our Ecuador alcabala tip summarizes the rule.

Annual property tax (impuesto predial). Owners of urban property pay an annual tax on the cadastral value, which is the land value plus any buildings (COOTAD Arts. 495 and 501). Each council sets its rate by ordinance within a legal band of 0.25 to 5 per thousand for urban property (Art. 504) and 0.25 to 3 per thousand for rural property (Art. 517). On a cadastral value of US$100,000, the band works out to between US$25 and US$500 a year, before any municipal fees and surcharges that appear on the same bill. If you own several properties in the same canton, their values are added together before the rate is applied (Art. 505). The tax can be paid from 1 January and is due by 31 December, and Article 512 rewards early payment: 10% off in the first half of January, falling month by month to 1% in the second half of June, with a 10% surcharge from 1 July. Our Ecuador property tax quick-read covers the rate bands and the exemption for residents aged 65 or more.

When you sell. The municipal gains tax is the one most foreign owners meet. Cuenca’s procedure states it plainly: 10% of the gain, reduced by 0.5 points for each year of ownership, until 20 years. If you sell a Cuenca apartment after six years with a taxable gain of US$40,000, the rate is 10% − 3 = 7%, or US$2,800. The law lets the municipality deduct the purchase cost, improvements, special improvement levies already paid, and the loss in value of money (Arts. 557 and 559). Whether national income tax also applies to your gain depends on the type of property and your tax situation, and we could not confirm the current text of the exemption from an official source today, so ask an Ecuadorian tax adviser before you buy. When you send the proceeds home, the 5% ISD applies: wiring US$150,000 out in one fortnight would cost 5% of (US$150,000 − US$1,446), or US$7,427.70.

Does Owning Property Give You Residency?

It can. The Foreign Ministry’s temporary investor residence visa (visa de residencia temporal inversionista) accepts a purchase deed for a property in Ecuador, in the applicant’s name and registered in the canton’s Registro de la Propiedad, for an amount of at least 100 SBU: US$48,200 in 2026. A fixed-term deposit of at least 730 days or shares in an Ecuadorian company also qualify. The visa lasts 2 years; the fees are US$50 to apply and US$270 when it is granted, with a 50% discount for applicants aged 65 or older. After more than 21 continuous months of temporary residence, you can apply for permanent residence before your current status expires. If you have a pension, the jubilado visa asks for a pension of at least 3 SBU (US$1,446 a month in 2026) plus US$250 a month per dependent; see our pensioner visa requirements and the Latin America retirement visa table.

Risks to Weigh

Safety. The US State Department rates Ecuador Level 2: Exercise Increased Caution overall. Its update of 18 August 2026, as reported by Primicias, kept that level but lists Level 4 “Do Not Travel” areas, including Nueva Prosperina, Pascuales and the areas of Guayaquil south of Avenida Portete de Tarqui, Durán, Huaquillas and Arenillas in El Oro, Quevedo, Quinsaloma and Pueblo Viejo in Los Ríos, and Esmeraldas city and the areas north of it. Manabí, Santa Elena, Santo Domingo and Sucumbíos are among the “Reconsider Travel” areas. Read the current advisory before scouting the coast.

Power cuts. About 75% of Ecuador’s electricity comes from hydroelectric plants, and droughts in 2024 forced rationing: in April, published schedules listed cuts of about three hours in Quito, Cuenca and Guayaquil, and from 23 September outages came almost daily, some lasting up to 14 hours a day, until the government announced in December 2024 that they would end for most Ecuadorians. When you view a property, ask whether the building has a generator and water pumps that work without grid power.

Title and informal property. Buy only property whose title is registered in the seller’s name, with a fresh lien certificate from the cantonal registry and a cadastral record that matches what you are buying. Be wary of lots sold on possession or a private document alone: without a registered deed you cannot check liens, and you cannot use the property for the investor visa, which requires a registered deed.

Liquidity. Plan for the possibility that resale takes time, and budget the exit costs from the start: the seller’s gains tax, any income tax, and 5% ISD on the money you send home.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a US citizen buy property in Ecuador?

Yes. Article 9 of Ecuador's 2008 Constitution gives foreigners in Ecuador the same rights and duties as Ecuadorians. The main restriction is the border security zone: under Article 40 of the Ley de Seguridad Pública y del Estado, foreign individuals and companies may not acquire land within 40 kilometers of the land borders or in reserved security areas, except in populated and urban spaces.

Does buying property in Ecuador give you residency?

It can. Ecuador's temporary investor residence visa accepts a property deed in your own name, registered in the canton's Registro de la Propiedad, for an amount of at least 100 unified basic salaries (SBU). With the 2026 SBU at US$482, that is US$48,200. The visa is valid for 2 years and costs US$50 to apply plus US$270 when granted; after more than 21 continuous months of temporary residence you can apply for permanent residence.

How much is the property transfer tax in Ecuador?

The alcabala is 1% of the contract price, or of the municipal cadastral value if that is higher (COOTAD Articles 532 and 535). Surcharges created by special laws can be added, but together they cannot exceed another 100% of the 1% rate. The law presumes the benefit is mutual and proportional to each party's share of the amount, so buyer and seller share it unless the contract says otherwise.
