Buying Property in Punta del Este as a Foreigner (2026): Prices, Taxes and Rules

By Charles Brossy Updated October 22, 2026 8 min read

Buying property in Punta del Este as a foreigner in 2026 is legally simple: Uruguayan law draws no distinction between foreign and local owners, and listings are priced in US dollars. The costs are less simple. Expect a 2% transfer tax on the cadastral value, notary fees of about 3% plus VAT, a 12% tax on rental income and on resale gains, and a rental market that lives on a three-month summer. Earlier versions of this article said Uruguay has no capital gains tax; that was wrong, and this page has been corrected and re-sourced as of October 22, 2026.

What Punta del Este Apartments Cost

We found no official price index for Punta del Este, so listing data is the best guide. InfoCasas' September 2026 guide to Playa Brava, the ocean-facing strip from Los Dedos to the La Barra bridge, gives these median asking prices:

The per-m² column is our own division of InfoCasas' medians. Branded towers sell well above these levels. The Fendi Château complex has two 27-storey towers with 150 residences of 190 to 550 m², and Maldonado has authorised a third tower, according to Montevideo Portal. Cipriani has confirmed a December 2026 opening for its hotel and casino, designed by Rafael Viñoly's studio, in a project Yogonet puts at an estimated US$700 million.

Rental Income: A Summer Market

Punta del Este has one high season, December through February, and AirROI's data shows occupancy swinging from about 20% in August to 69% in January. Data providers disagree widely on annual income: Airbtics reports median host revenue of about US$26,000 for September 2025 to August 2026, with 47% occupancy and a US$150 average nightly rate, while AirROI's average per active listing for the twelve months to September 2026 is about UYU 453,547 (roughly US$11,300) at 38% occupancy. Treat any single number with caution and underwrite on the low end, after management, cleaning, building fees (gastos comunes) and tax.

Taxes Foreign Owners Actually Pay

  • Transfer tax (ITP): 2% paid by the buyer and 2% by the seller, on the cadastral value set by the Dirección Nacional de Catastro, according to the DGI.
  • Notary: about 3% of the price plus 22% VAT, paid by the buyer, according to Multilaw's 2026 Uruguay guide.
  • Rental income: taxed at 12%: IRPF for residents (Multilaw notes this can be applied as 10.5% of gross rent or 12% of profit) and IRNR for non-residents.
  • Capital gains: the DGI taxes the gain on selling urban property at 12%, and the notary acts as withholding agent at the sale. For property bought before July 1, 2007, the seller can instead be taxed on 15% of the price.
  • Primary-school tax: a national tax on cadastral value at progressive rates of 1.5 to 3 per thousand, with lower-value property below the scale.
  • Wealth tax (Impuesto al Patrimonio): can apply to non-residents' Uruguayan assets above a minimum threshold; the rate depends on residence and filing status, so get advice before buying.

The Tax Holiday Changed in 2026

Many older guides promise a 10- or 11-year tax holiday for buyers who move to Uruguay. Under Law 20.446 (the 2025–2029 budget law), from January 1, 2026 the real-estate route requires property worth more than 12.5 million indexed units (UI), roughly US$2.1 million, according to Forvis Mazars. Qualifying new tax residents can opt to be taxed as non-residents on foreign capital income for the year they become resident and the ten following years. A further five years at half the normal rate is available only with a new property investment above UI 6.25 million or continued contributions, according to the amended tax code, and IMI Daily reports a 12% rate on most foreign-source income for residents without the holiday. People who secured the old regime before the end of 2025 keep its terms. The old, much lower investment threshold no longer applies to new residents.

Getting There and the Currency

American Airlines flies Miami to Montevideo's Carrasco airport only in the southern summer, resuming on December 3 this season, and MercoPress describes it as the only nonstop between Uruguay and the United States during the peak season. Punta del Este is a drive or a connection from there. Property is priced in dollars, but costs such as staff, building fees and taxes are in pesos: according to Ámbito, the dollar had lost 11.12% against the Uruguayan peso in 2025 by December 11. InfoCasas used a rate of 40.19 pesos per dollar on September 16, 2026, so check the current rate when budgeting.

The Risks, Honestly

  • Seasonality: most rental income arrives in three months, and winter occupancy is low.
  • Regional demand: Argentines remain the largest group of buyers, followed by Uruguayans, with Brazilian interest growing, according to El Observador, so Argentina's economy moves Punta's market.
  • Pre-construction risk: if you buy off-plan, check the developer's delivery record and how your deposit is protected.
  • Safety: read the current US State Department advisory for Uruguay before you buy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can foreigners buy property in Punta del Este?

Yes. Uruguay makes no distinction between nationals and foreigners: there are no restrictions on foreigners owning real estate, and foreign buyers have the same rights as Uruguayans.

Is there capital gains tax on property in Uruguay?

Yes. The gain on selling urban property is taxed at 12%, and the notary handling the sale withholds it. Rental income is also taxed at 12%.

How much does an apartment cost in Punta del Este?

InfoCasas' September 2026 data for Playa Brava shows median asking prices of US$200,000 for a 61 m² one-bedroom and US$325,000 for a 95 m² two-bedroom, roughly US$3,300–3,400 per m².
