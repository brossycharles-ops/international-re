Panama City Real Estate 2026: Prices, Yields and the Metro Line 3 Reality

By Charles Brossy April 20, 2026 · Updated October 20, 2026 8 min read

Is Panama City real estate still a good dollar yield play in 2026? Mostly yes, with caveats. Global Property Guide puts the city's average gross rental yield at 7.66%, rents and prices are in US dollars, and foreigners can own property in their own name. But new apartments now average about $2,669/m², and Metro Line 3, often sold as a reason to buy in the western suburbs, will not open until about 2028.

The Numbers by Neighbourhood

Panama City is not one market. New-build towers and resale apartments trade at very different prices, so always check which one a figure describes. Global Property Guide's square-metre series (Properstar data) puts new apartments at $2,669/m² in Q3 2026, down 2.8% on the quarter but up 4.0% on the year. Two patterns stand out. Resale apartments in central, older areas such as El Cangrejo cost far less than new towers and produce the highest yields: Global Property Guide's El Cangrejo samples run up to 9.52% for studios and one-bedrooms. Larger units in newer master-planned areas such as Costa del Este and Santa María yield less and are better treated as owner-occupier or long-hold purchases. All of these are gross yields; Global Property Guide says net yields are typically 1.5–2 percentage points lower once costs and vacancy are counted.

Short-term rentals are a different business. AirDNA's figures for the whole Panamá province (September 2026) show about 3,880 active listings at 69% occupancy and a $93 average nightly rate. No reliable neighbourhood-level data for Casco Viejo is published, so treat any agent's Airbnb projection there as unproven. Global Property Guide also notes that Panama City prohibits rentals shorter than 45 days in properties not registered as hotels, so confirm the building's status and rules before buying for short stays.

Metro Line 3: Not Open Until About 2028

Line 3 is a monorail from the Albrook hub across the Canal to Ciudad del Futuro in Panamá Oeste. Its first phase is about 25 km with 11 stations. Test runs began on 13 April 2026, and La Prensa reports that it is expected to start operating in 2028. At that point the elevated section was 81% complete, the underground section 42%, and the tunnel under the Canal 53%.

Earlier versions of this article said the line had opened in early 2026 and that homes near stations already carried a premium. That was wrong, and the premium figures could not be traced to any source, so we have removed them. If you are buying in Arraiján or Panamá Pacífico on the strength of Line 3, price in the possibility of further delays and value the property on today's commute.

The Dollar Advantage

The US State Department's 2025 Investment Climate Statement notes that Panama's official currency is the US dollar. The balboa exists only as coins at 1:1 parity (Global Property Guide). Rents, mortgages and sale prices are all in dollars, so a US-dollar investor carries no exchange-rate risk, unlike in Colombia, Mexico, Chile or Argentina. The same report notes the flip side: a dollarized economy has no central bank, lender of last resort or deposit insurance.

Mortgage costs are not low: the local mortgage reference rate (Superintendencia de Bancos data) was 6.50% in Q4 2025, and the rate on non-preferential housing loans was 6.23% in May 2026, according to Global Property Guide. Banks do not publish standard loan-to-value terms for non-residents, so budget for a large deposit or a cash purchase.

Foreign Ownership and Residency

Foreigners can buy titled property in Panama in their own name. The State Department notes that Panama has no specific rules on land acquisition by foreign investors. The main exception is the Constitution's ban on foreign ownership within 10 km of the borders (articles 290–291). Outside Panama City, much land is held only as rights of possession rather than registered title, so a title search at the Public Registry is essential.

Qualified Investor visa. Executive Decree No. 17 of 8 September 2026 (Gaceta Oficial 30613, 16 September 2026) replaced Executive Decree 722 of 2020. Permanent residency now requires at least $300,000 in a new, first-sale property or $500,000 in a resale property, held for five years. There are also securities ($500,000) and bank-deposit ($500,000–750,000) routes.

Pensionado visa. Retirees qualify with a lifetime pension of at least $1,000 a month, or $750 a month if they own Panamanian property worth over $100,000. Under Law 6 of 1987, they get discounts on medical bills, airfares and electricity.

Risks to Weigh

  • Safety: the US State Department rates Panama Level 2, "Exercise Increased Caution" because of crime (reissued 19 August 2026). It advises against travel to parts of the Darién and the Mosquito Gulf, far from Panama City.
  • Off-plan premiums: pre-construction asking prices run about $500–2,100/m² above resale in the table above, and new-apartment prices dipped 2.8% in Q3 2026, so an off-plan unit resold early can disappoint.
  • Infrastructure timing: 2028 is a target, not a guarantee, so do not pay today for a 2028 commute.

The Bottom Line

Panama City is not a cheap market. That distinction belongs to Nicaragua's Pacific coast, which carries far greater political risk, as our guide explains. Nor is it the only yield market; Medellín is an alternative priced in pesos, with currency risk. Panama's niche is a dollar-denominated, foreigner-friendly market where a well-bought resale apartment in a central area can yield 7–9% gross. Buy resale rather than off-plan for yield, check title and building rules, and treat Metro Line 3 as a future bonus, not the investment case.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average rental yield in Panama City in 2026?

Global Property Guide puts Panama City's average gross rental yield at 7.66% (October 2026). Its neighbourhood samples range from about 4.9% for large units in Costa del Este to 9.52% for small apartments in El Cangrejo. Net yields are typically 1.5–2 percentage points lower.

How much does an apartment cost per square metre in Panama City?

New apartments averaged about $2,669/m² in Q3 2026 (Global Property Guide, Properstar data). Resale asking prices in Q1 2026 ranged from about $1,770/m² in El Cangrejo to $2,900/m² in Santa María and $3,800/m² in Casco Viejo.

How much do you need to invest in property for Panama residency?

Under Executive Decree No. 17 of September 2026, the Qualified Investor visa requires at least $300,000 in a new, first-sale property or $500,000 in a resale property, held for five years. Retirees can instead use the Pensionado visa with a lifetime pension of at least $1,000 a month.
