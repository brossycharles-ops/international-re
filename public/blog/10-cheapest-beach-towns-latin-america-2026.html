10 Cheapest Beach Towns in Latin America 2026

By Charles Brossy May 15, 2026 6 min read

Caribbean condos in the Riviera Maya now sell for $3,000/sqm. Guanacaste beachfront regularly clears $400K. Even Tulum — once a backpacker secret — has crossed $5,000/sqm in its prime zones. But Latin America is large, and the affordable coast has not disappeared. It has just shifted. Here are the ten beach towns where serious value still exists in 2026.

No. 1 — Cheapest Entry

Máncora, Peru

Land from $45/sqm · Homes from ~$80K

Peru's premier surf town sits where the cold Humboldt Current meets warm equatorial water — meaning year-round warmth (24–28°C) and consistent waves. Raw land in the surrounding hills trades around $45/sqm, and modest beachside homes can still be found under $100K. The town is small, infrastructure is basic, and title due diligence matters — but for buyers with a long horizon and a tolerance for frontier friction, nothing on this list touches Máncora for raw entry price.

No. 2

San Juan del Sur, Nicaragua

Lots from $21K · Casitas from $85K

Nicaragua's Pacific coast remains 40–60% cheaper than equivalent Costa Rica addresses, and San Juan del Sur is its most established hub. Entry lots in the surrounding Tola and Popoyo corridor start at $21,000; one-bedroom casitas within five minutes of the beach run from $85,000. The coastal highway now under $400M in total investment has cut drive times from Managua and is the single biggest near-term price catalyst. Appreciation is tracking 7–10% annually. No. 3

Santa Marta, Colombia

2-bed from $86K · Cost of living from ~$900/mo

Colombia's oldest surviving city hugs the Caribbean coast against the backdrop of the Sierra Nevada mountains — a setting that should command far higher prices than it does. A furnished two-bedroom, two-bath apartment of 86 sqm was recently listed at $86,000. Santa Marta benefits from the same peso tailwind that makes Medellín so attractive to USD buyers, while the addition of a direct airport and growing Airbnb market is pulling up occupancy rates from their historically soft base.

No. 4

Pedasí, Panama

Land $30–80/sqm · Homes from $150K

The Azuero Peninsula is one of the last places in Panama where you can buy ocean-view land at $30–80/sqm and a modern three-bedroom home for under $200K. Pedasí itself is a compact fishing village with 95% fiber coverage, a small expat community, and healthcare costs running roughly 75% below US equivalents. Annual appreciation is tracking 8–12%, driven partly by a 35% jump in foreign property investment over the past three years. Monthly living costs for an owner run $1,050–$1,500.

No. 5

Huanchaco, Peru

Beachfront homes from ~$149K

Huanchaco is a reed-boat fishing village just north of Trujillo that has somehow held onto both its World Heritage identity and genuinely affordable pricing. Beachfront houses appear on the market from roughly $149,000 — a number that would be laughable on any equivalent Caribbean shoreline. Average rent in the city center is around $274/month, making it one of the lowest-overhead coastal bases in all of South America. Strong year-round surf draws a consistent stream of international visitors who convert to renters or buyers.

No. 6

Roatán, Honduras

Apartments from ~$100K · Dive tourism driver

The Bay Islands sit on the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef — the second-largest coral reef system in the world — making Roatán a globally recognized scuba destination. That dive-tourism engine underpins a short-term rental market that consistently outperforms the regional average. Entry-level apartments close to the water start around $100,000. Honduras's political risk profile is real and demands careful legal work, but for cash buyers focused on yield over capital appreciation, Roatán's reef-access premium at these prices is hard to ignore.

No. 7

Chitré, Panama

$756/sqm median · 15 min to beach

Chitré is technically a service city, not a beach town — but it sits 15 minutes from several of Panama's best Pacific beaches and offers a median price of $756/sqm, one of the lowest on this list for a market with full urban infrastructure. Studios average $97,500; two-bedroom homes $220,000. An expat community of roughly 500 North Americans has proven the livability case. For buyers who want Panamanian dollar stability, pensionado visa benefits, and beach access without the beachfront premium, Chitré is the logical base.

No. 8

Fortaleza, Brazil

Average apartment ~$108K · Strong USD advantage

Northeast Brazil's beachfront capital has 300+ days of sunshine, 25km of urban beach, and an average apartment price of roughly $108,000 at current BRL/USD rates. The real has weakened considerably against the dollar in recent years, which means USD buyers are effectively getting a structural discount on top of already-low local prices. Apartment prices rose 12.6% in 2025 — one of the fastest rates in Brazil — signalling that the market is not standing still. Foreign buyers need only a CPF (tax ID) to purchase with full title.

No. 9

Mazatlán, Mexico

Historic Center from ~$250K · 40–60% below Cancún

Mazatlán is Mexico's most affordable major beach resort, and the contrast with the saturated Riviera Maya is stark. The Historic Center offers condo units around $333/sqft, while the Marina district averages $318/sqft — versus $600–$900/sqft in Playa del Carmen and Puerto Vallarta. The Golden Zone runs higher, but still well below comparable beachfront elsewhere in Mexico. A major port, a functioning old town, and direct flights from major US cities give Mazatlán a liquidity floor that more remote affordable markets lack.

No. 10

Puerto Escondido, Mexico

Zicatela/inland from ~$180K · 9–12% annual appreciation

La Punta has gone premium — two-bed condos there now open at $350,000. But Zicatela and the older residential zones around Puerto Escondido still offer starter units from roughly $180,000, and the overall market is appreciating at 9–12% annually on the back of sustained surf and wellness tourism demand. That puts Puerto Escondido in a different category from the pure-value plays above: it is not the cheapest entry on this list, but it is the one most likely to look cheap in hindsight. Oaxacan gastronomy, a genuine international community, and improving flight links make it the most livable option for buyers who also want lifestyle.

How to Use This List

These ten markets span a wide range of risk profiles. San Juan del Sur and Máncora offer the lowest entry prices but require the most legal diligence and tolerance for thin liquidity. Mazatlán and Chitré offer lower upside but far more robust infrastructure, resale markets, and residency options.

A few principles apply across all ten:

  • Title insurance is non-negotiable in Nicaragua, Honduras, and Peru. Historical land records in all three countries are complex enough that even local lawyers miss encumbrances.
  • Currency matters. Colombia, Brazil, and Mexico give USD buyers an effective discount that inflates nominal yields; Panama and Ecuador eliminate currency risk entirely by dollarizing.
  • Beachfront restrictions exist everywhere. Mexico's fideicomiso zone, Costa Rica's Maritime Zone, Panama's coastal title rules — always confirm ownership structure before falling in love with a listing.
