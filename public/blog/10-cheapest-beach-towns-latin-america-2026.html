10 Cheapest Beach Towns in Latin America 2026

By Charles Brossy May 15, 2026 · Updated October 17, 2026 8 min read

The cheapest beach towns in Latin America in 2026 are no longer in the Riviera Maya, where Lamudi data put Playa del Carmen asking prices at roughly US$2,800–3,000 per square meter. Value has moved to smaller Pacific towns in Peru and Panama, to secondary cities such as Santa Marta and Fortaleza, and to markets that carry real political risk, such as Nicaragua and Honduras. Below are ten beach towns with lower entry prices, with what we could verify about each one, the ownership rules that apply, and the current US State Department travel advisory level. Prices are asking prices and move quickly, so treat them as a starting point for your own research, not a valuation.

No. 1 — Lowest Land Prices

Máncora, Peru

Beachfront land about $370–800/sqm (asking)

Peru's best-known surf town on the far-north Piura coast has some of the cheapest land on this list. Asking prices on Urbania in 2026 run from about $80 per square meter for large inland parcels to roughly $370–800 per square meter for beachfront lots. Foreigners can buy on the same terms as Peruvians under Article 71 of the Constitution; the only exclusion is within 50 km of a border. Peru's coast also has a 50-meter public beach strip and a 200-meter restricted zone behind it, so check exactly where a beach lot sits. The State Department rates Peru Level 2 and describes the SUNARP property registry as reliable. Liquidity is thin, so plan to hold.

No. 2 — Highest Risk

San Juan del Sur, Nicaragua

Cheap land, the highest political risk

San Juan del Sur is Nicaragua's most established Pacific beach town, and land prices are among the lowest on this list. The risks are serious, though. The State Department rates Nicaragua Level 3, “Reconsider travel,” and warns that “authorities may seize property, including land, homes, cash, and financial assets, without warning or legal justification.” Its 2026 Investment Climate Statement calls property rights “notoriously unreliable.” Nine beach homes owned by US retirees in nearby Tola, built on state land under the coastal-zone law, were seized in late August 2026; after press reports in October, an INSS source told Infobae the homes were being returned, without saying whether all nine would be (see our weekly news roundup). Our San Juan del Sur guide covers title checks in detail.

No. 3

Santa Marta, Colombia

Caribbean city · 1-bed rent about $750–1,100/mo (Numbeo)

Santa Marta, founded in 1525, is recognised by Colombian law as the oldest surviving city founded by Spain in South America. It sits on the Caribbean below the Sierra Nevada. Two things have changed for dollar buyers, however. The Colombian peso has strengthened sharply, from about 4,409 per dollar at the end of 2024 to about 3,175 in October 2026, so a dollar now buys about 28% fewer pesos than it did at the end of 2024. And Colombia is at Level 3 on the State Department scale, although the Magdalena department, where Santa Marta is, is not on its do-not-travel list. Numbeo puts a one-bedroom rent at about $750 to $1,100 a month, based on few contributors. See our Colombia guide for foreigners.

No. 4

Pedasí, Panama

Dollar economy · check title carefully

Pedasí, a small town on the Azuero Peninsula, is one of the cheaper ways into coastal Panama, with land listings well below Panama City prices. Panama uses the US dollar, which removes currency risk for dollar-based buyers, and the State Department rates it Level 2. The catch is title: the State Department notes that much of Panama's land outside the capital “lacks formal titles” and is held as possession rights, which can be converted to title under Law 80 of 2009 but face frequent legal challenges. Buy titled land, or budget for a titling process and a lawyer.

No. 5

Huanchaco, Peru

Next to Trujillo · 1-bed rent $250–385/mo (Numbeo)

Huanchaco is a fishing and surf town on the edge of Trujillo, in the same district as Chan Chan, the adobe city inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986. Running costs are low: Numbeo puts a one-bedroom apartment in Trujillo at about $384 a month in the centre and $252 outside it. We could not find a reliable public index of Huanchaco house prices, so get several local comparables before making an offer. The same Peruvian ownership rules as Máncora apply, and buyers pay the 3% alcabala transfer tax on resale purchases.

No. 6

Roatán, Honduras

Dive-tourism island · title risk

Roatán lies on the Mesoamerican Reef, and dive tourism drives its rental market. Legal work matters here more than almost anywhere on this list. The State Department rates Honduras Level 3 and estimates that about 80% of privately held land is untitled or improperly titled; it notes that title insurance is available but not widely purchased. Honduras also restricts foreign ownership within 40 km of its borders and shorelines, with exceptions for tourism zones and for one urban home plot of up to 3,000 square meters, so confirm how your purchase qualifies.

No. 7

Chitré, Panama

Inland service town · dollar economy

Chitré is a service city on the Azuero Peninsula rather than a beach town, close to the peninsula's Pacific beaches. It offers more urban services than the small beach villages nearby. We could not find reliable published price data for Chitré, so treat any quoted median with caution. For retirees, Panama's pensionado residency requires a lifetime pension of at least $1,000 a month, one of the main reasons buyers choose Panama. The same title caution as Pedasí applies outside the town centre.

No. 8

Fortaleza, Brazil

About R$8,963/sqm (~US$1,760) · +12.6% in 2025

Fortaleza, the capital of Ceará, has around 25 km of urban beachfront. FipeZAP put its average apartment asking price at R$8,963 per square meter in December 2025, about US$1,760 at October 2026 exchange rates, after a 12.61% rise in 2025, the fifth-fastest among Brazilian state capitals. The currency has worked against dollar buyers lately: the real strengthened from about R$6.19 per dollar at the end of 2024 to about R$5.08 in October 2026. Before buying anything, note that beachfront terrenos de marinha, the 33-meter strip measured from the 1831 high-tide line, belong to the federal government and are held under special tenure rather than full title. See our Brazil buying guide.

No. 9

Mazatlán, Mexico

Marina average asking about $192K

Mazatlán is a working port city with a restored historic centre and direct flights to Los Angeles and Phoenix. Propiedades.com put the average asking price of an apartment in Marina Mazatlán at about MXN 3.54 million (around $192,000) in August 2026. Foreigners buying a home within 50 km of the coast must hold it through a bank trust, or fideicomiso. Mexico is Level 2 nationwide, but Sinaloa state has its own, stricter warnings: the UK government advises against all but essential travel to Sinaloa, with the cities of Los Mochis and Mazatlán excepted. Check the State Department's restricted-area maps for Mexico before you travel.

No. 10

Puerto Escondido, Mexico

New two-beds from about $175K (Brisas de Zicatela)

Puerto Escondido, on the Oaxaca coast, is the priciest town on this list, but it is still far cheaper than the Riviera Maya. A new 70-square-meter two-bedroom in Brisas de Zicatela was listed at $175,000 in 2026. The fideicomiso rule applies to foreign buyers here too. A trust needs a permit from Mexico's Foreign Ministry (SRE) and runs for up to 50 years, renewable. The peso has also strengthened, from about 20.86 per dollar at the end of 2024 to about 18.40 in October 2026, which makes Mexican property more expensive in dollar terms. For comparison, Lamudi's June 2026 figures put Tulum at about MXN 45,995 per square meter, down 11.7% year on year; see our Tulum investment guide.

How to Use This List

These ten towns span very different risk profiles. Máncora, San Juan del Sur and Roatán have the lowest entry prices but need the most legal diligence and the most patience on resale. Panama and the Mexican towns offer more infrastructure and clearer processes. A few rules apply everywhere:

  • Independent title due diligence is essential, above all in Nicaragua and Honduras, where the State Department flags weak property rights and widespread untitled land. Hire your own lawyer, not the seller's.
  • Currency cuts both ways. The Brazilian real, Colombian peso and Mexican peso have all strengthened against the dollar since the end of 2024, which has made those markets more expensive for dollar buyers. Panama and Ecuador use the US dollar, so dollar-based buyers face no currency risk there.
  • Check the coastal rules. Mexico requires a fideicomiso within 50 km of the coast. In Costa Rica, the first 50 meters from the high-tide line are public, and the next 150 meters are leased by concession, which is not available to foreigners with less than five years' residence. Peru, Brazil and Honduras have their own coastal strips. Ecuador, by contrast, places no specific restriction on land purchases by foreigners.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can foreigners own beachfront property in Mexico?

Not directly. Within 50 km of the coast or 100 km of a border, foreigners hold residential property through a bank trust (fideicomiso). The trust needs a permit from the Foreign Ministry (SRE) and lasts up to 50 years, renewable, under Mexico's Foreign Investment Law.

Can a foreigner own the beach itself in Costa Rica?

No. The first 50 meters from the high-tide line are public. The next 150 meters are state land leased by concession, and concessions are not granted to foreigners with less than five years' residence or to companies more than 50% foreign-owned.

Which cheap Latin American beach towns carry the highest risk?

On this list, San Juan del Sur in Nicaragua and Roatán in Honduras. Both countries are at Level 3 on the US State Department's advisory scale. The State Department says Nicaraguan authorities may seize property without warning, and it estimates that about 80% of private land in Honduras is untitled or improperly titled.
