São Paulo Real Estate 2026: A Guide for Foreign Buyers

By Charles Brossy May 18, 2026 · Updated October 26, 2026 8 min read

The average asking price for a home in São Paulo was R$12,206 per square meter in September 2026, according to the FipeZAP index, and the index puts the city's average gross rental yield at 6.43% a year. Foreigners can buy urban apartments in their own name once they have a CPF tax number, and the city charges a 3% transfer tax (ITBI). The rest of this guide sets out what those numbers mean, what they leave out, and the tax, residency and currency risks a foreign buyer should weigh first.

What São Paulo Costs in Dollars

FipeZAP, the index Fipe compiles from online property listings, put São Paulo's average residential asking price at R$12,206/m² in September 2026, based on 737,899 listings. Prices rose 3.64% over the previous 12 months, less than consumer inflation (IPCA, 4.45%) over the same window, so in real terms São Paulo prices fell slightly. These are asking prices, not closing prices.

The dollar price depends on the exchange rate, and it moves fast. The Central Bank of Brazil's PTAX reference rate closed at R$5.04 per US dollar on 23 October 2026 and the Central Bank's series shows R$5.50 for 26 October 2026. At R$5.50 the city average works out to about US$2,220/m²; at R$5.04 it is about US$2,420/m². In 2026 so far the PTAX closing rate has ranged from about R$4.90 to R$5.50. The real can strengthen as well as weaken, and either move changes your return when it is measured in dollars.

Neighborhood Prices

FipeZAP publishes average asking prices for the districts that carry the most weight in its São Paulo sample. September 2026 figures, with the 12-month change and a dollar conversion at R$5.50:

  • Itaim Bibi (around Avenida Faria Lima): R$19,989/m² (about US$3,630/m²), +3.4% over 12 months.
  • Pinheiros: R$18,260/m² (about US$3,320/m²), −0.3%.
  • Jardins: R$18,028/m² (about US$3,280/m²), +6.7%.
  • Moema: R$16,520/m² (about US$3,000/m²), +5.0%.
  • Vila Mariana: R$14,879/m², +0.4%. Paraíso: R$14,108/m², +1.5%. Perdizes: R$13,455/m², +4.0%.
  • Bela Vista: R$12,703/m², +3.8%. Santana: R$9,288/m², +5.9%. Vila Andrade: R$8,372/m², +1.3%.

These are averages for whole districts. New buildings and top-floor units in Itaim Bibi or Jardins ask well above them, and older stock asks below. Fipe does not publish figures for smaller areas such as Vila Nova Conceição or Vila Madalena, so we do not quote prices for them.

Rental Yields: What the Index Says and What It Leaves Out

FipeZAP calculates its rental yield by dividing the average monthly asking rent per square meter by the average asking sale price per square meter and multiplying by 12. For September 2026:

  • São Paulo city: 6.43% gross (average asking rent R$65.78/m² a month, the highest of the 22 state capitals in the index).
  • FipeZAP index average (36 cities): 6.13% gross.
  • By size, nationally (not São Paulo alone): 6.74% for one-bedroom units, 6.45% for two-bedroom, 5.58% for three-bedroom and 4.86% for four bedrooms or more.

Using the same method on FipeZAP's district rents and prices (our calculation, not a Fipe figure), the gross yields come to about 5.7% in Itaim Bibi (rent R$94.2/m²), 6.6% in Pinheiros (R$100.1/m²), 5.6% in Jardins (R$83.9/m²) and 6.2% in Moema (R$85.3/m²).

What these numbers exclude: they are gross figures built from advertised rents and advertised prices for new leases. They do not deduct vacancy between tenants, the monthly condominium fee (condomínio), the annual IPTU property tax, agency or management fees, maintenance, Brazilian income tax on the rent, or currency conversion costs. Your net yield will be lower, and by how much depends on the building and how you rent it. FipeZAP's own report notes that the average yield was below Brazil's projected real interest rate for the next 12 months.

Can Foreigners Buy Property in São Paulo?

Yes. Brazil's restrictions on foreign buyers in Law 5.709/1971 apply to rural land (imóvel rural), with caps measured in land modules and by municipality. A city apartment in São Paulo is not covered by those caps, and a foreign individual can hold the title in their own name.

The practical steps:

  • Get a CPF. The Brazilian individual taxpayer number is required for the deed and for paying taxes. Receita Federal says foreigners can register whether they live in Brazil or abroad, and that registration is free, except at partner offices that charge R$7. Residents abroad fill in the online form and take the protocol and documents to a Brazilian consulate. See our guide on how to get a CPF from abroad.
  • Bring the money in through the official exchange market via a Brazilian bank or authorized institution, and keep the records: they matter for tax when you sell and for any residence application.
  • Sign a public deed (escritura pública) at a notary and register it at the property registry (Registro de Imóveis). The purchase is not complete until it is registered.
  • Pay the ITBI. In the city of São Paulo the transfer tax is 3% under municipal Law 11.154/1991 (as amended by Law 16.098/2014). The tax base is the property's market value (valor venal). A reduced 0.5% rate applies to part of the financed amount for lower-priced homes bought through Brazil's SFH housing finance system; the law indexes those limits, so check the current ones with the city. Notary and registry fees come on top and follow official state fee tables, so ask your notary for a quote before signing.

Does Buying Property Give You Residency?

It can, at a threshold. Under Normative Resolution 36/2018 of Brazil's National Immigration Council (as amended), a foreigner who buys urban property worth at least R$1,000,000 with funds from abroad can apply for residence authorization as a real-estate investor (about US$182,000 at R$5.50). The minimum can be up to 30% lower in the North and Northeast regions, but São Paulo is in the Southeast, so the full R$1 million applies here. Several properties can be combined to reach it, and the initial residence term is four years. Owning a cheaper apartment gives you no residence right. See also our page on Brazil's investor residence.

Taxes for Non-Resident Owners

  • Rental income: rent paid to a non-resident is subject to income tax withheld at source at 15% (Decree-Law 5.844/1943, Arts. 97 and 100, and Law 9.249/1995, Art. 28). The local agent (procurador) who collects the rent is responsible for withholding it. Residents of Brazil pay on rent under the progressive income tax table instead, which tops out at 27.5% in 2026; under Law 15.270/2025, monthly taxable income up to R$5,000 is effectively tax-free, with partial relief up to R$7,350.
  • Capital gains on sale: non-residents are taxed under the same rules as residents (Law 9.249/1995, Art. 18). The rates are progressive: 15% on the part of the gain up to R$5 million, 17.5% from R$5 million to R$10 million, 20% from R$10 million to R$30 million and 22.5% above R$30 million (Law 13.259/2016).
  • Low-tax jurisdictions: if you live in a country on the Receita Federal's list of low-tax jurisdictions, withholding on Brazilian-source income rises to 25% (Law 9.779/1999, Art. 8).
  • Your home country may tax the same income or gain. Whether a tax treaty applies depends on your country of residence, so take advice from a Brazilian tax adviser and one at home before you buy.

The Risks Worth Knowing

  • Personal safety: the US State Department rates Brazil Level 2: Exercise Increased Caution because of crime and kidnapping. It tells travelers not to visit informal housing settlements (favelas, vilas, comunidades), Brasília's satellite cities or international land borders. Choose the building, and the street, with that in mind.
  • Currency: the PTAX rate has ranged from about R$4.90 to R$5.50 per dollar in 2026 alone, a spread of more than 10%. Rent is paid in reais. A weaker real cuts the dollar value of both your rent and your sale price; a stronger one raises them. No one can tell you which way it will go.
  • Prices are not rising fast in real terms: São Paulo's 3.64% 12-month rise in asking prices trailed IPCA inflation of 4.45%.
  • Yields are gross: condominium fees, IPTU, vacancy, management and 15% withholding tax all come out of the 6.43% city average before you see any money.
  • Paperwork: you need a CPF, a Brazilian bank or authorized exchange channel, a notary, registration and, for non-residents, a local agent for tax. Use an independent Brazilian lawyer to check the property's registry certificate (matrícula) for liens and disputes before you pay a deposit.

The Bottom Line

São Paulo is Brazil's largest city, with 11.45 million residents at the 2022 census. It has monthly FipeZAP figures for both sale prices and rents, down to the district. That data shows asking prices of about R$12,200/m² across the city and R$18,000–20,000/m² in Itaim Bibi, Pinheiros and Jardins, with gross yields of roughly 5.6–6.6% before costs. The legal route for foreigners is clear, and R$1 million of urban property opens a residence route. Against that sit a volatile currency, gross yields that shrink once costs and the 15% withholding tax come out, and prices that are not keeping pace with inflation. It suits buyers who want long-term exposure to a large city economy, who can hold through currency swings and who will hire local legal and tax help.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a foreigner buy an apartment in São Paulo?

Yes. Brazil's restrictions on foreign buyers in Law 5.709/1971 apply to rural land, not city apartments. You need a CPF taxpayer number, which Receita Federal issues free to foreigners living in Brazil or abroad (partner offices charge R$7). The purchase is made by public deed at a notary and registered at the property registry.

How much is the property transfer tax (ITBI) in São Paulo?

3% of the property's market value (valor venal) under municipal Law 11.154/1991, as amended by Law 16.098/2014. Lower-priced homes bought through the SFH housing finance system pay a reduced 0.5% on part of the financed amount and 3% on the rest. Notary and registry fees are extra.

What rental yield can you expect in São Paulo?

FipeZAP put São Paulo's average gross rental yield at 6.43% a year in September 2026, against a FipeZAP index average of 6.13% across the 36 cities it monitors. That figure comes from asking rents and asking prices and does not deduct vacancy, condominium fees, IPTU, management or the 15% income tax withheld on rent paid to non-residents.
