Buying Property in San Juan del Sur, Nicaragua in 2026: Risks, Prices and the Legal Process

By Charles Brossy April 3, 2026 · Updated October 19, 2026 9 min read

Risk warning, October 2026: in late August 2026 Nicaraguan authorities took nine beach homes owned by US retirees at Playa Gavilán in Tola, Rivas, on the same Pacific coast as San Juan del Sur. Eight were handed back in October with no written guarantee. The US State Department rates Nicaragua Level 3, "Reconsider travel", and calls property rights there "notoriously unreliable". Every figure on this page was checked against the sources listed at the end on October 19, 2026.

Can a foreigner still buy property in San Juan del Sur, Nicaragua? Legally, yes: there is no general ban on foreign buyers. But this page used to call San Juan del Sur "Central America's last affordable beach town" and urge readers to buy before prices caught up. When we checked those claims against official data, the growth figures could not be sourced and the risks had grown. Tourism is well below its 2017 peak, short-term rental income is falling, and in 2026 the government took US retirees' homes on this coast. Here is what can and cannot be verified, and how to protect yourself if you still want to buy.

What Happened in Tola in 2026

The case closest to San Juan del Sur is the clearest warning. According to La Prensa, reporting a Confidencial investigation, nine US retirees who arrived in Nicaragua in the early 2000s built summer homes at Playa Gavilán, in Tola, Rivas, about 15 years ago. At the end of August 2026 the Procuraduría General de Justicia (the attorney general's office) ordered the homes taken, and social security institute (INSS) officials removed the guards while the owners were abroad. The owners say they received no notice or reason. INSS later offered to rent the homes back to them for $2,000 a month. Together the houses are valued at about $15 million.

Two details matter for any buyer on this coast. First, the homes were built on state land leased under the coastal law (Ley 690): the owners paid property tax and a $1,000 annual lease fee to the Tola municipality. Lawyers and business people quoted by La Prensa do not rule out that the government will justify the seizure by suspending that lease, and the paper notes that the land may fall within the Salinas de Nahualapa indigenous community, which is being surveyed for titling. Second, being up to date on taxes did not protect the owners. Eight of the nine homes were returned in the week of October 12, 2026, by phone call and without any document; the ninth owner, who says he also lost a hotel, a commercial plaza and a farm, had not had his property back.

This was not an isolated act. The State Department's 2026 Investment Climate Statement reports that from 2020 to April 2026 the government transferred 110 confiscated properties to INSS by presidential decree, and that the government has "reportedly weaponized its legal apparatus to orchestrate the theft of foreign-owned assets". The US Embassy told the Tola owners it does not intervene in property disputes and advised them to use the local courts.

Can Foreigners Own Property in San Juan del Sur?

The 2026 Investment Climate Statement says there are "no specific restrictions regarding foreign or non-resident investors aside from certain border and other properties considered important to national security". You can hold title in your own name. The rules that matter most on the coast:

  • Beaches are public. Under article 19 of Ley 690, the strip between low and high tide plus 50 metres inland from the average high-tide mark belongs to the state, and homes may not be built there. The next 200 metres inland is a restricted zone in which the state exercises control and use follows a coastal development plan. Rights acquired before the law took effect in 2009 are respected.
  • Lease is not ownership. As the Tola case shows, some coastal homes stand on land leased from the state or a municipality rather than on titled land. Find out which you are buying.
  • The 15 km border strip. Ley 1258 (August 5, 2025) put all territory within 15 kilometres of Nicaragua's land borders under state control and military oversight. The coast south of San Juan del Sur runs to El Naranjo on the Costa Rican border, so check whether any parcel falls inside the strip.
  • The registry. The State Department reports that the national property registry has been moved from the judiciary to the attorney general's office, that since 2019 most public access to registry records has been barred, and that "a cottage industry supplies false titles and other documents", particularly in tourist areas.
  • The courts. The 2025 constitutional rewrite made the judiciary subservient to the executive, and the government "regularly fails to enforce court decisions" on seizure, restitution or compensation.

Older title problems remain too. The US Embassy estimates the 1979–1990 government expropriated about 28,000 properties, many still disputed, and it advises "extreme caution" before buying, "especially coastal property".

What Property Costs: Asking Prices Only

We found no official house-price index for Nicaragua, and no source for the appreciation rates this page used to quote (7–10% a year for the Rivas coast, 12% for Tola and Popoyo, 35–50% over five years). Those figures have been removed. The specific lot and condo prices it listed for Popoyo and the Emerald Coast came from developer marketing we could not verify, so they are gone too.

What can be checked is listing data. On October 19, 2026, the Encuentra24 portal showed more than 333 houses for sale in San Juan del Sur, with asking prices on the first page of results ranging from about $82,000 to $1.6 million for an oceanfront house. Several first-page listings are in gated communities with monthly HOA fees. Asking prices are not sale prices, and with few buyers resale can take a long time. For a rough national benchmark, Numbeo's crowd-sourced data for Managua (October 2026) puts city-centre apartments at about $110 per square foot (about $1,190 per m²).

Rental Income Is Weaker Than Advertised

The old claims of 641 listings and occupancy that "will climb" are out of date. AirROI, a short-term rental analytics firm, estimates for San Juan del Sur municipality (August 2025 to July 2026):

That revenue is before cleaning, management, maintenance, utilities and taxes. The national tourism data behind it is weak. The Banco Central de Nicaragua's tourism satellite account shows foreign arrivals of about 1.08 million in 2025, down 0.6% on 2024 and about 42% below the 1.86 million of 2017. Foreign visitors spent $527 million, up only 3.2%. We found no source for the claim that "hospitality revenue grew 10% in 2025".

Economy, Currency and the Coastal Highway

  • Growth. The central bank reports GDP grew 4.9% in 2025. The World Bank (October 2026) projects 3.6% for 2026 and 3.2% for 2027, slowing as remittances and foreign investment, which the World Bank expects to be affected by shifts in US policy, fall. We could not confirm the "3.5% IMF forecast" this page previously cited.
  • Inflation. The central bank puts 2025 inflation at 2.7%, its lowest in several years.
  • Currency and banking. The córdoba has been fixed at 36.6243 per US dollar since January 1, 2024, and domestic prices must be quoted in córdobas. Banks must request foreign-currency purchases in writing 48 hours ahead, Nicaragua has lost several correspondent banking relationships, including Wells Fargo and Bank of America, and foreigners generally need legal residency to open a bank account.
  • Cost of living. Numbeo has no data for San Juan del Sur. For Managua (October 2026) it estimates a single person's monthly costs at about $678 and a family of four's at about $2,399, both excluding rent; basic utilities for an 85 m² apartment run about $151 a month and a meal at an inexpensive restaurant about $10. We could not source the old "$1,500 a month for a couple" figure, or "$2–$4 meals" and "$50–$120 utilities".
  • The Costanera highway. In November 2025 the government opened the first 30 km of the coastal highway, from El Naranjo on the Costa Rican border to Playa El Remanso in San Juan del Sur. The first phase covers 119 km in four sections through Rivas, Carazo and Managua. The finance ministry's preliminary estimate for the coastal highway project is $150 million, not the "$400 million" this page used to cite, and we found no source for a "30% cut" in Managua–Popoyo travel time.

If You Still Want to Buy: A Due-Diligence Checklist

  1. Hire your own lawyer, never the seller's or the developer's. The US Embassy keeps a list of local attorneys.
  2. Confirm what you are buying: registered title, a share in a condominium, or a lease or concession on state, municipal or community land.
  3. Order a full title search at the Rivas property registry, covering the 1979–1990 chain of ownership, and compare it with the municipal cadastre. Expect registry access to be difficult.
  4. Map the parcel against the 50 m and 200 m coastal zones, the 15 km border strip, and any indigenous community territory.
  5. Screen the seller, developer and HOA against the US OFAC sanctions list, and avoid property once owned by opposition figures, NGOs or confiscated entities.
  6. Test the money route: confirm your bank will send funds to Nicaragua and that you could move sale proceeds out.
  7. Budget for taxes and fees: the transfer income-tax withholding runs from 1% to 7% of the value, rising with price, plus notary and registry costs and annual property tax (IBI).
  8. Only commit money you could afford to lose, and plan for a slow resale.

The Bottom Line

San Juan del Sur is still a beautiful surf town with low living costs. But the "buy before it catches up" thesis does not survive the evidence. There is no reliable price or appreciation data, rental revenue is falling, foreign tourism is about 40% below 2017, and the US government reports that Nicaragua seizes foreign-owned property. In 2026 it did so to US retirees a short drive up the coast. If you buy here, treat it as a lifestyle purchase you can afford to lose, not an investment. Frequently Asked Questions

Can foreigners buy property in San Juan del Sur, Nicaragua?

Yes. The US State Department reports no specific restrictions on foreign or non-resident buyers apart from border and national-security areas. But the first 50 metres inland from the high-tide line are public, the next 200 metres are a state-controlled restricted zone, and some coastal homes stand on leased state land rather than owned title.

Is it safe to buy property in San Juan del Sur in 2026?

It is high-risk. The US rates Nicaragua Level 3, "Reconsider travel", and its 2026 Investment Climate Statement calls property rights "notoriously unreliable". In August 2026 the government took nine homes owned by US retirees at Playa Gavilán in Tola, Rivas; eight were returned in October without any written guarantee.

How much does a house cost in San Juan del Sur?

There is no official price index. On October 19, 2026, Encuentra24 listed more than 333 houses for sale in San Juan del Sur, with first-page asking prices from about $82,000 to $1.6 million. Asking prices are not sale prices, so get an independent valuation before you make an offer.
