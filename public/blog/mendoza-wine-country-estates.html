Buying a Vineyard in Mendoza, Argentina as a Foreigner (2026)

By Charles Brossy April 5, 2026 — Updated October 29, 2026 8 min read

Can a foreigner buy a vineyard in Mendoza, Argentina? Yes, but a vineyard is rural land, and that brings two sets of limits. The foreign-ownership caps of the Rural Land Law (Ley 26.737) apply again after the Supreme Court provisionally suspended their repeal on October 9, 2026, and parcels near the Chilean border may need prior approval from the national border-zones authority. On price, there is no official index: asking prices for vineyard land in the Uco Valley on the Mendoza portal InmoUp ran at roughly US$13,000–30,000 per hectare across the listings we checked on October 29, 2026.

Can Foreigners Own a Vineyard in Mendoza?

Foreigners can buy city apartments and houses in Argentina on the same terms as citizens. A vineyard is different. Ley 26.737 defines rural land as any plot outside an urban boundary, whatever it is used for, so a finca with vines is covered. The law sets these limits:

  • 15% cap: foreigners may hold no more than 15% of rural land nationally, and in each province and department (Art. 8).
  • 30% per nationality: buyers of one nationality may hold no more than 30% of that 15% (Art. 9).
  • 1,000 hectares: one foreign owner may hold at most 1,000 ha in the core farming zone, or an equivalent area set elsewhere by the rural-land council (Art. 10).
  • Water and borders: foreigners may not own land that contains or borders large permanent bodies of water, or land in border security zones except as the border-zone rules allow (Art. 10).
  • Who counts as foreign: companies more than 51% foreign-owned count as foreign. Foreigners with 10 years of continuous residence, or 5 years of residence plus Argentine children or an Argentine spouse, are exempt (Arts. 3–4).

Status in October 2026: President Milei's DNU 70/2023 repealed Ley 26.737 in its Article 154. On October 9, 2026 the Supreme Court suspended that article as an interim measure in the case Asociación Civil Árbol de Pie, without ruling on whether the decree is constitutional. The case returns to the federal court in Bariloche, and the suspension lasts until that court rules. On October 21, 2026 the Chamber of Deputies approved in general the government's private-property bill after adding a chapter that restores Ley 26.737. The bill goes back to the Senate, which passed it in August without that chapter; Senate committees had not agreed an opinion as of October 28. In practice, the limits apply today, but they could change again. Have your escribano confirm the current position and the province's quota before you sign.

Border security zone: Mendoza borders Chile, and part of the Andean side of the province lies in the Zona de Seguridad de Frontera. Under Decreto-Ley 15.385/44 (as amended by Ley 23.554) and the national rules for these zones, a foreign buyer needs prior approval (previa conformidad) to buy there; for rural land, foreign individuals must show 10 years of uninterrupted residence or go through an exception process. The province publishes a map tool (IDEMendoza) to check whether a specific parcel, including parcels in Luján de Cuyo, falls inside the zone. Check this before you make an offer, not after.

What a Vineyard Costs: Asking Prices, October 2026

We found no official or independent price index for vineyard land in Mendoza. The best evidence is current listings. On October 29, 2026, the "campos y viñedos" section of InmoUp, a Mendoza property portal, showed about 170 listings, including:

  • San Carlos (Uco Valley): 23.8 ha, of which about 22 ha planted, at US$700,000 (about US$29,400/ha).
  • San Carlos (Uco Valley): 101 ha, about 86 ha planted, at US$2.4 million (about US$23,700/ha).
  • Gualtallary, Tupungato (Uco Valley): 89 ha with a 66-ha vineyard project at US$2 million (about US$22,500/ha).
  • Agrelo, Luján de Cuyo: 5.3 ha with vines at US$200,000 (about US$37,700/ha).
  • Lavalle (north): 12 ha, partly in production, at US$75,000 (about US$6,250/ha).

These are asking prices from a handful of listings, not closed sales or appraisals. Price depends far more on water rights, plant age, variety and access than on location alone. Ask for the registered irrigation rights and the vineyard's registration with the Instituto Nacional de Vitivinicultura (INV) before you negotiate.

Houses and apartments. If you want a house in wine country rather than a working vineyard, InmoUp's observatory for September 2026 puts the median asking price at US$1,173 per m² for apartments in Mendoza province (up 6.4% in a year) and US$900 per m² for houses. Luján de Cuyo is the most expensive department, with houses at about US$1,218 per m², followed by Maipú at about US$1,022. Again, these are asking prices, and sale prices can be lower.

Wine Tourism: The Demand Side

The tourism economy is the strongest part of the story. The Observatorio Económico del Turismo del Vino (run by the province's tourism board EMETUR and the wine industry body COVIAR) counted 1,590,567 wine-tourism visits in Mendoza in 2024, up 27.8% from 2018. About 62% of visitors were Argentine and 38% international, with Brazilians, Americans, Canadians and Chileans most visible. Average spending was about US$18.60 for a winery visit with tasting and about US$66.60 for a meal.

The number of wineries open to visitors rose from 146 in 2018 to 230 in 2024. By zone: Luján de Cuyo 80, Uco Valley 50, Maipú 42 and the Gran Este 20. The Uco Valley added 26 wineries open to tourism over the period, the biggest increase, and the Gran Este had the fastest growth in visits (+94.4%).

We found no reliable source for rental yields or rental growth in Mendoza's wine districts, so we don't quote one. If you plan to rent to visitors, build your own numbers from current nightly rates and occupancy, and check whether the municipality requires a tourist-rental registration.

The Wine Industry: Read the Headwinds

The farming side is under pressure, and that matters if you buy a working vineyard. Official INV data for 2025 (the Mendoza share is 2024 data):

  • Vineyard area: 196,220 ha across 20,939 vineyards nationally, down from 22,039 vineyards in 2024, so about 1,100 fewer in a year. Mendoza held about 71% of the country's vineyard area in 2024.
  • Exports: US$661 million FOB of wine in 2025, down 7.2% and the lowest value since 2009.
  • Home market: consumption fell to 15.77 litres per person in 2025, the lowest on record.
  • Malbec: 46,890 ha at the end of 2025, 23.9% of the national vineyard, with 39,771 ha (84.8%) in Mendoza. Malbec wines exported US$404.7 million in 2025: 71.9% of the volume and 69.4% of the value of Argentina's varietal wine exports.

The takeaway: a vineyard that has to pay its way from grape sales is a hard business right now. Many foreign buyers instead look at a house with a small vineyard, a lodge near the wine routes or a plot in a managed vineyard estate, where the value rests on lifestyle and tourism rather than bulk grape prices. None of these is a guaranteed return.

Three Zones to Know

Luján de Cuyo: the closest wine district to Mendoza city, with the most wineries open to tourism (80) and the highest house asking prices in the province. The Agrelo listing above is here.

Uco Valley (Tupungato, Tunuyán, San Carlos): higher-altitude vineyards south of the city and the biggest increase in wineries open to tourism (+26 since 2018). Most of the planted-vineyard listings above are here. Parts of the valley lie closer to the Andes and the border zone, so check the parcel map.

Maipú: close to the city, with 42 wineries open to tourism and lower house prices than Luján de Cuyo.

How to Buy, and What It Costs

  • CUIT tax ID: since March 2, 2026, ARCA (the former AFIP) issues a CUIT to foreign individuals instead of the old CDI (RG 5803/2025). Non-residents can apply through a local representative.
  • Escribano: the notary checks title, liens and taxes, prepares the deed and registers it. For rural land, ask them to confirm the Ley 26.737 position and the border-zone status in writing.
  • Stamp duty (sellos): Mendoza's 2026 tax law (Ley 9680) sets 2% on real estate operations in the province, scheduled to fall to 1.75% in 2027.
  • No transfer tax: the national 1.5% ITI was repealed by Ley 27.743 in July 2024.
  • Annual wealth tax: owners resident abroad pay Bienes Personales at 0.50% of the property's value each year, as a single final payment made by a local substitute taxpayer (Ley 23.966, Art. 26).

Currency Rules

Property in Mendoza is priced in US dollars. Argentina lifted most exchange controls (the cepo) for individuals on April 14, 2025. One restriction remains: anyone who buys dollars at the official rate cannot buy dollar-settled securities for 90 days, and the central bank said in May 2026 that removing the remaining controls on companies is not a priority. The gap between the official and parallel ("blue") dollar is close to zero: about ARS 1,560 against ARS 1,565 on October 28, 2026. Keep records of every transfer into Argentina; you will need them to prove the source of funds and to move money out later. Controls have been tightened before and could be again.

The Bottom Line

Mendoza is a real wine-tourism destination, with about 1.6 million winery visits a year. But buying a vineyard as a foreigner means rural-land caps that are back in force for now, possible border-zone approval, and a grape industry with falling exports and consumption. Buy for the lifestyle or the tourism, budget conservatively, and use an escribano and lawyer who handle rural property. Frequently Asked Questions

Can a foreigner buy a vineyard in Mendoza, Argentina?

Yes, but with limits. A vineyard is rural land under Ley 26.737, which caps foreign ownership at 15% of rural land in each province and department, 30% of that share per nationality and 1,000 hectares per foreign owner in the core farming zone (or the equivalent elsewhere). DNU 70/2023 repealed the law, but the Supreme Court provisionally suspended that repeal on October 9, 2026. Foreigners with 10 years of continuous residence are exempt. Parcels in the Andean border security zone also need prior approval from the national authority.

How much does a vineyard in Mendoza cost per hectare?

There is no official price index. Asking prices on the Mendoza portal InmoUp on October 29, 2026 put vineyard land in the Uco Valley (San Carlos, Gualtallary) at roughly US$13,000–30,000 per hectare, a 5.3-hectare planted property in Agrelo (Luján de Cuyo) at US$200,000, and a 12-hectare finca in Lavalle at US$75,000. These are asking prices, not sale prices.

What taxes does a foreign buyer pay on property in Mendoza?

Mendoza's 2026 tax law sets stamp duty (sellos) on real estate operations at 2%, falling to 1.75% in 2027. The national 1.5% transfer tax (ITI) was repealed in July 2024. Owners living abroad pay Bienes Personales at 0.50% a year on the property's value through a local substitute taxpayer. You also need a CUIT tax ID from ARCA and a notary (escribano).
