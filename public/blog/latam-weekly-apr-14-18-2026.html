Weekly Roundup

Latin America Real Estate Weekly — April 14–18, 2026

By Charles Brossy April 18, 2026 · Updated October 21, 2026 5 min read

Four markets in the week of April 14–18, 2026: Costa Rica's investor-residency rules, Nicaragua's property tax and travel risk, Buenos Aires prices and yields as mortgages returned, and Chile's lowest mortgage rates since 2021. Every figure below was re-checked against official data or the index it comes from.

Correction, October 21, 2026: an earlier version of this roundup said Costa Rica raised its investor and rentista residency threshold to $200,000 in February 2026, put Buenos Aires gross rental yields at 7.3% and quoted several projections we could not trace to any source. None of that held up. This version replaces those figures with sourced ones and removes what could not be verified.

Costa Rica: The Investor Residency Minimum Was $150,000

No change took effect in February 2026. Under Law 9996 (the 2021 law to attract investors, rentistas and pensioners), the investor category requires capital of at least US$150,000 in real estate, registrable assets, shares, securities or productive projects. That was the amount in force in April 2026, and the Costa Rican Tourism Institute's announcement of the law gives the same figure. The rentista route was never an investment threshold at all; it is a test of stable income, at least US$2,500 a month under Article 82 of the immigration law.

What to watch: the law's five-year incentive window closed on July 14, 2026. Costa Rican immigration lawyers disagree about what happens to new investor applications after that. Some, such as AG Legal, argue the US$150,000 minimum in Article 8 still applies because only the law's tax incentives expired; others warn that new filings could fall back to the US$200,000 in the older immigration regulations, and Mora & Iglesias notes no official body has settled it. We found no official notice from the immigration authority (DGME) settling it, so confirm the current amount with DGME or a Costa Rican immigration attorney before you plan a purchase around residency.

Separately, the regulations under Costa Rica's Condominium Property Law (Law 7933) were replaced by a new decree published in October 2025. It took effect in January 2026 and covers matters such as virtual assemblies, condo-hotels and EV charging. For buyers in new developments it changes HOA governance, not the right to buy. Claims about Guanacaste prices rising "8–12% a year" and vacation-rental yields of "8–12%" did not survive checking. Global Property Guide puts Costa Rica's national gross rental yield at about 7.6% and describes a correction in luxury coastal prices that began in 2024. It publishes no index showing 8–12% annual gains.

Nicaragua: Low Property Tax, High Travel Risk

Nicaragua's annual real estate tax (IBI) is 1% of a taxable base set at 80% of cadastral value, so the effective rate is about 0.8% of cadastral value. Payment is due in two instalments, half in the first quarter and the rest by June 30. Cadastral values are usually far below market prices, so the tax bill is small.

The bigger issue is risk. The US State Department rates Nicaragua Level 3: Reconsider Travel, citing arbitrary enforcement of laws and the risk of wrongful detention of US citizens. Earlier versions of this page quoted "analyst" growth forecasts of 7–12% for Tola and San Juan del Sur and a national average price of $105,000; we could find no index or published analysis behind either number, so both are gone. If you are considering Nicaragua, read the advisory first and budget for title due diligence.

Argentina: Mortgages Are Back; Yields Are Below 6%

Two policy changes set up the Buenos Aires market of 2026. In December 2023, Article 249 of Decree 70/2023 repealed the 2020 Rental Law (Law 27,551) and let landlords and tenants agree contract terms freely. Peso-priced apartment listings in the city rose about fourfold (+316%) between December 2023 and March 2024, according to Reporte Inmobiliario data. In April 2025, the central bank's Communication "A" 8226 lifted the foreign-exchange controls ("cepo") on individuals.

Mortgage lending, almost dormant for years, returned through inflation-indexed (UVA) loans. The central bank's June 2025 monetary report recorded housing loans up 320% in real terms year on year. In the City of Buenos Aires, the notaries' association counted 13,953 property purchases with a mortgage in 2025, up 179.4% from 2024. That implies about 5,000 in 2024, not the "more than 11,000" this page used to claim.

Prices and yields are more modest than the old version said. The Zonaprop Index for April 2026 put the average asking price of a CABA apartment at US$2,460 per m². That was up just 2.2% in twelve months and still 12.2% below the historic peak. Neighbourhood averages ranged from about US$1,058/m² in Lugano to US$6,135/m² in Puerto Madero. The city's gross rental yield was 5.83% (17.2 years of rent to repay the price). The highest yields were in the south: Lugano 10.7%, Parque Avellaneda 8.1% and Nueva Pompeya 8.0%. Palermo, Núñez and Puerto Madero were the lowest, at under 5%. Those are gross figures, before expenses, vacancy and Argentina's tax on rental income.

For a side-by-side yield comparison with another high-income market, see our Montevideo rental yield analysis and the Buenos Aires rental yield analysis. For why Buenos Aires stands out on value, read Buenos Aires Is the Best-Value Capital City in the World.

Chile: Mortgage Rates at Their Lowest Since 2021

Chile's central bank data showed the average rate on UF-indexed housing loans falling for a fourth straight month to about 4.06% in March 2026, the lowest since December 2021. Lower rates arrived alongside a large overhang of unsold new homes: the Chilean Chamber of Construction (CChC) put national available stock at roughly 105,000 units in April 2026.

For foreigners the caveat matters more than the headline. Chilean banks generally lend only to borrowers with permanent residence and local income, so most foreign buyers purchase with cash using a Chilean tax ID (RUT). Projections of "3–7% appreciation in 2026" and claims that buyers routinely negotiate 3–7% off asking prices could not be traced to a published source and have been removed. For where to buy, see Santiago's supply squeeze and the Santiago vs. Lake District breakdown.

The Bigger Picture

An earlier version of this section quoted a "US$1.16 trillion" value for the Latin American real estate market. It came from a market-research press release we could not verify, and different firms' estimates vary several-fold, so we have dropped it. The takeaway that holds up: across these four markets, the cheapest entry points carry the most risk (Nicaragua's travel advisory, Argentina's policy swings). Meanwhile the safer markets (Chile, Costa Rica) are where financing and residency rules matter most to a foreign buyer. Check the official rule on the day you buy.

Frequently Asked Questions

What was Costa Rica's investor residency minimum in 2026?

Law 9996 sets the investor category at a minimum of US$150,000 in real estate, shares, securities or productive projects. The law's five-year incentive window closed on July 14, 2026, and lawyers disagree on whether new applications now need US$200,000, so confirm with DGME before you apply.

What is the gross rental yield in Buenos Aires?

The Zonaprop Index put the City of Buenos Aires average at 5.83% gross in April 2026. Lugano (10.7%), Parque Avellaneda (8.1%) and Nueva Pompeya (8.0%) were highest, and Palermo, Núñez and Puerto Madero lowest, at under 5%.

Can foreigners get a mortgage in Chile?

Rarely. Chilean banks generally require permanent residence and Chilean income, so most foreign buyers pay cash using a Chilean tax ID (RUT), even though average mortgage rates fell to about 4.06% in March 2026.
