Dominican Republic Real Estate 2026: Punta Cana, Las Terrenas & Cabarete

By Charles Brossy Updated October 26, 2026 14 min read

Should a foreigner buy real estate in the Dominican Republic in 2026? The basics are favourable: foreigners can own titled property in their own name, the annual property tax (IPI) only applies above RD$10.7 million (about US$174,000) of combined holdings, and the country drew a record 11.68 million visitors in 2025. Gross rental yields are solid but not spectacular — Global Property Guide puts Punta Cana at 9.07% in Q3 2026 — and since June 2026 a 10% capital-gains tax applies when you sell. This guide covers prices by area, taxes, residency and the risks.

The risks are real. Older land can carry title problems, infrastructure outside the resort zones is uneven, and the US State Department advises travellers to exercise increased caution because of crime. Most developer pricing is quoted in US dollars, but local running costs are paid in pesos.

The Macro Picture

Tourism scale. The Tourism Ministry counted 11,676,901 visitors in 2025: 8,861,169 by air and 2,815,732 cruise passengers. That is roughly double Costa Rica (2,943,991 visitors) and Panama (3,004,266, preliminary) combined, and the DR's 8.86 million air arrivals were about a quarter of the Caribbean's roughly 35 million stay-over arrivals in 2025 (Caribbean Tourism Organization). Punta Cana airport handled 51% of the DR's air arrivals.

Property tax. The IPI (Impuesto al Patrimonio Inmobiliario) is 1% a year on the combined DGII-assessed value of an individual's Dominican real estate above RD$10,695,494 for 2026, paid in two halves in March and September. Property held in a trust pays on the full value. At about RD$61.4 per US dollar, the threshold is roughly US$174,000; a property assessed at US$200,000 would pay about RD$15,800 (≈US$257) a year.

Dollar pricing and peso costs. Developers and most resort-area sellers quote in US dollars, but staff, utilities and maintenance are paid in pesos. Inflation has been moderate: the Central Bank reported 4.95% for 2025, within its 4% ± 1 target band.

Taxes in Detail

  • Transfer tax: 3% of the value, due within six months of the sale (Law 173-07; DGII Norma General 08-14); in practice the buyer pays it.
  • Annual property tax (IPI): 1% above RD$10,695,494 of combined holdings for individuals in 2026 (DGII Guía 17). Guía 17 also exempts the sole home of an owner aged 65 or over.
  • Capital gains: Law 30-26, promulgated on June 18, 2026, taxes an individual's gain on selling property at a single, final rate of 10%; before, individuals' gains were taxed as ordinary income (top rate 25%). A habitual home is exempt if the proceeds are reinvested in a new one within six months, as is a habitual home sold by someone over 65. The law does not define how the gain is calculated: a draft regulation was published for public consultation in September 2026, and it would not count an off-plan purchase as reinvestment.
  • Mortgage-related tax: KPMG reports that Law 30-26 cuts the 2% tax on real-estate operations such as mortgage registration to 1% in 2027 and abolishes it from 2028; ask your attorney how it applies to your purchase.
  • Rental income: individuals pay progressive income tax with a top rate of 25% in 2026 (a 27% bracket is added from 2027); companies pay 27%. Law 30-26 also raised the withholding on rent paid to individuals from 10% to 15%.
  • Inheritance: the DR does charge a succession tax — 3% of the net estate on property located in the country (Law 2569). Law 30-26 exempts small estates (under RD$1 million, or RD$2 million for direct-line heirs).

The CONFOTUR Tourism Incentive Law

Under Law 158-01 (CONFOTUR), classified tourism projects get an exemption period of 15 years counted from the end of construction, not from your purchase. Buying directly from the developer, the first transfer of a unit can be exempt from the 3% transfer tax, and the DGII exempts first buyers who are individuals from IPI for the rest of that period; later resales do not qualify. Ask for the project’s classification resolution. Details, sources and the June 2026 tax changes are in our Punta Cana real estate investment guide and our CONFOTUR explainer.

Residency for Investors and Retirees

  • Investor residency: a minimum investment of US$200,000 with a foreign-investment certificate from ProDominicana (CEI-RD) or the free-zone council grants permanent residency directly. The first card is valid for one year, then renewals are for four years.
  • Pensionado: a pension of at least US$1,500 a month, plus US$250 per dependant (Law 171-07).
  • Rentista: stable income of at least US$2,000 a month from abroad, plus US$250 per dependant (Law 171-07). Rent from Dominican property counts only if the capital came from abroad.
  • Extra benefits: Law 171-07 also exempts pensionados and rentistas from the transfer tax on their first home and halves their IPI.

Citizenship. Under Law 1683, foreigners can apply for naturalization after two years of uninterrupted residence, or six months for those who own property or found a business in the country. Law 1683 contains no requirement to renounce your existing nationality, and the Constitution lets Dominicans hold another nationality; whether you keep your original citizenship depends on your home country's rules. The Buying Process

The Dominican Republic registers land under a Torrens system (Law 108-05): ownership is evidenced by a Certificado de Título issued by the Registro de Títulos. The weak spot is the older constancia anotada — a registered right over an undemarcated share of a larger parcel. Law 108-05 stopped new ones being issued (except for condominiums); a deslinde, a court-supervised survey, converts the share into its own title.

  1. Hire an independent local attorney before you make an offer and get a written fee quote.
  2. Title search: your attorney checks the Certificado de Título at the Registro de Títulos for liens, encumbrances and disputes, and confirms the property has its own title rather than a constancia anotada.
  3. Promise of sale (Promesa de Venta): the binding contract that sets the price, deposit and timetable.
  4. Closing and registration: pay the balance, the 3% transfer tax within six months, and register the new title in your name or your company's.
  5. Total closing costs: the 3% transfer tax plus legal, notary and registration fees, and the 2% mortgage-related tax if you borrow — budget roughly 4–5% and confirm with your attorney.

Honest Risks: What Can Go Wrong

Title risk on older or rural property. Buy property with its own Certificado de Título. Land held as a constancia anotada needs a deslinde before you have an individually titled lot; avoid it unless an experienced local attorney handles the regularization. For a first purchase, titled condos from established developers are the lower-risk choice.

  • Pre-construction risk: quality and delivery vary widely between developers. Check the developer's completed projects and visit them before you pay stage payments.
  • HOA fees and building finances: resort condos carry monthly fees for pools, security and grounds; ask for the budget and reserve fund before you buy.
  • Short-term rental management: management companies take a share of revenue and results depend on the building and the manager; get written quotes and past occupancy data.
  • Crime: the US State Department advises travellers to exercise increased caution (Level 2) in the DR because of crime.
  • Infrastructure outside resort zones: power cuts, water pressure and road quality vary; check for generator backup and water storage.

The Bottom Line

The Dominican Republic combines unrestricted foreign ownership, a property tax that most mid-priced homes avoid, record tourism and gross yields around 9% in Punta Cana. It is not a free lunch: asking prices in Cabarete and Las Terrenas are now above Punta Cana's median, sellers pay a 10% capital-gains tax since June 2026, heirs pay 3% succession tax, and CONFOTUR exemptions last up to 15 years from a project's completion and only for first buyers.

The risks are manageable with basic due diligence: buy titled property, use an independent attorney, buy from established developers, and check a new build's CONFOTUR resolution yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can foreigners buy property in the Dominican Republic?

Yes. Foreigners can own titled property in their own name, with no local partner. Buy property with its own Certificado de Título and have an independent attorney check it at the Registro de Títulos.

Is there property tax in the Dominican Republic?

Yes, above a threshold. Individuals pay 1% a year (IPI) on the combined assessed value of their Dominican real estate above RD$10,695,494 in 2026 — about US$174,000. Property held in a trust pays on the full value.

How much income do I need for DR residency?

Under Law 171-07, pensionados need a pension of at least US$1,500 a month and rentistas at least US$2,000 a month from abroad, plus US$250 per dependant. Investing US$200,000 with a foreign-investment certificate gives permanent residency directly.

What tax do I pay when I sell DR property?

Law 30-26, promulgated on June 18, 2026, taxes an individual's capital gain at a single 10% rate, with exemptions for reinvesting in a new habitual home within six months and for sellers over 65 selling their habitual home.
