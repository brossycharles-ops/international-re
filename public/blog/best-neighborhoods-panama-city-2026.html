Best Neighborhoods in Panama City for Property Buyers 2026

By Charles Brossy April 30, 2026 · Updated October 30, 2026 12 min read

The best neighbourhoods in Panama City for a foreign buyer in 2026 are El Cangrejo and San Francisco for long-term rental income, Punta Pacífica and Costa del Este for modern towers, and Casco Viejo for heritage character at the highest price per m². On current asking-price data, central resale apartments cost about US$1,770–3,800/m² and earn gross yields of roughly 6–9.5% before costs.

Panama’s official currency is the US dollar; its own balboa circulates only as coins at par, so a dollar-based buyer takes no currency risk. The market is cooling rather than booming: registrations at the Public Registry fell 6.9% in the first half of 2026, and new-home sales fell from 4,737 in 2024 to 2,866 in 2025. That favours patient buyers. This guide compares eight areas using sourced figures; where no reliable figure exists, we say so.

Why Panama City: the macro case

The US dollar removes currency risk for dollar earners. Foreigners can own property on broadly the same terms as Panamanians; the exceptions — land within 10 km of a border, island territory, and a ban on foreign governments acquiring national territory except for embassies — do not affect city apartments. Panama’s multinational-headquarters regime (SEM) had 192 licensed companies in May 2026, part of the corporate tenant base in the central districts. The risks: a cooling market, high-crime pockets (the US State Department rates Panama at Level 2, “exercise increased caution”, citing crime and civil unrest, with “do not travel” warnings for parts of Darién and the Mosquito Gulf) and strict rules on short-term rentals.

Panama’s historic district was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1997 and, since July 2025, is part of the “Colonial Transisthmian Route of Panamá” listing. UNESCO notes that the Panama City components are vulnerable to urban development pressure; ask your lawyer which heritage permits any renovation would need. It is the most expensive area on this list: Panama Equity’s Q1 2026 asking prices are about US$3,800/m² resale and US$4,300/m² for new projects, and Encuentra24’s June 2026 average for the San Felipe corregimiento was about US$3,998/m² (down 5.1% on a year earlier). There is no published long-term yield for Casco, and its tourist appeal does not make holiday letting legal: rentals under 45 days need a tourist-lodging permit (see below).

Strengths

  • Heritage character and limited stock
  • Walkable to restaurants and the waterfront
  • Strong tourism footfall

Considerations

  • Highest prices per m² in this guide
  • Rentals under 45 days need a tourist-lodging permit (fines from B/.5,000)
  • Old buildings: commission a structural and title review
  • Heritage rules apply to renovation

El Cangrejo is the cheapest established central district in Panama Equity’s Q1 2026 survey: about US$1,770/m² resale and US$2,800/m² for new projects. Global Property Guide’s October 2026 sample gives the highest gross yields in the city here, about 7.0–9.5% (the top figure is for studios and one-bedrooms). Short-Term Rentals: the 45-Day Rule

Article 21 of Law 80 of 2012 prohibits rentals of less than 45 days in the District of Panamá — which includes Casco Viejo and every neighbourhood in this guide — unless the owner holds a public tourist-lodging permit. Fines run from B/.5,000 to B/.50,000, and advertising such a rental is also sanctioned. A bill to allow 1–90-day tourist rentals (Bill 301) had passed only its first debate, in January 2026, as of June 2026. Buy for long-term letting unless the building or unit is already licensed, and check the building’s own rules too.

The Buying Process in Panama: What Foreigners Need to Know

Global Property Guide puts a typical purchase at one to three months. The usual sequence:

  1. Offer and deposit: agree price and terms; a deposit is usually paid into escrow or held by a lawyer (the amount is negotiated).
  2. Due diligence: your lawyer searches the title at the Registro Público for liens, mortgages and encumbrances, and checks the building’s fees are paid.
  3. Promise to purchase (promesa de compraventa): the binding preliminary contract setting price, payment and deadlines.
  4. Deed and registration: the final deed (escritura pública) is signed before a notary and registered at the Registro Público; ownership passes on registration.

Financing: the average non-preferential housing-loan rate was 6.23% in May 2026. Our 2026 Neighborhood Picks

Best long-term yield: El Cangrejo, which has the lowest entry price and the highest gross yields in the sourced data — mainly on studios and one-bedrooms, so budget for more tenant turnover.

Best all-rounder: San Francisco: mid-range resale prices, steady asking rents across unit sizes and gross yields of about 7–8%.

Best for low-hassle modern towers: Punta Pacífica or Costa del Este. Resale prices are well below new projects; check each tower’s maintenance fees, which decide the net return.

Best for character, not yield: Casco Viejo. The most expensive area per m², with no published yield and a permit requirement for stays under 45 days. Buy it to live in or to let long-term.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best neighborhood in Panama City for rental income?

On Global Property Guide's October 2026 asking-price data, El Cangrejo shows the highest long-term gross yields (about 7–9.5%), followed by San Francisco and Punta Pacífica (about 7–8%). These are gross figures before vacancy, fees and tax. Rentals under 45 days need a tourist-lodging permit under Law 80 of 2012, so short-term letting is not a simple yield play.

What is the average price per square meter in Panama City?

Global Property Guide puts new apartments at about US$2,669 per m² in Q3 2026. By neighborhood, Panama Equity's Q1 2026 resale asking prices run from about US$1,770 in El Cangrejo and US$1,900 in San Francisco to US$2,500 in Costa del Este and US$3,800 in Casco Viejo, with new projects up to about US$4,300.

Can foreigners buy property in Panama City?

Yes. Foreigners can own property on broadly the same terms as Panamanians. The exceptions are land within 10 km of a border and island territory (special conditions only), and foreign governments may not acquire national territory except for embassies; Global Property Guide notes the first 22 m of beachfront cannot be privately owned. None of these affect city apartments.
