Retirement Uruguay

Retire in Uruguay 2026: Visa, Tax Benefits & Best Places

Updated October 27, 2026 · 12 min read · By Charles Brossy

To retire in Uruguay in 2026, most people apply for ordinary permanent legal residence, which has no fixed income minimum: as of October 2026, the Dirección Nacional de Migración asks a retiree or pensioner paid from abroad for a notary’s certificate stating the source and monthly amount of the income, and the Foreign Ministry says it must be “sufficient for maintenance.” The often-quoted figure of at least US$1,500 a month belongs to a separate, optional retiree regime under Law 16.340, which also requires a US$100,000 home or bond holding. Uruguay’s new-resident tax holiday was rewritten by Law 20.446 from January 1, 2026, and the gain on selling property is taxed at 12%.

This guide covers the residency routes, how the tax rules work after the 2026 change, property taxes, what rents cost in the main retirement areas according to Uruguay’s statistics institute, and how healthcare works for retirees. Earlier versions of this page described a “Rentista visa” at about $1,500 a month, a 0% foreign-income tax holiday of 11 years and a 7% flat rate afterwards; those statements were out of date or wrong, and the page was corrected and re-sourced on October 27, 2026.

Uruguay for Retirees at a Glance

🏛️

Same Rules as Locals

Uruguay’s investment law (Law 16.906) gives foreign investors the same treatment as nationals; you do not need residency to buy.

📊

Tax Holiday, With Conditions

From 2026, new tax residents can opt to pay non-resident tax on foreign investment income for the year they arrive plus ten years, if they meet Law 20.446’s tests.

🛂

Citizenship in 3 or 5 Years

The Constitution grants legal citizenship after 3 years of habitual residence with a family in Uruguay, or 5 years without.

🏥

Mutualista Healthcare

Private non-profit healthcare institutions (mutualistas) accept individual members; their fees and copays are capped by government decree.

Where the US$1,500 figure comes from: Law 16.340 says a foreign retiree must receive a minimum of US$1,500 a month to use its regime, which starts at the Uruguayan consulate nearest to where you live (or at Migración if you are already in Uruguay as a temporary resident). In return you may import household furniture and effects and, once, a vehicle without duties; salaried employment is barred unless the Executive approves it. If you do not need those customs benefits, ordinary permanent residence is the usual route and sets no fixed amount.

Ordinary permanent residence can be started online on gub.uy, followed by an appointment at a Migración office with the original documents. Migración publishes no processing time, and its UI-based fees are readjusted periodically. For the full document list, see our quick-read on Uruguay residency requirements for retirees. How Uruguay Taxes Retirees in 2026

Uruguay taxes residents mainly on Uruguayan-source income, with some foreign income brought into the net. You become a tax resident if you spend more than 183 days in a calendar year in Uruguay, or if the main core or base of your activities or economic or vital interests is there (Texto Ordenado 2023, Título 7, art. 2). As of October 2026, the rules that matter most for a retiree are these:

  • Pensions from abroad: Uruguay’s tax on pensions (IASS) applies to pensions paid by Uruguayan institutions, and the law excludes pensions that come from contributions to non-resident social-security institutions (Law 18.314, art. 2). Foreign pensions are also not on the list of foreign-source income that IRPF reaches, which is income from capital held with non-resident entities and gains on those assets (Título 7, art. 6). Confirm your own case with a Uruguayan tax adviser.
  • Foreign investment income: dividends and interest from abroad, and gains on those assets, are taxed under IRPF for residents. The general IRPF rate on capital income is 12%, and dividends are taxed at 7% (Título 7, art. 37).
  • The new-resident holiday (Law 20.446): people who become tax residents from January 1, 2026 can opt to pay IRNR instead of IRPF on that foreign investment income for the year they become resident plus the ten following years, which in practice leaves it untaxed in Uruguay. To qualify they must spend more than 183 days a year in Uruguay, or invest more than UI 12.5 million in real estate, or put at least UI 625,000 a year into qualifying investment funds, and must not have been tax residents in the previous two years. Afterwards they can choose to pay IRPF at half the normal rate for five more years (subject to investment conditions) or a fixed annual amount. People who opted for the previous regime by December 31, 2025 keep its terms. The regime was regulated by Decree 188/026 in August 2026.
  • Rent and property gains in Uruguay: rental income is taxed at 12%, and the gain on selling urban property is taxed at 12%, withheld by the notary at the sale. For property bought before July 1, 2007, the seller can instead be taxed on 15% of the price.

For more on how the holiday changed, see our Uruguay tax holiday tip and the tax section of our Montevideo rental yield analysis.

US citizens: the IRS taxes US citizens and resident aliens on worldwide income from all sources, wherever they live, so a Uruguayan tax holiday does not remove US tax obligations. Work with a cross-border tax adviser before you move.

Property Taxes and Closing Costs

Foreigners pay the same property taxes as Uruguayans. At purchase the buyer pays a 2% transfer tax (ITP) and the seller another 2%, both on the cadastral value set by the Dirección Nacional de Catastro rather than on the price, according to the DGI. Owners then pay an annual Contribución Inmobiliaria, set by each department, plus the national Primaria tax. Ask the escribano (notary) handling the purchase for the current bill on the specific property. Our guide to property tax in Uruguay for foreigners covers the rates and notary fees in more detail.

Healthcare in Uruguay for Retirees

FONASA, the national health fund administered by the BPS, finances care for people covered by the BPS health insurance (mainly workers in Uruguay), certain BPS pensioners and public employees (Law 18.131, art. 1). A retiree living on a pension from abroad is generally not in that system and joins a mutualista (an Institución de Asistencia Médica Colectiva) directly as an individual member, paying a monthly fee plus copays (tasas moderadoras) for consultations, tests and medicines.

The government caps how much mutualistas may raise these fees: Decree 317/025 allowed individual fees and copays to rise by at most 2.5% from January 1, 2026. Fees vary by provider, age and plan, so ask each mutualista for its current price list before joining. Earlier versions of this page quoted monthly costs and named a preferred provider without a source; those figures have been removed.

How much money do you need to retire in Uruguay?

For ordinary permanent residence there is no fixed minimum: you certify your monthly income through a notary and it must be sufficient for maintenance. The optional Law 16.340 retiree regime requires at least US$1,500 a month plus a US$100,000 home or bond holding kept for 10 years. For living costs, INE’s August 2026 average rents were about US$704 a month in Pocitos, US$864 in Carrasco, US$808 in Punta del Este and US$417 in Colonia del Sacramento.

Does Uruguay tax foreign pension income?

Uruguay’s pension tax (IASS) excludes pensions that come from contributions to foreign social-security institutions, and foreign pensions are not among the foreign-source income IRPF taxes. Foreign dividends and interest are taxed, but people who become tax residents from January 1, 2026 can opt out of IRPF on them for the year they arrive plus ten years if they meet Law 20.446’s conditions. US citizens still owe US tax on worldwide income.

What is the Uruguay Rentista visa?

What is often called a Rentista or Pensionado visa is, in Uruguay, ordinary permanent legal residence: Migración asks retirees and pensioners paid from abroad for a notary’s certificate stating the source and monthly amount of their income, and the Foreign Ministry asks for income sufficient for maintenance; no amount is published. The US$1,500-a-month figure belongs to the optional Law 16.340 regime. Legal citizenship can follow after 3 years of habitual residence with a family in Uruguay, or 5 years without.

Is Uruguay safe for retirees?

Uruguay has crime that retirees should plan for. The UK Foreign Office warns of bag-snatching, pickpocketing and muggings, sometimes armed, in Montevideo, advises against walking downtown or in the port area alone, especially at night, and says crime rates are generally lower in other parts of the country. Check your own government’s travel advice before you move.
