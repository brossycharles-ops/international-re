Belize Real Estate 2026: Ambergris Caye, Caye Caulker & the Honest Investor Guide

By Charles Brossy April 30, 2026 · Updated October 27, 2026 12 min read

Belize's case for foreign buyers rests on three things: English is the official language, the Belize dollar has been fixed at BZ$2 to US$1 since May 1976, and the country does not levy a capital gains tax on property sales. The costs are just as concrete. As of October 2026, a buyer who is not a Belizean or CARICOM national pays 8% stamp duty on the value above BZ$20,000 (about US$10,000). Short-term rentals on Ambergris Caye averaged 35% occupancy over the latest 12 months tracked. The Qualified Retired Persons (QRP) program asks for US$2,000 a month of income from outside Belize.

The caveats are real. Infrastructure is thin outside the cayes, the market is small enough that resale can be slow, and the US State Department rates Belize at Level 2 (Exercise Increased Caution) for crime. This guide covers where buyers look, what the taxes are under the current law, what the rental data shows, and what the QRP retirement program offers in 2026.

Why Belize: The Structural Case

Most North American buyers who end up in Belize arrive one of three ways: they searched for English-speaking Caribbean real estate, they heard about the QRP retirement program, or they came to dive the Barrier Reef. All three lead to the same small market.

The language advantage is real. In Panama, Costa Rica, Colombia, and Mexico, buying property means working in Spanish or through a reliable bilingual intermediary. English is Belize's official language, so contracts, title documents and court proceedings are in English. Being a foreign buyer involves less friction.

The currency peg removes most exchange-rate risk. The Belize dollar was pegged to the US dollar at BZ$2 to US$1 on 11 May 1976, and the Central Bank of Belize marked 50 years of the peg in May 2026. The Central Bank fixes its buying rate for US dollar instruments at BZ$2.00; selling rates are slightly higher. This differs from Panama, which uses the US dollar outright, but the effect for a dollar-based buyer is similar.

No capital gains tax on the sale. Belize does not levy a capital gains tax on property sales, and the Belize Tourism Board lists capital gains tax among the taxes QRP members are exempt from. That helps on exit, but two costs still apply. A foreign buyer pays 8% stamp duty, which also means any foreign buyer of your property faces that cost when you sell. And, as of October 2026, Belize Tax Service guidance charges business tax on gross rental receipts while you hold the property (see the tax table below).

Where to Buy: The Key Zones

Ambergris Caye — The Primary Market

Belize's largest island and the centre of the expat and tourism real estate market. San Pedro town is the commercial hub, with restaurants, dive shops, and most of the island's condo inventory. North of San Pedro are the resort-style condo developments and beachfront villas. Ambergris Caye is also where the rental data is deepest: over the 12 months tracked to October 2026, an average short-term rental listing there earned BZ$75,100 (about US$37,550) a year at 35% occupancy and an average daily rate of BZ$538 (about US$269). Revenue was down 7% and occupancy down 6% from the prior 12 months, while the number of active listings across the Belize District market grew 12% year on year. Check occupancy at the specific building before buying.

Caye Caulker — The Smaller, Slower Island

Smaller, slower, and cheaper than Ambergris. Known for its "Go Slow" culture, with golf carts rather than cars and a younger, more budget-conscious visitor base. For buyers who want beach exposure without Ambergris price tags, it is the usual alternative. The trade-offs are lower nightly rates and a thinner resale market.

Placencia Peninsula — Best for Low-Density Lifestyle

A long, narrow peninsula on the southern mainland coast. It appeals to retirees who want more land, privacy, and a quieter life than the cayes offer. The Placencia Village to Maya Beach corridor has the best infrastructure. Short-term rentals exist, but tourism volume is lower than on Ambergris. This is a lifestyle-first, yield-second market.

Corozal — The Budget Retirement Zone

Northern Belize, near the Mexican border. It is not a beach market in the caye sense, since Corozal Bay is calm and shallow. It is where buyers look for the lowest prices in the country, and it draws budget-conscious North American retirees. The trade-offs are limited medical facilities and entertainment, and daily life that leans toward Chetumal across the border in Mexico.

The QRP Program: What It Actually Offers

Belize's Qualified Retired Persons (QRP) program is run by the Belize Tourism Board (BTB) under the Retired Persons Incentives Act. These are the terms on the BTB's program page, as of October 2026:

  • Age requirement: 40 years or older. Dependants can include a spouse and children under 18.
  • Income requirement: US$2,000 a month (US$24,000 a year) of retirement income from outside Belize, in US dollars, pounds sterling, euros or Canadian dollars. Pensions, annuities, Social Security, investment accounts and personal savings all count. You must also sign an undertaking to deposit that amount in a bank, credit union or licensed financial institution in Belize.
  • Other conditions: a security clearance by the Ministry of National Security, and at least 30 consecutive days in Belize every year. The program is open to citizens of Commonwealth countries, the United States, EU member states, all South American countries and listed Asia-Pacific countries.
  • Status granted: a QRP Resident Card for you and your dependants, allowing multiple entries without a visa. The card is renewed every year. The BTB page does not describe the status as permanent residence. If a path to citizenship matters to you, ask an immigration attorney how QRP time counts.
  • Fees (US dollars, non-refundable): US$150 application fee, US$1,000 applicant fee, US$750 per dependant, US$200 per QRP ID card, and US$25 a year to renew the card. A single applicant pays US$1,350 in total.
  • Key benefits:
    • Duty- and tax-free import of household and personal effects within the first year, with no dollar limit
    • Duty-free import of a motor vehicle no more than 5 years old (a replacement every three years, once the previous vehicle is properly disposed of), a light aircraft under 17,000 kg, and a motor boat
    • Exemption from all taxes and duties on income from outside Belize, and from capital gains tax and inheritance tax
  • Running a business: a QRP who wants to carry on business in Belize needs BTB approval. That requires a business plan that employs at least 5 Belizean citizens, at least BZ$1 million (about US$500,000) of investment capital, and proof of at least 90 days' residence in Belize.
  • Processing time: the BTB page does not state one. Get a current estimate from the BTB before you plan a move date around approval.

QRP vs. Other Regional Retirement Visas

As of October 2026, Belize's US$2,000 a month is higher than its neighbours' thresholds. Panama's Pensionado needs B/.1,000 (US$1,000) a month and gives permanent residence. Costa Rica's pensionado needs US$1,000 under Article 81 of Law 8764. Ecuador's jubilado visa needs 3 basic wages, US$1,446 a month in 2026. The Dominican Republic's pensioner route needs US$1,500 under Law 171-07. Belize's advantages are an English-language process and broad import-duty exemptions. The inheritance point is easy to miss. Section 72 of the Stamp Duties Act charges duty on transfers of land "by sale, exchange or gift, or pursuant to testamentary disposition or devolution on intestacy." Only transfers by will or intestacy to Belizean citizens or Belizean companies are exempt. A foreign heir inheriting a Belize property should budget for the same 8% duty a foreign buyer pays. Talk to a Belizean attorney about how you hold title before you buy.

The Buying Process

Belize gained independence from Britain in 1981 and kept English common law. The process will look familiar to North American buyers:

  1. Offer and acceptance: a letter of intent, usually with a deposit. Agree in writing on when the deposit becomes non-refundable.
  2. Attorney engagement: hire a Belizean attorney who works for you, not the seller or developer. They search the title at the Lands Registry and confirm whether the property is freehold or leasehold.
  3. Sale agreement: the binding contract. It should say who pays stamp duty and when the balance is due.
  4. Title transfer: the transfer is stamped, and a new land certificate is issued in the buyer's name. Stamp duty (8% for foreign buyers on the value above BZ$20,000) and registration fees are paid at this stage.

Leasehold vs. freehold: some land in Belize, particularly national land, is held under a lease from the government rather than as freehold title. A lease is not the same as owning the land outright. Your attorney's title search should show which you are buying. Get that in writing before you pay more than a refundable deposit.

Honest Risks

  • Crime and the travel advisory: the US State Department rates Belize at Level 2 (Exercise Increased Caution) because of crime, and advises travellers to reconsider travel to Southside Belize City. That is the advisory issued March 12, 2026, still current when we checked in October 2026. Read the full advisory before you visit, and ask your attorney and neighbours about security at the specific property.
  • Market size and liquidity: Belize is a small market, and the buyer pool is mostly North American expats and retirees. Foreign buyers also pay 8% stamp duty, which adds to the cost of buying your property from you. Expect resale to take longer than in a large city market.
  • Rental supply is growing faster than demand: active short-term listings in the Belize District market rose 12% year on year, while Ambergris Caye revenue per listing fell 7% and occupancy fell 6%. Do not assume past price gains or rental income will repeat.
  • Infrastructure: power outages and water-pressure problems happen on the cayes. Ask whether the building has generator backup. Check internet service at the specific address.
  • Hurricane exposure: Belize sits in the Caribbean hurricane belt. Get insurance quotes before you commit, and have a surveyor check how the building is constructed. Newer developments and older structures vary widely.
  • Healthcare: San Pedro has limited medical facilities. Serious problems mean travel to Belize City or abroad. Plan for evacuation cover if you have ongoing specialist needs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there capital gains tax in Belize?

No. Belize does not levy a capital gains tax on property sales, and the Belize Tourism Board lists capital gains tax among the taxes QRP members are exempt from. The main costs on a property are elsewhere. A buyer who is not a Belizean or CARICOM national pays 8% stamp duty on the value above BZ$20,000. Rental income carries a 3% business tax on gross receipts. And a foreign heir pays 8% stamp duty on inherited land, even though Belize has no inheritance tax.

What is the QRP visa in Belize?

The Qualified Retired Persons (QRP) program, run by the Belize Tourism Board, is open to people aged 40 or older with at least US$2,000 a month (US$24,000 a year) of retirement income from outside Belize. As of October 2026, members get a QRP Resident Card, renewed annually, and must spend at least 30 consecutive days a year in Belize. Benefits include duty-free import of household effects in the first year, plus a vehicle, light aircraft or boat. Members are also exempt from tax on foreign income, capital gains tax and inheritance tax. Fees are US$150 to apply, US$1,000 per applicant, US$750 per dependant and US$200 per ID card, so a single applicant pays US$1,350.

What are rental yields like on Ambergris Caye?

We don't publish a yield figure, because it depends on what you pay. Over the 12 months tracked to October 2026, an average Ambergris Caye short-term rental listing earned BZ$75,100 (about US$37,550) gross. Occupancy averaged 35% and the average daily rate was BZ$538 (about US$269). Revenue was down 7% on the prior 12 months. Divide a unit's own trailing revenue by your all-in cost to get a gross yield. That figure is before the 3% business tax on gross rent, management, HOA dues, land tax, insurance and maintenance.

Can foreigners own land in Belize?

Yes. Foreigners can hold freehold title in their own name, and the title system follows English common law. Foreign buyers pay a higher stamp duty than citizens: 8% of the value above BZ$20,000, against 5% for Belizean citizens, Belizean companies and qualifying CARICOM nationals. Some land, particularly national land, is held under a government lease rather than as freehold, so have your attorney confirm which you are buying.
