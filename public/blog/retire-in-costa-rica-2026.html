Retire in Costa Rica 2026: Pensionado Visa, Caja & Costs

April 29, 2026 · Updated October 23, 2026 · 12 min read · By Charles Brossy

Updated October 23, 2026: visa rules, health insurance, costs and comparisons re-checked against official sources.

To retire in Costa Rica in 2026, most foreigners use the pensionado residency: you must prove a permanent pension of at least US$1,000 a month, enrol in the national health insurer (CCSS, known as "the Caja"), and renew every two years until you can apply for permanent residence after three. This guide covers the visa options, the health insurance rule, what living costs actually look like on published data, and how Costa Rica compares with Panama and Colombia.

Costa Rica abolished its army as a permanent institution in its 1949 Constitution, and life expectancy at birth was about 81 years in 2024, according to the World Bank. The US State Department rates it Level 2, "Exercise Increased Caution", mainly because of crime.

The honest summary: the pensionado route is simple, but Costa Rica has become more expensive for dollar retirees. The colón has climbed to its strongest level against the dollar in the Central Bank's published series, so a dollar pension buys fewer colones than it used to. Health insurance through the Caja is mandatory and is a monthly fixed cost on top of everything else.

Costa Rica Retirement Visas: Your Options

Pensionado

US$1,000/mo

For a permanent, stable pension from abroad (for example US Social Security or a company pension). Temporary residence for up to two years, renewable. Caja enrolment required.

Rentista

US$2,500/mo

For stable passive income of at least US$2,500 a month for at least two years. Since the 2023 regulation, a bank deposit paid back to you in monthly instalments no longer counts as income.

Inversionista

US$150,000*

Law 9996 set the investment amount at US$150,000. Only its five-year tax-incentive window (Article 5) closed on July 14, 2026; the law firms we checked say US$150,000 still applies and that immigration's (DGME) checklist still shows it. Confirm with DGME or a lawyer before you invest.

Permanent residence

After 3 years

Open to people who have held temporary residence for three consecutive years. Naturalisation takes 7 years of official residence for most nationalities (including US citizens) and 5 years for Central Americans, Spaniards and Ibero-Americans by birth.

US Social Security can count: the requirement is a permanent pension of at least US$1,000 a month, and Costa Rican immigration lawyers list Social Security retirees among eligible applicants. Foreign documents must be apostilled or legalised, and immigration's document list changes, so check the current DGME checklist before you gather paperwork.

Caja (CCSS) Health Insurance: The Non-Negotiable Cost

Pensionado and rentista residents must be insured with the Caja Costarricense de Seguro Social (CCSS), and proof of up-to-date coverage is required when you renew your residence. Foreign residents who don't work join as asegurados migrantes (migrant insured persons). The CCSS sets the income your contribution is based on, which cannot be lower than its minimum contribution base or the income your immigration category requires (US$1,000 a month for a pensionado), and applies its board's contribution scale. New members wait six months before coverage for complex surgery and pre-existing conditions.

We have removed the percentage table this guide used to show (12.5% of declared income, or $125 a month on $1,000). We could not find an official 2026 CCSS scale for voluntary insured residents to confirm it, so ask the CCSS for a quote based on your declared income before you budget. Many retirees also buy private health insurance for faster specialist appointments; premiums depend heavily on age, so get quotes rather than relying on averages.

Falling behind on the Caja puts your residency at risk: renewal requires proof of CCSS coverage. Treat the contribution as a fixed cost from your first month of residence.

Where Retirees Look

Central Valley

Escazú / Santa Ana

San José's western suburbs, close to the capital's private hospitals; Hospital Clínica Bíblica, for example, runs a site in Santa Ana as well as its central San José hospital. Expect rents above the national averages above.

Central Valley

Atenas and Grecia

Smaller towns west of San José, within reach of the international airport. Atenas is often marketed as having "the world's best climate", but we could not find the National Geographic study usually credited for it, so treat that as marketing.

Pacific Dry

Guanacaste (Tamarindo, Nosara, Flamingo)

The dry northern Pacific coast, served by Liberia's Guanacaste airport (LIR), which has direct flights from the United States. Most beach towns sit near the maritime zone, where land is held by concession rather than title.

Central Pacific

Manuel Antonio / Quepos

Rainforest-backed beaches next to Manuel Antonio National Park. Wetter and greener than Guanacaste.

Caribbean

Puerto Viejo / Cahuita

The southern Caribbean coast, with Afro-Caribbean culture and a more remote feel. Check road access and distance to hospitals carefully.

How much income do you need to retire in Costa Rica?

The pensionado residency requires a permanent pension of at least US$1,000 a month. Without a pension, the rentista route requires stable income of at least US$2,500 a month for at least two years. On top of that you must pay the Caja (CCSS) health contribution, calculated on an income base the CCSS sets (at least your visa's income threshold). Numbeo estimates a single person's costs in San José at about $1,000 a month excluding rent, with a 1-bedroom outside the centre at about $760.

How does the Costa Rica pensionado visa work?

It is a temporary residence granted for up to two years and renewable, for people with a permanent pension of at least US$1,000 a month. Holders must stay insured with the CCSS, which is checked at renewal. After three consecutive years of temporary residence you can apply for permanent residence.

How long until a US retiree can become a Costa Rican citizen?

Naturalisation by residence requires seven years of official residence for most nationalities, including US citizens. Central Americans, Spaniards and Ibero-Americans by birth need five years. The Supreme Electoral Tribunal (TSE) handles applications and requires a residence card (DIMEX), not just a passport.
