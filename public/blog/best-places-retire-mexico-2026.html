Best Places to Retire in Mexico 2026: 8 Cities Ranked

Updated October 20, 2026 · 13 min read · By Charles Brossy

Proximity to the US, a lower cost of living, a warm climate, and long-established expat communities make Mexico a common first choice for North American retirees exploring a move abroad.

But "retire in Mexico" covers a lot of ground. San Miguel de Allende and Cancún are both in Mexico, and they're about as similar as Paris and Ibiza. This guide ranks the top 8 retirement destinations on the metrics that actually matter. Rents, visa thresholds, IMSS fees, exchange rates and the travel advisory were rechecked against official and published sources on October 20, 2026; each is listed in the Sources section.

How we ranked these cities: Cost of living (couple), healthcare quality and access, safety record, English-language availability, expat community size, climate, and proximity to US airports. Each city is assessed for who it's actually best for — there's no single winner.

San Miguel is Mexico's most celebrated expat city for a reason: it's genuinely beautiful, entirely walkable, has a massive English-speaking community, world-class restaurants, good private hospitals, and a year-round spring climate at altitude. It's not the cheapest option, but it delivers a lifestyle that's hard to replicate anywhere in the world at this price.

The large expat community means you'll never lack for English-speaking social infrastructure — book clubs, art classes, volunteer organizations, golf, language exchanges. The flip side is that San Miguel has become expensive by Mexican standards precisely because so many foreigners have moved there.

Lake Chapala is home to one of Mexico's largest and longest-established communities of North American retirees, spread along the Ajijic–Chapala–Jocotepec corridor. At about 5,100 ft, it has mild temperatures year-round. Numbeo has too few recent entries for Ajijic to compare rents reliably, so ask local agents for current prices.

The downside: the lakeside communities are aging infrastructure-wise, the lake itself has significant pollution issues, and the area is extremely car-dependent. Healthcare requires driving to Guadalajara (45 min) for anything serious.

Puerto Vallarta is the original Mexican beach retirement town — established enough to have good private hospitals (including CMQ), strong English infrastructure, and direct US flights on multiple airlines. The Banderas Bay setting is genuinely stunning. The LGBTQ+ community is particularly large and well-integrated.

Costs have risen with popularity — Zona Romántica and Versalles are no longer cheap, and Puerto Vallarta has the highest average city-centre one-bedroom rent of the cities in our table (MXN 23,800 on Numbeo as of October 2026). The rainy season (June–October) brings daily downpours and high humidity. But for beach-first retirees, PV remains the gold standard.

Mérida, capital of Yucatán state, has the lowest average city-centre one-bedroom rent of the cities on this list with enough Numbeo data (about MXN 11,500 a month as of October 2026). Its colonial centro is stunning, the food scene (Yucatecan cuisine is arguably Mexico's best regional cooking) is world class, and it's the closest major Mexican city to the US East Coast. The Yucatán Peninsula's cenotes, beaches (1 hour away), and Mayan ruins are incredible day-trip territory.

The main challenge: Mérida is genuinely hot. June–September temperatures regularly exceed 95°F with high humidity. AC is non-negotiable and adds to utility bills. But it's the best value-for-quality city on this list for retirees who can handle the heat.

Mexico Retirement Visa: What You Need to Qualify

Mexico offers two residency tracks for retirees. Neither requires a job offer — they're purely income and asset based.

Visa TypeIncome Requirement (Washington consulate, 2026)DurationPath to Permanent
Residente TemporalMXN 83,640/mo pension or salary for the last 6 months (~$4,540), or MXN 1,394,000 minimum balance over 12 months (~$75,660)Up to 4 yearsApply for permanent after 4 years
Residente Permanente (retiree)MXN 139,400/mo pension for the last 6 months (~$7,570), or MXN 5,576,000 average balance over 12 months (~$302,630)IndefiniteRetirees with a pension can apply directly — no renewal

These are the amounts on the 2026 checklists of the Mexican Embassy in Washington, D.C., as of October 2026, converted at the Banxico FIX rate of MXN 18.4253 per dollar (October 19, 2026). Thresholds are set as multiples of Mexico's minimum wage or the UMA, and each consulate sets and updates its own figures: Washington's amounts equal 300, 5,000, 500 and 20,000 days of the 2025 general minimum wage (MXN 278.80), while the 2026 minimum wage is MXN 315.04 a day. The New York consulate's 2026 checklist, for example, asks temporary applicants for $4,200 a month or a $71,500 balance. Check the checklist of the consulate where you will apply. Washington's permanent-residence route for retirees requires proof of official retirement or age 62 or over.

Pension income counts: Washington accepts pension deposits for both visas, and each applicant files their own application; a spouse can instead apply through the family-unity route that the New York checklist describes. A US couple whose combined Social Security is $3,000–4,000 a month would fall short of Washington's temporary-residence income test for a single applicant (about $4,540), so check the savings route or a family-unity application. You apply at a Mexican consulate (fee $56 at both Washington and New York; Washington says visas are normally ready the same day, New York estimates 10 business days), then have 30 calendar days after entering Mexico to request your residence card at an INM office.

Healthcare for Retirees in Mexico

Private healthcare in Mexico is excellent in major cities and remarkably affordable. IMSS's voluntary health insurance (Seguro de Salud para la Familia) is open to people in Mexico without other social security. For enrollments or renewals from March 1, 2026, the annual fee per person is MXN 20,600 at ages 60–69, MXN 21,500 at 70–79 and MXN 22,150 at 80 and over (about $1,120–1,200 at the October 2026 exchange rate). Quality and wait times vary by facility. Most retirees combine IMSS enrollment with a private insurance policy for specialist access and private hospitals.

What is the best place to retire in Mexico in 2026?

For most US and Canadian retirees, the top overall choice is San Miguel de Allende (walkable, large English-speaking community, excellent climate, good hospitals). For community and climate, Lake Chapala/Ajijic — a large, long-established North American community and mild weather year-round. For beach retirement, Puerto Vallarta. For low rents and proximity to the US East Coast, Mérida.

How much money do you need to retire in Mexico?

There is no official figure. As of October 2026, Numbeo's crowd-sourced estimates put a single person's monthly costs, excluding rent, at about $714 in Mérida, $766 in Mexico City, $773 in Puerto Vallarta and $775 in San Miguel de Allende. A one-bedroom apartment in the city centre averages about MXN 11,500 a month in Mérida and MXN 23,800 in Puerto Vallarta. Add health insurance and travel home. For residency, the consulate's income test is often the binding number: the Mexican Embassy in Washington asks for at least MXN 83,640 a month in pension or salary for temporary residence in 2026.

What visa do I need to retire in Mexico?

Most retirees apply for a Temporary Resident (Residente Temporal) or, if retired with a pension, a Permanent Resident (Residente Permanente) visa at a Mexican consulate. Each consulate sets its own amounts. As of October 2026, the Mexican Embassy in Washington, D.C. asks temporary applicants for MXN 83,640 a month in pension or salary over the last six months, or a minimum balance of MXN 1,394,000 over 12 months; retirees applying for permanent residence need MXN 139,400 a month in pension, or MXN 5,576,000 in savings. The New York consulate instead lists $4,200 a month or a $71,500 balance for temporary residence. After four years as a temporary resident you can apply for permanent residence. Switching from tourist status to residency inside Mexico is only allowed in specific cases, such as family ties to a Mexican citizen or resident. Our quick-read on the Mexico temporary residency income requirement explains the official UMA-based rule and why consulates quote different amounts.

Is Mexico safe for retirees in 2026?

Safety varies by state and even within a state. The US State Department's Mexico Travel Advisory, last reissued on May 29, 2026, rates Mexico overall at Level 2 (Exercise Increased Caution) due to terrorism, crime, and kidnapping, and says US government employees may not travel to certain high-risk areas. Check the advisory and its maps of restricted areas for the exact state and city you are considering: Guanajuato for San Miguel de Allende, Jalisco for Lake Chapala and Puerto Vallarta, Yucatán for Mérida.
