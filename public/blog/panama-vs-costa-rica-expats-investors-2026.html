Panama vs Costa Rica for Expats & Investors (2026)

By Charles Brossy  ·  April 29, 2026 · Updated October 21, 2026  ·  10 min read

Panama vs Costa Rica for expats and investors comes down to a trade-off. Panama has a dollarized economy, lower day-to-day costs excluding rent, lower average asking prices per square meter and a retiree visa that grants permanent residence from the start. Costa Rica has cheaper city-centre rents in San José, slightly higher average gross rental yields and a lower annual property tax. Both carry a US State Department Level 2 advisory. Every figure below is sourced and dated; the full list is at the end.

In both countries foreigners can buy titled property in their own name, with two exceptions worth knowing. In Costa Rica, the first 200 meters inland from the high-tide line belong to the state: the first 50 meters are public and cannot be occupied, and the remaining 150 meters can be held only by municipal concession, not ownership. In Panama, foreign nationals and foreign-owned companies cannot own property within 10 kilometers of the national borders. Panama's economy runs on the US dollar. Costa Rica's currency is the colón, although property listings are quoted in dollars, and taxes and notary fees are colón costs.

Cost of Living in Detail

Numbeo's crowd-sourced figures (both pages updated October 20, 2026) show Panama City cheaper for everyday spending but more expensive for rent. Estimated monthly costs excluding rent are about $856 for one person and $3,117 for a family of four in Panama City. In San José they are about $1,005 and $3,742. That makes Panama City roughly 15% to 17% cheaper before housing.

Rent reverses the picture. A one-bedroom apartment in the city centre averages about $1,305 a month in Panama City against about $944 in San José. A three-bedroom in the centre averages about $2,179 and $1,936. Add a central one-bedroom to a single person's non-rent costs and the totals are close: roughly $2,160 in Panama City and $1,950 in San José.

Selected Numbeo averages, October 2026:

  • Basic utilities for a 915 sq ft apartment: Panama City $131, San José $95
  • Broadband internet (60 Mbps or more): Panama City $49, San José $60
  • Meal at an inexpensive restaurant: Panama City $11.50, San José $12.24
  • Three-course dinner for two, mid-range restaurant: Panama City $47.50, San José $61.67

These are crowd-sourced averages for the capital cities only. Beach towns such as Tamarindo, Nosara or Bocas del Toro can differ a lot, and we have not found reliable current data for them.

Real Estate: Prices and Yields

Prices. Global Property Guide cites an average asking price of about $1,881 per m² in Panama City in September 2025, up 6.81% year on year (RIAL survey data from Di Tella University and Zonaprop). For San José apartments it shows about $2,900 per m² in March 2026 (Encuentra24 data). Guanacaste apartments averaged about $2,990 per m² in July 2025. The two countries' figures come from different data series, and a separate apartment-only series on the same Global Property Guide page puts Panama City nearer $2,745 per m², so the gap between apartment prices is narrower than the headline numbers suggest.

Panama yields. Global Property Guide puts Panama's average gross rental yield at 6.94% in Q2 2026, down from 7.03% in Q4 2025. Its Panama City table shows how much this varies by neighborhood. In El Cangrejo, a studio or one-bedroom has a median price of about $170,000 and rent of about $1,200 a month, an 8.47% gross yield. In the East Coast area the figures are about $310,000, $2,100 and 8.13%. In Bella Vista the studio and one-bedroom yield is 9.53%.

Costa Rica yields. The country average is 7.63% in Q2 2026, down from 7.80% in Q4 2025. In the Central Valley areas foreign buyers favour, the averages are 8.05% in San José, 7.86% in Santa Ana and 6.29% in Escazú.

These are gross yields, before tax, maintenance, management fees and vacancy. Global Property Guide notes that net yields are typically 1.5 to 2 percentage points lower. We have not found a reliable, current source for short-term rental yields in Casco Viejo or Guanacaste, so we do not quote one.

Taxes for Property Owners

Costa Rica charges an annual municipal property tax of 0.25% of the appraised value. Homes whose construction value is above ¢143 million in 2026 also pay the solidarity housing tax, at 0.25% to 0.55% by band. Sellers pay 15% on capital gains, with an option of 2.25% of the price on a first sale under certain conditions. A 1.5% transfer tax applies to the higher of the sale price or the registered value; who pays it is negotiable.

Panama property tax depends on how the property is used, under Law 66 of 2017. A registered primary residence pays 0% on the first $120,000 of cadastral value, 0.5% on value up to $700,000 and 0.7% above that. Other residences, including rentals, pay 0% on the first $30,000, then 0.6%, 0.8% and 1% in bands. On a sale, the gain is taxed at 10%. A 3% advance is withheld on the higher of the price or the cadastral value (the seller may treat it as the final tax), plus a 2% transfer tax that does not apply to new construction. So an investor's rental condo in Panama can pay more annual tax than the same unit in Costa Rica, even though Panama taxes sale gains less.

Visa and Residency

Both countries set the Pensionado (retiree) income floor at $1,000 a month, but the status differs. Panama's permit requires a lifetime pension of at least $1,000 a month and grants permanent residence. Panamanian law also gives retirees, including foreign residents, discounts such as 25% at restaurants, 50% on cinema and theatre tickets, 50% on hotel stays from Monday to Thursday, 20% on medical consultations and 25% on electricity up to a set usage limit. Costa Rica's Pensionado is a temporary residence category under Law 8764, with the same $1,000 floor. You can apply for permanent residence after three consecutive years of temporary residence. Temporary residents must stay insured with the CCSS public health system to renew.

For non-retirees, Panama's Qualified Investor program now requires $300,000 in first-sale property or $500,000 in resale property, under Executive Decree 17 of September 8, 2026. There is also a $500,000 five-year deposit option at a state-owned bank. Panama's Friendly Nations route has required a real economic tie since 2021; Executive Decree 226 of 2021 added a three-year fixed deposit of at least $200,000 as one option. Check the other qualifying routes with Panama's migration service. Costa Rica's Rentista category requires stable income of at least $2,500 a month. Its Inversionista category was set at $150,000 by Law 9996. The law's five-year window for its extra incentives ended on July 14, 2026. Lawyers such as AG Legal say the $150,000 threshold still applies, while other commentators say it may revert to the general $200,000 amount. Confirm with Costa Rica's immigration directorate (DGME) before you rely on either figure.

Healthcare

Both capitals have large private hospitals. In Panama City, Pacífica Salud's Hospital Punta Pacífica is a consulting client of Johns Hopkins Medicine International. In Costa Rica, legal residents are part of the CCSS (Caja) public system: insurance with the CCSS is a condition for renewing residence, so budget for it alongside any private cover. We have not found reliable current price comparisons for specialist visits or surgery, so we do not quote any.

Climate

The two capitals also differ in setting. Panama City is a lowland tropical city on the Pacific side. Its rainfall is seasonal, with a clearer dry period early in the year than on the Caribbean coast. San José and its suburbs (Escazú, Santa Ana) sit in Costa Rica's Central Valley, a highland valley that holds most of the country's large cities, so they sit at higher elevation than Panama City. If a highland setting near a capital city matters to you, Costa Rica's Central Valley has the edge. If you plan to live in air-conditioned high-rises and work in the city, the climate matters less.

Safety and Travel Advisories

As of October 21, 2026, the US State Department rates both countries Level 2: Exercise Increased Caution, citing crime. Costa Rica's advisory was issued on April 2, 2026. Panama's was issued on August 19, 2026, and keeps Level 4 “Do Not Travel” warnings for the Mosquito Gulf and parts of the Darién region on the Colombian border.

The Honest Verdict

🇵🇦 Choose Panama if…

You want to hold dollars with no exchange-rate risk

You want permanent residence from day one as a retiree

You value the Law 6 retiree discounts

You want a lower average entry price per square meter

You expect a large gain on resale (10% tax on the gain against Costa Rica's 15%)

You'll be in a city most of the time

🇨🇷 Choose Costa Rica if…

A highland setting near the capital matters most

You want cheaper city-centre rent while you test the country

You want slightly higher average gross yields

You want a lower annual tax on a rental property

You are happy to join the CCSS public health system

You qualify for the lower Inversionista threshold

One option is to combine the two: rent first in the country you plan to live in, and buy where the numbers work best. Whichever you choose, check current tax and residency rules with a local lawyer before you sign, because Panama changed its investor rules in 2026 and Costa Rica's Law 9996 incentive window closed.

FAQ: Panama vs Costa Rica

Is it cheaper to live in Panama or Costa Rica?

It depends on rent. Numbeo's October 2026 data puts a single person's monthly costs excluding rent at about $856 in Panama City and $1,005 in San José. But a central one-bedroom rents for about $1,305 in Panama City and $944 in San José, so the totals end up close.

Which retirement visa is better, Panama or Costa Rica?

Both require a pension of at least $1,000 a month. Panama's Pensionado requires a lifetime pension, grants permanent residence and comes with legal discounts of 15–50% under Law 6 of 1987. Costa Rica's is a temporary residence. You can apply for permanent residence after three years, and you must stay insured with the CCSS public health system.

Are property taxes lower in Panama or Costa Rica?

For a rental property, usually Costa Rica: 0.25% a year of appraised value, against Panama's 0.6–1% on value above $30,000. A registered primary home in Panama pays nothing on the first $120,000 of value. On a sale, Panama taxes the gain at 10% against 15% in Costa Rica, but its 2% transfer tax is higher than Costa Rica's 1.5%.
