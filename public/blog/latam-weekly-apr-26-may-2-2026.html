Weekly Roundup

Latin America Real Estate Weekly — April 26 – May 2, 2026

By Charles Brossy April 26, 2026 · Updated October 30, 2026 5 min read

Five markets from the week of April 26 – May 2, 2026, re-checked against official and published data on October 30, 2026. Mexico’s beach markets led the country’s house-price index, Colombia’s renters outnumber owners while new-home sales fall, São Paulo prices are rising slowly, Costa Rica’s much-quoted “36% correction” turns out to be 2024 broker data, and Uruguay’s new tax-holiday rules took effect.

Mexico: Beach Markets Lead the SHF Price Index

Mexico’s official house price index from the Sociedad Hipotecaria Federal (SHF) rose 8.74% year on year nationally in Q1 2026. Quintana Roo was the fastest-rising state at 13.42%, and Yucatán rose 10.65%. Among municipalities, Solidaridad (Playa del Carmen) led at 13.55%, Benito Juárez (Cancún) was second at 13.31%, and Mérida rose 10.41%. Growth then slowed: in Q2 2026 the national index rose 7.31%, Quintana Roo 9.80%, Solidaridad 9.96%, Benito Juárez 9.69%, Yucatán 8.69% and Mérida 8.49%.

Mortgage credit stays expensive: Banxico’s average rate on fixed-rate home loans was 11.45% in January 2026, and Global Property Guide’s Banxico-based series shows 11.33% in June 2026. Corrected October 30, 2026: an earlier version gave Q1 figures that do not appear in SHF’s Q1 2026 release.

Colombia: Renters Outnumber Owners, but New-Home Sales Are Falling

BBVA Research estimated in April 2025 that renting households (7.3 million) had overtaken owner-occupiers (7.1 million); its 2026 update puts them at 7.7 million against 7.2 million. DANE’s Quality of Life survey, as reported in June 2026, shows renting as the most common tenure for the fourth year running, at 40.8% of households against 38.1% who own. That supports landlords’ demand.

New-home sales point the other way. BBVA Research had forecast growth of 11.5% in 2026, but Camacol reports 78,423 new homes sold in the first half of 2026, down 9.9% on a year earlier. Mortgage rates remain high: in early October 2026 Superfinanciera data showed peso rates of about 12.4–16.4% a year for non-subsidised (No VIS) loans and 11.4–15.4% for subsidised (VIS) ones. DANE’s new-housing price index rose 8.47% year on year in Q1 2026. Corrected October 30, 2026: an earlier version said renters overtook owners for the first time in 2025 and quoted an unsourced median price.

Brazil: São Paulo Prices Edge Up; Urban Property Is Open to Foreigners

São Paulo’s average asking price on the FipeZAP index was R$12,019 per m² in April 2026 and R$12,206 in September 2026, up 3.64% in 12 months; the upmarket Jardins district averaged about R$18,028 per m² in September. Nationally, FipeZAP asking prices rose 5.51% in the 12 months to September 2026. Brazil remains Latin America’s largest economy on IMF estimates.

Foreigners can buy urban property. Rural land is restricted under Law 5.709 of 1971, and rural property within the 150 km border strip needs prior federal consent under Law 6.634 of 1979. Corrected October 30, 2026: an earlier version claimed a “30–50% currency discount”, a 5–9% annual growth outlook and neighbourhood price ranges that we could not source.

Costa Rica: What the “36% Correction” Really Measured

The widely repeated 36% fall in Guanacaste prices comes from Coldwell Banker Costa Rica’s update on August 2024 data: the average sale price of single-family homes in Guanacaste and the Nicoya Peninsula fell 36% to $967,506, with homes taking about 418 days to sell. It was a change in the average price of the homes that sold, in one month’s report, not a fall from a 2024 peak, and the same report showed Central Valley single-family prices up 17%. There is no official Costa Rican house price index; Global Property Guide’s series based on Encuentra24 asking prices showed San José up 12.48% year on year in June 2026.

Henley & Partners’ Private Wealth Migration Report 2025 estimated a net inflow of 350 millionaires into Costa Rica in 2025, holding about US$2.8 billion. Corrected October 30, 2026: an earlier version presented 2024 broker data as a current correction and quoted unsourced discounts and decade-long appreciation forecasts.

Uruguay: The Tax Holiday Changed on January 1, 2026

From January 1, 2026, Law 20.446 of December 16, 2025 changed Uruguay’s tax holiday for new residents. People who become tax residents from that date, and were not tax resident in the two previous years, can opt to pay the non-resident tax (IRNR) instead of IRPF on foreign dividends, interest and related capital gains for the year they become resident plus the next ten, if they spend more than 183 days a year in Uruguay, invest more than UI 12.5 million (about US$2.07 million in October 2026) in real estate, or put at least UI 625,000 a year into qualifying funds. Foreign buyers hold property with the same rights as Uruguayans.

The holiday covers foreign investment income only. Rent from a Uruguayan property is taxed at 12%, and so is the gain when you sell it. The full rules, including the options after the eleventh year, are in our guide to Uruguay’s tax holiday, and buyer costs are in our closing-costs guide. Corrected October 28, 2026: an earlier version of this section said foreign gains and rent were untaxed indefinitely and quoted unsourced appreciation, closing-cost and threshold figures.

The Bigger Picture

The official data tell a more mixed story than the headlines. Mexico’s beach markets are still rising fastest but slowing; Colombia has strong rental demand but falling new-home sales and high mortgage rates; São Paulo prices are rising a little more slowly than inflation; Costa Rica has no official price index, so treat any single broker statistic with care; and Uruguay’s new tax holiday is open to people who were not tax resident in the previous two years and meet a residence or investment test. In every market, check the local rules on foreign ownership, rental tax and short-term letting before you buy.

Frequently Asked Questions

How fast are house prices rising in Mexico's beach markets?

On the SHF house price index, prices rose 13.42% a year in Quintana Roo and 13.55% in Solidaridad (Playa del Carmen) in Q1 2026. Growth slowed in Q2 2026 to 9.80% in Quintana Roo and 7.31% nationally.

Can foreigners buy property in Brazil?

Yes, urban property is open to foreigners. Rural land is restricted under Law 5.709 of 1971, and rural property in the 150 km border strip needs prior consent under Law 6.634 of 1979.

What changed in Uruguay's tax holiday in 2026?

Under Law 20.446, people who become tax resident from January 1, 2026 and were not resident in the two prior years can opt to be taxed under the non-resident tax (IRNR) instead of IRPF on foreign dividends, interest and related gains for the year of arrival plus ten more years, if they also meet a 183-day, UI 12.5 million real-estate or UI 625,000-a-year fund test.
