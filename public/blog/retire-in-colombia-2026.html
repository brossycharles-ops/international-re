Retire in Colombia 2026: Visa, Cost of Living & Best Cities

April 29, 2026 · Updated October 16, 2026 · 12 min read · By Charles Brossy

Retiring in Colombia is possible on a foreign pension, but the Pensionado visa is not cheap to qualify for. The income rule is three times Colombia's monthly minimum wage: COP 5,252,715 a month in 2026, about US$1,650 at October 2026 exchange rates. That is higher than the US$1,000 pension thresholds in Panama and Costa Rica. Medellín is still cheaper to live in than Panama City: about 10% cheaper including rent, by Numbeo's October 2026 data. You also need health insurance: the visa does not let you join Colombia's social security system.

This guide covers the visa rules, fees and timeline from Colombia's Foreign Ministry (Cancillería), what a single retiree spends in Medellín, the health-insurance rule, and the safety picture. Updated October 16, 2026.

Colombia vs Panama and Costa Rica in one line: Colombia asks for more pension income (about US$1,650/month vs US$1,000), but day-to-day costs in Medellín are lower than in Panama City. Because the threshold is set in pesos, its dollar value rises and falls with the exchange rate.

The Colombia Pensionado Visa: What You Need

The Pensionado visa is a migrant (M) visa for foreigners who receive a lifetime pension. It does not allow you to work in Colombia.

Does US Social Security count? Foreign government pensions qualify, so US Social Security retirement benefits fit the rule. Cancillería asks for a pension certificate from the paying body, apostilled and translated into Spanish. Check the amount, though: a US$1,500 monthly benefit falls below the 2026 threshold of about US$1,650.

Step-by-Step Application Process

  1. Gather documents: passport; a pension certificate stating the monthly amount; a police or criminal-record certificate from the countries where you lived in the last 3 years; a medical certificate; and proof of health insurance for your stay.

  2. Apostille and translate: foreign documents need an apostille and a Spanish translation. In the US, federal documents (an SSA benefit letter, an FBI background check) are apostilled by the US Department of State; state documents by the state.

  3. Apply online: the process is fully virtual. The visa office can ask for physical documents or an interview.

  4. Pay the fees: the US$56 study fee when you apply; the US$279 visa fee only if the visa is approved.

  5. Receive the e-visa: the official term is up to 30 calendar days, longer if extra documents or an interview are requested.

  6. Register with Migración Colombia: within 15 calendar days of arrival (or of the visa being issued, if you are already in Colombia), register to get your Cédula de Extranjería (foreign ID card).

Best Cities for Retirees in Colombia

Spring Climate

Medellín

About $1,460–1,880/mo + insurance*

Averages around 72°F (22°C) year-round. Large expat community in El Poblado and Laureles, a metro system, and major private hospitals such as Clínica Las Américas and Clínica El Rosario. The most popular choice for foreign retirees.

Caribbean Beach

Cartagena

Tourist city

UNESCO-listed walled city on the Caribbean, hot year-round. Best for retirees who put beach access and colonial architecture ahead of cost; check local rents, which carry a tourism premium in the historic centre.

Beach + Nature

Santa Marta

Smaller market

Where the Sierra Nevada meets the Caribbean, near Tayrona National Park. A smaller expat community and less developed infrastructure than Cartagena.

Capital City

Bogotá

Big-city costs

Nearly 8 million people at about 2,640 m (8,660 ft), so the climate is cool. Home to Fundación Santa Fe de Bogotá, one of Colombia's top-ranked hospitals. Suits retirees who want a major city.

Coffee Region

Manizales / Armenia

Smaller cities

Mild mountain climate, colonial towns and coffee-farm country. Smaller expat communities; check local rents yourself, as published data for these cities is thin.

Southwest

Cali

Check the advisory

Warm climate and a famous salsa scene, with less expat infrastructure. Cali city is excluded from the US State Department's Level 4 warning for Valle del Cauca, but the rest of the department is Level 4.

*Medellín range built from Numbeo data (October 2026) for one person: rent of a 1-bedroom outside or in the centre, Numbeo's living costs without rent, before health insurance. See below.

What Does It Actually Cost? A Medellín Budget

Numbeo's Medellín data (updated October 15, 2026) puts a single person's monthly costs without rent at about US$832. A 1-bedroom apartment rents for about US$627 outside the centre and US$1,046 in the centre. That gives roughly US$1,460–1,880 a month before health insurance, which the visa requires and which you must price for your age.

Healthcare for Retirees in Colombia

Health insurance is a visa condition. Cancillería requires Pensionado applicants to show health coverage in Colombia, or a policy covering accident, illness, hospitalisation, death and repatriation for the whole stay. It also states that the Pensionado visa does not allow you to join Colombia's social security system (which includes the public EPS health system), unless a bilateral or multilateral agreement provides otherwise.

  • In practice: plan on private cover, such as an international health policy or a Colombian private plan that meets the visa's coverage list. Premiums depend heavily on age and pre-existing conditions, so get quotes before you apply.
  • Check treaties: if your country has a social security agreement with Colombia, ask whether it changes the rule for you.
  • Hospitals: Medellín and Bogotá have large private hospitals, including Clínica Las Américas and Clínica El Rosario in Medellín and Fundación Santa Fe de Bogotá.

Don't build your budget on cheap public-system figures. Online guides often quote low EPS contributions for retirees, but the Pensionado visa itself does not give access to the social security system. Add the cost of a private policy to the Numbeo figures above.

Is Colombia Safe for Retirees?

The US State Department rates Colombia Level 3: Reconsider Travel (updated March 31, 2026), citing crime, terrorism, civil unrest and kidnapping. It rates some areas Level 4: Do Not Travel: Arauca, Norte de Santander, Cauca (except Popayán), Valle del Cauca (except Cali) and the area within 10 km of the Venezuelan border. The main retiree cities are outside the Level 4 areas, but the advisory applies to them too. Use registered ride apps rather than street taxis at night, avoid showing valuables, and read the current advisory before you commit.

How much income do you need for Colombia's retirement visa?

The Pensionado visa requires a lifetime pension of at least three times Colombia's monthly minimum wage. In 2026 that is COP 5,252,715 a month, about US$1,650 at October 2026 exchange rates. The dollar figure moves with the peso.

How much does the Colombia Pensionado visa cost and how long does it take?

Cancillería charges a US$56 study fee when you apply and a US$279 visa fee if approved, US$335 in total. The application is online, and the official decision time is up to 30 calendar days after the study fee is paid.

How much does it cost to retire in Medellín?

Using Numbeo data from October 2026, a single person spends roughly US$1,460 a month with a 1-bedroom outside the centre and about US$1,880 in the centre, before health insurance. The Pensionado visa requires private-style health cover, because it does not give access to Colombia's social security system.

Is Colombia safe for retirees in 2026?

The US State Department rates Colombia Level 3: Reconsider Travel (March 31, 2026), with Level 4 for Arauca, Norte de Santander, most of Cauca, Valle del Cauca outside Cali, and the Venezuelan border area. The main retiree cities are outside the Level 4 areas, but normal big-city precautions apply.
