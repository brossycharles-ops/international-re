Latin America Real Estate — October 2026 Market Update

By Charles Brossy October 14, 2026 6 min read

Our last multi-market snapshot ran in June. Since then the summer data has landed, and the picture has shifted: rents are still climbing, but prices have risen fast enough that gross yields are compressing; borrowing has become more expensive in several markets; and buyers paying cash have more leverage than they did a year ago. Here's what changed, market by market, with every figure sourced at the end.

Costa Rica — Busier, but the Buyer Now Has Leverage

Global Property Guide's July 2026 survey put new-build and prime apartments in the San José metro area at USD 2,902 per square meter — second in the region only to Mexico City. That ranking says more about foreign demand than local incomes, and the market is now clearly two-speed: coastal Guanacaste and the Nicoya Peninsula are carrying unsold high-end inventory, while the Central Valley keeps gaining relocating buyers who prioritise healthcare and airport access.

The quiet story for dollar buyers is the currency. The colón traded around CRC 450 per dollar in August, against roughly 506 in June 2025 and a peak near 685 in mid-2022 — a dollar buys about a third fewer colones than four years ago, which raises the local-currency cost of construction, closing fees and maintenance even where dollar asking prices are flat. Average gross yields slipped to 7.63% in Q2 2026 (from 7.84% a year earlier), with Heredia still the strongest submarket at 8.69%. Supply is coming: construction registrations in the first half of 2026 ran 21% above the same period of 2025. See our San José city guide for neighborhood detail.

Panama — Rents Up Double Digits, Mortgages Still Down

Panama City apartment asking rents reached about $14.70 per square meter in June 2026, up 13.5% year on year, according to Encuentra24 listing data analysed by Global Property Guide. Prices have moved too — Ancón asking prices were up 18.5% and Bella Vista 12.0%, while Casco Viejo fell 5.1% — so the Panama City gross yield actually eased to 7.57% in April 2026 from 7.83% a year earlier.

The split is between cash and credit. New mortgage lending was down 19.9% in January–May 2026, yet developer condominium inventory hit a reported nine-year low and resale listings fell sharply in Costa del Este, El Cangrejo and Santa María. Add GDP growth of 4.8% in Q1 and a 17.4% jump in first-half visitor arrivals, and the upper end of Panama City looks like a genuine recovery — the mortgage-dependent lower end does not, yet. Colombia — Higher Rates and a New Government

Colombia is the market where the macro backdrop changed most. Banco de la República raised its policy rate from 9.25% at the start of the year to 12% by the end of June 2026, and weighted mortgage rates for non-subsidised homes reached about 15.2%. Abelardo de la Espriella won the June 21 presidential runoff and took office on August 7. The peso traded around COP 3,249 per dollar in mid-July.

For foreign buyers the fundamentals are intact: DANE's new-home price index rose 8.47% year on year in Q1 2026, and gross yields average 7.01% nationally — 7.71% in Bogotá and 7.25% in Medellín. Expensive local credit thins domestic competition, which tends to favour buyers bringing dollars. Start with our Medellín deep-dive.

Mexico — Rent Cap Upheld, Prices Still Climbing

Mexico's Supreme Court validated Mexico City's rent cap in February 2026, limiting annual increases on existing leases to the prior year's inflation. That matters most for long-term landlords; short-term rental conversions accelerated ahead of the World Cup. Prices haven't stalled: the Sociedad Hipotecaria Federal index rose 7.3% year on year in Q1 2026, and Banorte put Mexico City at MXN 57,921 (about USD 3,283) per square meter in January.

Gross yields average 6.06% nationally and 6.55% in Mexico City — lower than Central America, with deeper liquidity. Remember that coastal buyers need a fideicomiso while Mexico City buyers can hold title directly; see our tip on direct ownership in Mexico City.

Argentina — The Dollar Recovery Is Losing Steam

June's update described Argentina's first sustained dollar price recovery in a decade. The summer data says it is slowing. The Buenos Aires statistics office (IDECBA) put the average asking price of used two-room apartments at US$2,343 per square meter in Q2 2026, up just 1.4% year on year. Zonaprop's index showed its weakest annual gain in 28 months.

Neighborhoods are diverging: Núñez rose 10.1% and Villa Pueyrredón 11.0%, while Puerto Madero, the most expensive district at US$5,805/sqm, fell 3.4%. The 2025 mortgage boom has cooled, which removes a source of local demand. For value buyers that's not bad news — it means negotiating room in mid-priced northern barrios. Our Argentina guide for foreigners covers the buying process.

The October 2026 Takeaway

Three themes run through every market this autumn. Yields are compressing because prices have outrun rents (Costa Rica, Panama). Credit is tightening (Colombia's rate hikes, Panama's mortgage slump, Argentina's cooling loan market), which hands negotiating power to cash buyers. And currency matters again — a strong colón quietly raises Costa Rican running costs for dollar holders. If you're buying in the next two quarters, model net yield in dollars, negotiate harder than you would have a year ago, and check rent-control rules before you underwrite long-term leases. Frequently Asked Questions

Are Latin American rental yields still above 7% in late 2026?

In several markets, yes: Costa Rica averages 7.63% (Q2 2026), Panama City 7.57% (April 2026) and Colombia 7.01% (Q1 2026). Yields are drifting lower because prices have risen faster than rents.

Is it harder to get a mortgage in Latin America in 2026?

In several markets. Colombia's policy rate reached 12% by June, Panama's new mortgage lending fell 19.9% in January–May, and Argentina's 2025 mortgage boom has cooled. Cash buyers have more leverage as a result.

Which Latin American city has the highest price per square meter in 2026?

Global Property Guide's July 2026 survey of new-build and prime apartments ranked Mexico City first at USD 2,947/sqm, with the San José, Costa Rica metro area close behind at USD 2,902/sqm.
