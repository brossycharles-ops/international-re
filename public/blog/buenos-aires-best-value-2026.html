Is Buenos Aires Property Good Value in 2026?

By Charles Brossy April 10, 2026 · Updated October 18, 2026 8 min read

Is Buenos Aires property good value in 2026? On price, yes: Zonaprop's September 2026 index puts the city-wide average asking price at US$2,484 per m², about 65% below Lisbon or Madrid. But it is not the bargain-and-boom story told in early 2026. Prices rose only 1.3% in dollars over the past year, the average gross rental yield is 5.7%, and some growth forecasts quoted earlier this year could not be traced to any source. This page, first published in March 2026, has been re-checked and corrected against current data.

Buenos Aires is still one of the great cities of the Americas, and its property is cheap by European standards. Argentina's 2024–2026 reforms also removed the worst of the old currency mess. What follows is what the numbers actually show, barrio by barrio, with the risks a foreign buyer should weigh.

What Does a Square Metre Cost in Buenos Aires?

Zonaprop's index tracks asking prices for apartments listed on its portal. In September 2026 the city average was US$2,484/m², up 0.3% in the month and 1.4% so far in 2026, and still 11.3% below its historical peak. Prices vary nearly six-fold across the city:

How Does That Compare With Lisbon, Madrid and Miami?

The gap with southern Europe is wider than this page originally claimed. Idealista data for September 2026 put the median asking price in the city of Lisbon at €6,256/m², about $7,040 at the European Central Bank's reference rate of $1.1253 per euro on 16 October 2026. Madrid's idealista asking price was about €6,458/m², or roughly $7,270. The Buenos Aires average is therefore about 65% lower, and even Palermo is about half the price of Lisbon.

Miami depends on what you compare. Realtor.com's median listing price for all homes in the Miami metro area was $355 per square foot in September 2026 (about $3,820/m²), so Buenos Aires is about 35% cheaper than the metro-wide figure; prime waterfront condos in Miami cost far more.

Prices Have Recovered, but Growth Has Slowed

Dollar prices rose 5.4% in 2025, after a first rebound in 2024 that ended years of decline. In 2026 the climb has slowed to 1.4% year to date and 1.3% over twelve months. Three quarters of barrios rose over the year, led by Villa Riachuelo (+11.1%) and Saavedra (+6.8%). Earlier versions of this article cited year-on-year growth of 5.6% and then about 8%, and a "BBVA Research forecast" of 6–12% growth for 2026; we could not find any such BBVA forecast and have removed it.

A Mostly Cash Market, With Mortgages Returning

Property in Buenos Aires is priced in US dollars and most purchases are still paid in cash, but it is no longer a cash-only market. The Colegio de Escribanos (notaries' association) recorded 6,055 deeds in the city in August 2026, 4.9% fewer than a year earlier, and 1,059 of them, about 17.5%, used a mortgage. That share is lower than a year ago: January–August 2026 mortgages were about a third below the same months of 2025. In the first quarter of 2026, deeds totalled 12,580, and only March crossed 5,500.

For a foreign buyer, cash means no credit checks and fewer conditions, and the deed is signed before a notary (escribano), who checks title and liens. Argentine banks lend mainly in inflation-indexed pesos to local earners, so most foreigners should assume they will pay cash.

Capital Controls: Mostly Gone for Individuals

In April 2025 Argentina lifted most currency controls (the cepo) for individuals, who can again buy dollars on the official market. The gap between the official and informal "blue" dollar rates has since fallen to around 1%. Controls are not entirely gone, though. A 90-day "cross restriction" introduced in September 2025 limited individuals who buy official dollars from also trading in the financial-dollar markets for 90 days, and companies can still only remit dividends from fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025. Check the current rules with your bank or notary before you wire money in or plan to take sale proceeds out.

Rental Yields in USD

Zonaprop's September 2026 index puts the city-wide gross yield at 5.70%, the equivalent of 17.5 years of rent to recover the purchase price. Yields run inversely to price: Villa Lugano (9.6%), Parque Patricios (7.7%) and Nueva Pompeya (7.6%) lead, while Palermo and Núñez (4.6%) and Puerto Madero (3.2%) trail. Global Property Guide's Q2 2026 survey puts Argentina's average at 5.46%, though its Buenos Aires sample covers the province, not the city.

Net yields are lower once you pay building fees (expensas), the ABL city property tax, vacancies and management. A previous version of this page quoted Global Property Guide at 7.30% and 8–9% yields in Villa Crespo, Almagro and Villa Urquiza; neither figure matches current data, and none of those barrios is among the highest-yielding.

Where Value Hunters Look

  • Villa Ortúzar and Chacarita: next to Palermo at about $2,600–$2,850/m², roughly 18–24% below Palermo's average.
  • Parque Patricios: home to the city's Distrito Tecnológico, created by city Ley 2.972, with prices around $1,740/m² and a 7.7% gross yield. Higher yields in the south come with lower resale liquidity and more variable streets.
  • Palermo, Belgrano and Recoleta: the most liquid and most popular with foreign renters, but with the lowest yields.

How to Buy as a Foreigner

Foreigners can buy urban property in Argentina on the same terms as citizens. Since 2 March 2026, individuals register for a CUIT tax ID, which replaced the old CDI under ARCA General Resolution 5803/2025. You'll need:

  • A CUIT (Argentine tax ID)
  • A local escribano (notary) to handle the transaction
  • The purchase price in US dollars
  • Budget about 5–8% for buyer closing costs in Buenos Aires City (notary, stamp duty, agent), per brokers quoted by Ámbito; most foreign investors should expect the upper end, because the stamp-duty exemption for a buyer's sole family home rarely applies to them. The federal 1.5% transfer tax (ITI) paid by sellers was repealed in 2024 (Ley 27.743)

There is no minimum investment and no residency requirement to buy a city apartment. Rural land is different: in October 2026 the Supreme Court provisionally restored the foreign-ownership limits of the Rural Land Law (Ley 26.737) while a court case continues. Non-residents also owe Argentina's personal assets tax (Bienes Personales) at 0.5% a year, paid through a local representative, Property sales by Argentine-resident individuals from 2026 are exempt from the former 15% capital-gains tax; non-residents should confirm their own treatment with a local adviser.

If you're comparing across Latin America, our deep-dives on Mendoza wine country estates and Santiago vs. the Chilean Lake District show how Buenos Aires stacks up against other Southern Cone opportunities. For buyers also evaluating Central America, the foreign ownership rules in Costa Rica offer a useful legal contrast.

The Risks

  • Policy risk. Argentina has reversed liberal reforms before; DNU 70/2023, which freed the rental market, has faced repeal attempts in Congress, most recently a failed session on 15 October 2026.
  • Slow appreciation. With prices up about 1% in a year, returns depend mainly on rent, not on a quick resale gain.
  • Liquidity. With only about one deed in six using a mortgage, resale depends on a mostly cash buyer pool.
  • Safety. The US State Department rates Argentina Level 1, "Exercise normal precautions", the lowest level.

The Bottom Line

Buenos Aires in 2026 offers big-city property at about a third of southern European prices, a 5.7% average gross yield in dollars and far fewer currency hurdles than before 2025. It is good value for a patient, cash buyer who wants rental income. It is not, on current data, a market where prices are rising fast: the case for buying rests on price and yield, not on a boom.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an apartment cost per square metre in Buenos Aires in 2026?

Zonaprop's September 2026 index puts the city-wide average asking price at US$2,484/m², ranging from about $1,071 in Villa Lugano to about $6,165 in Puerto Madero. Palermo averages about $3,454/m².

What rental yield can you get in Buenos Aires?

The city-wide average gross yield was 5.70% in September 2026, according to Zonaprop. It is 4.6% in Palermo and up to 9.6% in Villa Lugano; net yields are lower after fees, taxes and vacancies.

Can foreigners get a mortgage to buy in Buenos Aires?

In practice most foreigners pay cash. About 17.5% of Buenos Aires deeds in August 2026 used a mortgage, according to the Colegio de Escribanos, and Argentine mortgages are mainly inflation-indexed peso loans for local earners.
