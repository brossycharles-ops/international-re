Argentina Real Estate for Foreigners 2026: The Complete Buying Guide

By Charles Brossy April 29, 2026 · Updated October 16, 2026 14 min read

Yes, foreigners can buy property in Argentina with the same rights as citizens. You don't need residency, a visa or a minimum investment to buy a city apartment. What has changed is the price story. Buenos Aires is no longer a deep discount: Zonaprop's September 2026 index puts the average asking price at US$2,484 per m², only 11.3% below its all-time high. The city's average gross rental yield is 5.7%.

This guide covers the legal process (including the CUIT tax ID that replaced the CDI in March 2026), real buying costs, prices and yields by neighborhood, taxes for non-residents, currency rules and the main risks. Updated October 16, 2026.

The 2026 picture in one paragraph: property is priced and paid in US dollars. The peso has lost more than 99.9% of its value against the dollar since it left the 1-to-1 peg in 2002 (about ARS 1,535 per dollar in mid-October 2026). Dollar prices fell after 2018 but have mostly recovered. Buyers today get dollar yields of roughly 4.5–6% in the main expat neighborhoods, not a bargain-basement entry price.

Can Foreigners Own Property in Argentina?

Yes. Foreigners can own apartments, houses and commercial property in their own name, with no cap on urban property and no extra tax for being foreign. Rural land is different:

  • Rural Land Law (Ley 26.737): foreign ownership of rural land is capped at 15% nationally and in each province and municipality, no single nationality may hold more than 30% of that share, and one foreign owner may hold at most 1,000 hectares in the core farming zone (or the equivalent elsewhere). Milei's DNU 70/2023 repealed the law, but on October 9, 2026 the Supreme Court provisionally suspended that repeal, so the limits apply again while the case continues.
  • Border security zones: under Decreto-Ley 15.385/44, any purchase by a foreign person or company in a designated border security zone needs prior approval from the national border-zones commission (CNZS). Approval is discretionary and can take 6–12 months. Buenos Aires City and Buenos Aires Province are exempt; Patagonian areas such as Bariloche can be affected. Ask your escribano before you sign.

What You Actually Need to Buy

  • A CUIT tax ID. Since March 2, 2026, ARCA (the tax agency formerly called AFIP) no longer issues the CDI to individuals; foreigners get a CUIT instead (RG 5803/2025). Non-residents apply through a representative with an Argentine address; those physically in Argentina at the time can also apply in person at an ARCA office. Allow time; it is not a same-day formality for most foreign buyers.
  • A valid passport. You can buy on a tourist entry.
  • US dollars. Sale prices are quoted and generally paid in dollars.
  • An escribano. Argentina's notary checks title, prepares the deed and registers it. The fee (plus 21% VAT) is part of the total buying costs below; ask for a written quote.

The Step-by-Step Buying Process

  1. Get your CUIT Apply to ARCA in person or through a local representative. You need it to sign the deed.
  2. Find a property and negotiate Prices are quoted in dollars. Zonaprop reports that listings cut in the previous six months were marked down by about 6% on average, a guide to how much room sellers give.
  3. Sign the reservation and boleto de compraventa The preliminary contract fixes the price and timeline and usually involves a deposit. Have your escribano review it before you sign.
  4. Title and lien search The escribano checks the title history, mortgages, embargoes and any unpaid building fees (expensas) or property tax.
  5. Escritura (deed) Buyer and seller sign the deed before the escribano and the balance is paid. Funds from abroad have to be documented for anti-money-laundering checks.
  6. Registration The escribano registers the deed with the Registro de la Propiedad Inmueble. Registration is what makes your ownership effective against third parties.

Buenos Aires City exempts a buyer's sole family home from stamp duty up to ARS 226.1 million, but a non-resident investor will normally not qualify. When you sell: from January 1, 2026, individuals' gains on property sales are exempt from income tax under Ley 27.802 and Decreto 406/2026, which expressly covers people resident abroad. Take tax advice before you sell, as the rules are new.

Currency Controls and How Money Moves

Argentina's exchange controls (cepo) were largely lifted for individuals on April 14, 2025, when the central bank moved to a floating band. A partial restriction returned in September 2025: anyone who buys dollars at the official rate cannot buy certain dollar-settled financial assets for 90 days. The gap between the official and parallel ("blue") dollar has been near zero in 2026: about ARS 1,535 vs 1,545 on October 15, 2026.

  • Bank transfer: wire dollars to an Argentine account through the regulated market and keep every record.
  • Cash at the escribanía: paying in dollar bills at signing is traditional in Argentina, but the money still has to be accounted for under anti-money-laundering rules.
  • Document everything: keep wire receipts and statements showing the money leaving your home account. You will need them to prove the source of funds and to move sale proceeds or rent out later.

What Milei's Reforms Mean for Foreign Buyers

  • Rental law repealed: DNU 70/2023 repealed the 2020 rental law (Ley 27.551), so lease terms and increases are freely agreed. An attempt in Congress to overturn the decree failed for lack of quorum on October 15, 2026.
  • Currency opening: the April 2025 lifting of most controls and the near-zero exchange gap make it simpler to bring money in and take it out, though the September 2025 restriction shows rules can tighten again.
  • Fiscal surplus: 2024 was the first year with an overall (financial) budget surplus since 2010.
  • Inflation: much lower than in 2023–24 but still high: 1.9% in September 2026 and 33.4% over 12 months (INDEC).
  • RIGI: the large-investment regime (Ley 27.742) offers 30 years of tax, customs and FX stability for projects of US$200 million or more. It doesn't apply to home buyers.
  • Politics: Milei's party won the October 26, 2025 midterms with about 41% of the vote. The next presidential election is in 2027.

The honest risks: Argentina has a long history of sovereign defaults and currency crises. Mortgage lending is small (the stock is below 2% of GDP) and most buyers pay cash, although Zonaprop reports 17% of Buenos Aires City deeds used a mortgage in August 2026. That limits demand and resale liquidity. A return of tight exchange controls would make it harder to take rent or sale proceeds out. The US State Department rates Argentina Level 1, "Exercise Normal Precautions" (July 2026).

FAQ: Argentina Real Estate for Foreigners

Can foreigners buy property in Argentina?

Yes. Foreigners can buy urban property with the same rights as citizens and need no visa or residency. You need a CUIT tax ID from ARCA, which replaced the CDI for individuals in March 2026. Rural land is capped for foreigners under Ley 26.737, which the Supreme Court provisionally reinstated in October 2026.

How much does an apartment cost per m² in Buenos Aires in 2026?

Zonaprop's September 2026 index puts the Buenos Aires City average asking price at US$2,484 per m², 11.3% below its all-time high. Palermo averages US$3,454 per m² and La Boca US$1,621. The city's average gross rental yield is 5.7%.

What are the closing costs when buying in Argentina?

Brokers quoted by Ámbito put total buyer costs at about 5–8% of the price in Buenos Aires City and 8–12% in Buenos Aires Province. City stamp duty is 2.7% up to ARS 226.1 million and 3.5% above, usually split between buyer and seller. The 1.5% transfer tax (ITI) was repealed in 2024.

Can I get a mortgage as a foreigner in Argentina?

In practice, rarely. Mortgage credit is below 2% of Argentina's GDP and most purchases are paid in cash; Zonaprop reports 17% of Buenos Aires City deeds used a mortgage in August 2026, mostly by residents with local income. Plan to pay the full price in dollars.

Is Argentina Right for You?

Argentina suits buyers who (1) have patient dollar capital and a long holding period, (2) accept Latin American political and currency risk, and (3) want to spend time in Buenos Aires and own rather than rent. Gross yields of about 4.5–6% in the main expat barrios are reasonable but not exceptional.

It is not right for buyers who need mortgage leverage, quick resale, or predictable policy. The old pitch of buying at half of 2018 prices no longer holds: city prices are within about 11% of their peak. Base any purchase on today's yield, not on a big price rebound.
