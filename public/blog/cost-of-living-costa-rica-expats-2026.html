Cost of Living in Costa Rica 2026: Honest Numbers for Expats

By Charles Brossy April 30, 2026 · Updated October 27, 2026 11 min read

Costa Rica is often sold as an affordable retirement destination. The numbers in 2026 are more nuanced: it is cheaper than the United States, but it is the most expensive country in Central America on Numbeo's 2026 mid-year Cost of Living Index (56.8, against 46.5 for Panama, 44.4 for Mexico, 35.6 for Colombia and 31.0 for Ecuador). As of October 2026, Numbeo estimates that a single person in San José spends about $1,003 a month before rent, and a one-bedroom apartment outside the centre averages about $939 a month.

This guide sticks to figures we can source. Four places, crowdsourced Numbeo price data checked in October 2026, the official exchange rate and tax rate, and the residency rules from the law itself. Where a reliable figure does not exist, we say so rather than guess.

What Drives Costs in Costa Rica

Before the numbers, understanding the structural cost drivers helps you budget accurately:

  • IVA (value-added tax) at a general rate of 13%. Costa Rica's IVA applies at 13% to most goods and services, from restaurants to electronics, according to the Ministry of Finance's tax directorate (DGT). Some items, such as basic-basket groceries, medicines and private health services, carry reduced rates. The tax is included in the shelf price you see.
  • Vehicles carry import taxes and IVA. Imported cars pay customs taxes, and sales of new and used vehicles are charged IVA at the 13% general rate, according to the Ministry of Finance. Price a specific car with a dealer or customs broker before you plan around one; we do not publish an average figure.
  • The colón floats, and it has moved a lot. The Central Bank's (BCCR) reference rate was ₡451.24 buying and ₡455.53 selling per US dollar on October 27, 2026. That is far from fixed: the dollar peaked at ₡698.4 on June 23, 2022, and by March 2026 the colón was at its strongest level against the dollar since July 2005, according to the Universidad Nacional's economic observatory. If your income is in dollars, a stronger colón makes everything priced in colones more expensive for you.
  • Beach towns carry a premium. On Numbeo's October 2026 figures, a one-bedroom in central Tamarindo averages about $1,928 a month, against $1,185 in central San José and $659 in central Atenas.
  • Residents must join the public health system. Pensionado and rentista residents must be insured with the Caja Costarricense de Seguro Social (CCSS) and prove uninterrupted coverage when they renew their residence card (Ley 8764, art. 80). Budget for that contribution from day one.

The Car Cost Most Budgets Skip

The monthly figures above do not include a car, which matters more outside San José. Imported vehicles pay customs taxes, and vehicle sales carry 13% IVA. Get a quote for the specific vehicle you want, then spread its cost over the years you expect to keep it and add that to your monthly budget.

Healthcare Costs in Detail

  • CCSS (public system, the "Caja"): Pensionado and rentista residents must be insured with the CCSS, and they must show uninterrupted coverage from the date residence was granted when they renew their card (Ley 8764, arts. 78 and 80). The CCSS sets the monthly contribution; it is not a flat dollar fee, so ask the CCSS for your amount once your residence is approved.
  • Private healthcare: You can add private insurance or pay private clinics on top of CCSS coverage. Premiums depend on insurer, age and medical history, and consultation prices vary by provider. Get written quotes from the state insurer INS and private insurers; we do not publish price ranges we cannot source.
  • Tax on care: Private health services are among the items that carry a reduced IVA rate rather than the 13% general rate, according to the DGT's rate schedule.

What the premium buys is harder to price. Costa Rica's 1949 Constitution abolished the army as a permanent institution (Art. 12), and the US State Department rates Costa Rica Level 2, "Exercise Increased Caution", because of crime (advisory issued April 2, 2026): petty crime is common, and violent crime also affects tourists.

The Pensionado Visa: Key Numbers

Costa Rica's Pensionado residency requires a permanent, stable monthly pension from abroad of at least US$1,000 (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería 8764, art. 81). Temporary residence is granted for up to two years and is renewable (art. 79), and after three consecutive years of temporary residence you can apply for permanent residence (art. 78). Naturalisation by residence requires seven years of official residence for most nationalities, including US and Canadian citizens, or five years for Central Americans, Ibero-Americans and Spaniards by birth, plus passing the Spanish and social studies exams, according to the Supreme Electoral Tribunal (TSE).

The Rentista residency requires permanent, stable income of at least US$2,500 a month, from abroad or from Costa Rica's national banking system; that amount also covers a spouse and children under 25 (Ley 8764, art. 82). The implementing regulation adds rules on how the income must be documented and guaranteed, so check the current requirements list of the Dirección General de Migración y Extranjería (DGME) before you apply.

The Bottom Line

Costa Rica is not the cheapest option in Latin America. If minimizing monthly spend is the main goal, Numbeo's figures put Cuenca, Medellín and Buenos Aires well below San José. What Costa Rica offers instead is a different trade-off: a country that abolished its army in its constitution and a public health system that residents join by law, at a price closer to Lisbon or Panama City than to the cheapest cities in the region.

For the right buyer, someone who values stability and ease over pure cost efficiency, the premium can be worth it. For pure budget retirees, the math points elsewhere. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to live in Costa Rica per month?

As of October 2026, Numbeo estimates that a single person in San José spends about $1,003 a month excluding rent, and a family of four about $3,731. Rent comes on top: a one-bedroom apartment averages about $939 a month outside the city centre and $1,185 in the centre. Countrywide, Numbeo puts a single person at about $955 a month and a family of four at about $3,547 before rent.

Is Costa Rica expensive for expats?

By regional standards, yes. Numbeo's 2026 mid-year Cost of Living Index puts Costa Rica at 56.8, the highest in Central America (Belize 49.9, Panama 46.5) and above Mexico (44.4), Colombia (35.6), Ecuador (31.0) and Portugal (48.7). It is still below the United States (69.7): Numbeo estimates Costa Rica's living costs are 17.8% lower and its rents 40.9% lower than in the US. Most goods and services also carry Costa Rica's 13% general IVA rate.

What does healthcare cost in Costa Rica?

Pensionado and rentista residents must be insured with the Caja Costarricense de Seguro Social (CCSS) and show uninterrupted coverage when they renew their residence card (Ley 8764, art. 80). The CCSS sets the monthly contribution; it is not a flat fee published in dollars. Private insurance and consultation prices vary by insurer, age and provider, so get written quotes from the INS and private insurers. This guide does not publish price ranges it cannot source.

Where is the cheapest place to live in Costa Rica?

Of the places covered in this guide, the Central Valley towns of Atenas and Grecia have the lowest rents on Numbeo as of October 2026: a one-bedroom outside the centre averages about $549 a month in Atenas and $632 in Grecia, against $939 in San José and $949 in Tamarindo. Atenas and Grecia have few Numbeo contributors (10 and 9 in the past 18 months), so treat those figures as indicative.
