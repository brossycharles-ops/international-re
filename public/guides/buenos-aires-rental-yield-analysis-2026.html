Buenos Aires Rental Yields 2026: Barrio-by-Barrio Data

By Charles Brossy June 5, 2026 · Updated October 16, 2026 9 min read

How much do Buenos Aires rentals actually yield in 2026? Zonaprop's September 2026 index puts the citywide average gross rental yield for apartments at 5.70%, about 17.5 years of rent to recover the purchase price. Prices average USD 2,484/m². Yields vary sharply by barrio, from 3.2% in Puerto Madero to 9.6% in Lugano. The well-known northern barrios where most foreigners buy sit at the low end of that range. The data below is barrio by barrio, followed by the costs, taxes and risks to model.

Updated October 16, 2026. Figures are listing-based (Zonaprop Index CABA, September 2026) and are gross, before expenses and taxes.

The Deregulation Effect: Why This Market Changed

In December 2023, President Milei's emergency decree DNU 70/2023 (Article 249) repealed the 2020 Rental Law (Ley 27.551). That law had imposed three-year minimum leases with annual adjustments, and many landlords had pulled their units off the long-term market. A government impact study using Mercado Libre data found that by June 2024, rental listings across greater Buenos Aires (AMBA) were 211.9% higher than in December 2023. Real rents per m² fell a cumulative 26.6% over the same period.

The decree is still politically contested. The Senate voted to reject it in March 2024. An opposition attempt to overturn it in the Chamber of Deputies failed for lack of quorum on 15 October 2026 (121 deputies present, 129 needed). A DNU stays in force unless both chambers reject it, but an investor should treat the current freely negotiated lease regime as policy that could be reversed.

Puerto Madero: Prestige, Not Income

At USD 6,165/m², the waterfront towers of Puerto Madero are the most expensive apartments in the city, and at 3.2% gross they are its worst-yielding. Puerto Madero is also the most expensive barrio to rent in, with an average two-room (one-bedroom) rent of about ARS 1.27 million a month, but rent still doesn't keep pace with the price. Treat it as a wealth-preservation or lifestyle purchase.

Palermo and Núñez: Liquid, But Mid-4% Yields

Palermo (USD 3,454/m²) and Núñez (USD 3,409/m²) are among the most expensive barrios after Puerto Madero, and both yield 4.6% gross. Palermo is the most liquid barrio for resale to foreign buyers and the easiest to rent. Núñez is a quieter, more residential barrio in the north. Neither is a cheap entry point, and both sit in Zonaprop's lower-return group.

Belgrano, Recoleta and Villa Urquiza: The Middle Ground

Belgrano sits somewhere between USD 2,850 and 3,400/m² (its bar in the Zonaprop chart is unlabelled), and Recoleta and Villa Urquiza at about USD 2,800/m². Zonaprop does not publish yields for these barrios, so be sceptical of any broker who quotes a precise figure. At these prices, a USD 200,000 budget buys roughly 60–70 m² before closing costs. In Recoleta's many pre-war buildings, check maintenance costs and expensas carefully.

The South: Where the Yield Is

The city's highest gross yields are in the south: Villa Lugano at 9.6%, Parque Patricios at 7.7% and Nueva Pompeya at 7.6%. Entry prices are far lower (Lugano is the cheapest barrio at USD 1,071/m²). The trade-offs are local-only tenant demand, thinner resale liquidity and more management work. These are not barrios most foreign buyers know, and you will need a trusted local manager.

The Expensas Trap: Gross Yield Is a Starting Point

Expensas are mandatory monthly building fees for doormen, elevators, cleaning, insurance and reserves. The owner pays them whether or not the unit is let. According to ConsorcioAbierto data reported by iProfesional, the average expensa in CABA was about ARS 354,652 a month in July 2026. That is roughly USD 230 at Banco Nación's mid-October 2026 rate of about ARS 1,535 per dollar. Buildings with amenities such as pools, gyms and 24-hour security charge well above that. On a USD 150,000 apartment yielding 5% gross (USD 625 a month), an average expensa alone takes more than a third of the rent. Ask for the last 12 months of expensas statements before you make an offer.

Property tax (ABL/Inmobiliario) is charged on the city's fiscal valuation (valuación fiscal homogénea), not on your purchase price. For 2026 it rises with inflation for properties with a fiscal value up to ARS 32 million, and by inflation plus 1% a month above that. Ask the seller for the latest boleta and model the real bill.

Buying Costs and Taxes in Buenos Aires City

  • Total buyer costs: local brokers quoted by Ámbito put them at roughly 5–8% on top of the price. That covers notary (escribano), stamp duty, registry fees and the agent's commission.
  • Stamp duty (impuesto de sellos): under the 2026 Ley Tarifaria, 2.7% when the value is up to ARS 226.1 million and 3.5% above that. Brokers quoted by Ámbito note an exemption for a buyer's sole family home, which usually won't apply to an investment purchase.
  • When you sell: the old 1.5% transfer tax (ITI) was repealed in July 2024 (Ley 27.743). The 15% tax on gains from property bought after 2018 was charged through 2025. For sales from 1 January 2026, a 2026 income-tax reform exempts individuals' real-estate sale gains, subject to a regulatory decree that sets the conditions. How the exemption applies to non-residents and habitual traders is not yet settled, so take tax advice before you sell.
  • Foreign ownership: there is no restriction on foreigners buying urban apartments. Separate limits on rural land (Ley 26.737) were provisionally reinstated by the Supreme Court in October 2026, but they do not affect city condos. Sale prices are quoted and generally paid in US dollars.

Short-Term Rentals: Registration Required

Short-term rentals are a real option in Palermo, Recoleta and San Telmo, but they are regulated. Under CABA Law 6.255, every property offered as a temporary tourist rental must be registered with the city's Registro de Propiedades de Alquileres Temporarios Turísticos. Registration is free and mandatory, and the registry number is required to list on booking platforms. We found no authoritative, current short-term yield figure for the city. Model occupancy, nightly rates, platform and management fees, utilities and expensas yourself rather than relying on a broker's headline number. Check that the building's rules (reglamento de copropiedad) allow it before you buy.

Risks Worth Modelling

  • Inflation: it has fallen sharply from 2023–24 levels but is still high. INDEC put year-on-year CPI at 33.5% in August 2026 and 33.4% in September 2026. Peso-denominated leases need frequent indexation clauses.
  • Currency: most currency controls on individuals were lifted on 14 April 2025 under a managed floating band. A 90-day "cross restriction" on individuals was restored in September 2025. The gap between the official and parallel ("blue") dollar was zero in late May 2026 and about 1% in mid-October 2026 (ARS 1,535 vs 1,545). Controls have been reimposed before, so don't assume dollar access is permanent.
  • Evictions: the Cámara de Propietarios estimates that an ordinary eviction trial for non-payment takes 18–24 months at minimum. An "express eviction" bill passed the Senate and, as of mid-October 2026, was still pending in the Chamber of Deputies. A guarantor (garante) or rent-guarantee insurance (seguro de caución) is the standard mitigation.
  • Political reversal: the lease deregulation behind today's supply rests on a decree that Congress is still trying to overturn.

The Bottom Line

Buenos Aires is a deep, dollar-priced market with transparent listing data. Its headline yield of 5.7% gross is respectable, not spectacular. The barrios foreigners favour (Palermo, Núñez, Puerto Madero) yield 3–4.6% gross before expensas and tax, so they are mostly appreciation and lifestyle plays. The higher-yielding south demands local management and a tolerance for lower liquidity. Model expensas, the real ABL bill, an 18–24-month eviction scenario and the political risk to the lease regime before you commit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What gross rental yield does Buenos Aires offer in 2026?

Zonaprop's September 2026 index puts the citywide gross yield for apartments at 5.70%, about 17.5 years of rent to recover the price. Yields range from 3.2% in Puerto Madero to 9.6% in Villa Lugano, with Palermo and Núñez at 4.6%.

What taxes apply when buying property in Buenos Aires City?

Stamp duty is 2.7% for values up to ARS 226.1 million and 3.5% above that (Ley Tarifaria 2026), and a buyer's sole family home can qualify for an exemption. Brokers estimate total buyer costs at roughly 5–8% of the price.

Do I need a permit to rent short-term in Buenos Aires?

Yes. Under Law 6.255, every property offered as a short-term tourist rental must be registered with the city's Registro de Propiedades de Alquileres Temporarios Turísticos. Registration is free, and the registry number is needed to list on booking platforms.
