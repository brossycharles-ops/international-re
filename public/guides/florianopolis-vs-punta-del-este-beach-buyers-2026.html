Florianópolis vs Punta del Este: Beach Property Comparison 2026

By Marco Delgado June 10, 2026 7 min read

Two of South America's most desirable coastal markets, separated by roughly 1,200 km and a fundamental divide in price level. Florianópolis offers entry below $2,500/sqm in a city that has risen 52% over five years and still attracts double-digit short-term rental yields in the right pocket. Punta del Este averages $4,000/sqm, transacts in USD, and delivers the regulatory simplicity and lifestyle prestige of Uruguay's most internationally recognised resort. Here is what the 2026 data actually shows.

Florianópolis: High Momentum, Lower Entry

Florianópolis — "Floripa" to Brazilians — is an island city off the coast of Santa Catarina with 42 beaches and a tech economy that has drawn São Paulo professionals, Argentine expats, and US digital nomads in steadily increasing numbers. The result is a market that rose 52% in price over five years (2021–2026), making it Brazil's second most expensive capital for residential real estate, yet still sitting well below $3,000/sqm in its mid-market neighbourhoods.

The location calculus is straightforward. Jurerê Internacional — Floripa's most exclusive address, with private beach clubs, gated compounds, and a roster of Brazilian celebrities as neighbours — commands asking prices of $3,500–$5,500/sqm and entire homes ranging from R$2.5M to R$8M ($450K–$1.44M). Lagoa da Conceição, the bohemian lagoon district preferred by long-stay expats and surf-culture buyers, runs $2,200–$3,500/sqm for homes and apartments. Value-oriented investors look to Ingleses do Rio Vermelho on the island's north tip (~$1,600/sqm) and Estreito on the mainland side (~$1,780/sqm), where commuter demand drives reliable long-term rental yields of 6–7.5%.

The most important number for STR investors: Floripa's short-term rental market can deliver 7–12% gross yield near the beaches — but verify condo bylaws before buying. Brazil's Superior Tribunal of Justice confirmed in 2021 that condominium associations may ban Airbnb-style rentals, and some Jurerê buildings have exercised that right. For long-term rentals, the average gross yield across the city sits at roughly 5.7%, with studios near the Federal University of Santa Catarina (UFSC) consistently hitting 6.5–7.5%. Foreign buyers face no restrictions on urban condominium purchases in Brazil — the federal rural land law (5.709/1971) does not apply to city apartments. Transactions close in 60–90 days via a Brazilian cartório (notary), with a transfer tax (ITBI) typically at 2–3% of the declared value. Brazil's Digital Nomad Visa (VITEM XIV) allows a two-year stay for remote workers earning above roughly $1,500/month, making it straightforward to manage a property from the ground.

Punta del Este: USD Security, Global Prestige

Punta del Este is a different proposition entirely. Uruguay's jewel resort sits on a narrow peninsula 140 km east of Montevideo, where the Atlantic meets the Río de la Plata. It has spent decades cultivating a reputation as the "Monaco of South America" — luxury tower developments, a world-class marina, casinos, and a January high season that draws wealthy Argentines, Brazilians, and Europeans. The result is one of the most USD-stable, legally transparent real estate markets in Latin America.

The city-wide average of $4,000/sqm already prices Punta del Este above most Latin American peers, but the internal range is dramatic. The Peninsula — the most sought-after address, surrounded on three sides by water — commands $6,000–$10,000/sqm for new luxury builds. Trophy buildings like Trump Tower and Mouette push toward the top of that band. Playa Brava and Playa Mansa beachfront apartments run $5,000–$7,000/sqm. More accessible entry exists in Maldonado (the adjacent city) and outlying zones like Piriápolis, where prices drop to $2,500–$3,500/sqm — still Uruguay's legal market, with all the same property rights.

The rental yield story has a seasonal asterisk. Annual lease yields of 4.5–6% are achievable and stable, but the real upside comes from seasonal rentals targeting the December–March high season, which can push gross yields to 7–10% on well-positioned apartments — provided you accept low occupancy for the other nine months. International buyers now represent 75% of recent luxury segment purchases, with Argentines, Brazilians, and Americans leading demand. The practical advantage Punta del Este has over Floripa for USD-denominated investors: all transactions are priced and settled in US dollars, eliminating the currency translation layer entirely.

Uruguay's foreign buyer framework is among the cleanest in the region — no restrictions, no special permits, no local partner requirements, and closings typically in 30–60 days. Property acquisition tax (ITP) runs approximately 3–4% of the declared value. Uruguay also offers one of Latin America's most accessible residency programmes: buy any property, show modest income ($1,500/month), and permanent residency follows in 1–3 years, eventually unlocking Uruguay's territorial tax system — meaning foreign-sourced income is not taxed at all. Head to Head: Which Market Fits Your Profile?

Choose Florianópolis if you want a lower-dollar entry point into a fast-appreciating beach market with genuine year-round demand — not just a seasonal resort. The BRL price tag means a weaker Brazilian real actually makes entry cheaper for USD buyers, though it also means appreciation gains are partly captured in a depreciating currency. Floripa is the better bet for buyers who want to live in the property part of the year and rent it out the rest: the tech and nomad community sustains occupancy through shoulder season in ways that Punta del Este's winter months simply do not.

Choose Punta del Este if USD denomination, legal transparency, and the credibility of a globally recognised resort address matter more than yield optimisation. Uruguay's territorial tax system is a meaningful long-term benefit for high-income buyers — the combination of property ownership, residency, and tax planning can be materially valuable. The caveat: you are buying into a market where January is the economy and the rest of the year is the off-season. Underwrite occupancy conservatively; the gross yield ceiling is real, but the floor can be surprisingly low.

Both markets allow foreigners to own property with full title, identical rights to citizens, and no prior residency requirement. The core trade-off is clear: Florianópolis gives you more upside per dollar invested in a growing market with year-round demand, while Punta del Este gives you USD security, prestige, and one of Latin America's best residency-to-tax pathways.
