Medellín vs Buenos Aires for Expat Investors: 2026 Market Comparison

By Marco Delgado May 13, 2026 7 min read

Two cities. Two very different investment cases. Medellín offers lower entry prices, double-digit nominal appreciation, and a surging digital nomad economy. Buenos Aires offers a recovering USD market that is still roughly 10% below its 2016 peak — a rare case of a major South American capital where prices are cheaper now than a decade ago. Here is what the 2026 data actually shows.

Medellín: Lower Entry, Higher Appreciation Momentum

Medellín is the clearest example of a market where the early-mover window is still open but closing. Prices in El Poblado — the neighborhood most popular with expats and digital nomads — rose roughly 10% in nominal terms through 2025, with one in four properties sold to foreign buyers or Colombians living abroad. New construction in prime El Poblado pockets now approaches $3,500/sqm, but resale inventory still sits in the $1,600–$2,300/sqm range, giving value-oriented buyers a meaningful entry point before new-build pricing becomes the market benchmark.

The yield story depends on where you buy. El Poblado's gross rental yields have compressed to 3.5–5.5% as purchase prices have outpaced rents — common in any neighborhood that goes from hidden gem to international hotspot in under a decade. The smarter yield play is Laureles, the residential and commercial barrio immediately west of El Poblado. Laureles delivers 7–8.5% gross on well-chosen apartments, with strong long-term tenant demand from young Colombian professionals and a growing expat corridor, yet without El Poblado's premium pricing. Castilla and Estadio/Suramericana offer similar numbers for investors willing to go further afield.

Colombia's Digital Nomad Visa (Type V) requires roughly $1,400/month in foreign-source income — low by regional standards — and allows a 2-year stay renewable once. It does not automatically confer residency, but it makes Medellín legal to live in while you manage your investment directly. Foreign property ownership is unrestricted, title is registered in pesos, and transactions typically close in 60–90 days with a Colombian notary. Buenos Aires: A Recovering Market Still at a Discount

Buenos Aires is one of the few major cities in the world where you can buy in a genuinely world-class cultural capital at prices that are cheaper in USD than they were a decade ago. The city-wide average of ~$2,450/sqm in early 2026 is still roughly 10% below January 2016 levels in dollar terms, despite a 5% USD recovery in 2025 and another 5% forecast for 2026. For a patient buyer, that gap represents the recovery upside.

The prime expat neighborhoods tell a more nuanced story. Palermo — Buenos Aires's most internationally recognized barrio, with its parks, restaurant scene, and co-working ecosystem — runs around $3,400/sqm. Recoleta, the city's most architecturally storied district, sits at $3,500–$4,300/sqm. At the top, Puerto Madero's waterfront towers command $6,100/sqm, making it among the most expensive residential real estate in South America. The key yield insight for Buenos Aires is that gross figures of 6–9% are achievable in popular barrios on studio and one-bedroom units, but the city's mandatory expensas (building maintenance fees) consume 15–30% of gross rental income — a cost that many online yield figures omit entirely. Underwrite with expensas included before buying.

Real estate transactions in Argentina are denominated in US dollars and settled in cash, which eliminates currency risk at the point of sale. The Argentine peso's ongoing volatility does not directly affect property pricing, though it complicates day-to-day cost-of-living planning. The Rentista residency visa is available for passive income holders and leads to permanent residency after two years — faster than most Latin American equivalents. Argentina's bienes personales wealth tax applies to Argentine-held assets above a threshold, so factor that into your net-return calculation; the rate has been reduced significantly under the current administration but it remains real.

Head to Head: Which Market Fits Your Profile?

Choose Medellín if you want lower entry prices, strong nominal appreciation, and a city with genuine momentum behind it — tourism arrivals up, digital nomad infrastructure maturing, and the Colombian peso providing a natural buffer against USD-denominated cost increases. The Digital Nomad Visa makes it easy to be on the ground managing your asset. Laureles is the value neighborhood; El Poblado is the lifestyle choice with compressed yields.

Choose Buenos Aires if you want to buy a world-city asset at a genuine discount to its historical peak, in a market where USD-denominated pricing insulates you from local inflation. The cultural depth, European urban fabric, and recovering international profile make it a credible long-term hold. Just model expensas into your yield from day one, and treat the gross figures you see advertised as ceiling rather than floor.

Both cities allow foreigners to own property with identical rights to citizens — no local partners, no special permits, no prior residency required.
