Medellín vs Buenos Aires for Expat Investors: 2026 Market Comparison

By Charles Brossy May 13, 2026 · Updated October 17, 2026 8 min read

Medellín vs Buenos Aires comes down to two different investment cases. Medellín offers higher gross rental yields (about 6.9% on average, per Global Property Guide), but prices are in Colombian pesos. The peso gained about 22% against the dollar in the 12 months to October 2026, so dollar prices there have climbed fast. Buenos Aires prices are in US dollars and still sit 11.3% below their historical peak. Yields there are lower (5.7% city-wide, 4.6% in Palermo, per Zonaprop), and the country's economic record is more volatile. Here is what current data shows, checked on October 17, 2026.

Medellín: Higher Yields, Peso Exposure

Medellín's average asking price is about COP 4.5 million per m² across the metro area (Portafolio, February 2026), or roughly US$1,400 at today's exchange rate. Metrocuadrado's August 2026 data shows a wide spread by estrato (the local socio-economic tier): about COP 5.1–6.5 million per m² in estratos 3–4 (≈US$1,600–2,050) and COP 7.7–12.3 million in estratos 5–6 (≈US$2,400–3,900). In El Poblado, the area most popular with foreigners, Ciencuadras puts apartment asking prices at about COP 8.0 million per m² (≈US$2,530). Portafolio gives a COP 6–9.5 million range for high-demand new projects in El Poblado, Envigado and Sabaneta. The sources do not fully agree: Portafolio's metro-area average sits below Metrocuadrado's lowest Medellín band, so treat all of these as asking-price ranges.

On yields, Global Property Guide's Q3 2026 survey puts Medellín's average gross rental yield at 6.88%, against 7.06% for Colombia as a whole. Its El Poblado samples range from 6.35% (2-bed) to 7.75% (larger units), and Laureles from 6.21% to 7.57%. These are gross figures from long-term asking rents. Management fees, building fees (administración), vacancy and tax come off the top. Furnished short-term rentals also need a national tourism registration (RNT) and must comply with the building's rules.

The big swing factor is the currency. The official COP/USD rate moved from 3,877 on October 17, 2025 to 3,175 on October 17, 2026, so the dollar buys about 18% fewer pesos and the peso has gained about 22%. A dollar buyer who bought a year ago gained on paper; a dollar buyer today pays about 22% more for the same peso price, and rent is collected in pesos. Foreign buyers must bring the money in through the official exchange market and register it as foreign investment with Banco de la República.

For residency, Colombia's Digital Nomad visa (V Nómada Digital) needs foreign income of at least 3 monthly minimum wages, which is COP 5,252,715 or about US$1,650 in 2026. It lasts up to 2 years but is a visitor visa: Cancillería says V visas do not grant temporary or permanent resident status. The property route is the M Inversionista visa: real estate registered as foreign investment worth at least 350 minimum wages (COP 612.8 million, about US$193,000). Note that the US State Department rates Colombia Level 3, "Reconsider Travel". Unsure whether the Colombia or Argentina profile fits your goals? Buenos Aires: Dollar Prices Still Below the Peak

Zonaprop's September 2026 index puts the average Buenos Aires asking price at US$2,484 per m². Prices rose 5.4% in 2025 but only 1.4% so far in 2026, and they remain 11.3% below the series peak of about US$2,800 reached around 2018–2019. For a patient buyer, that gap is the recovery case.

Palermo averages US$3,454 per m² and Puerto Madero US$6,165, the most expensive barrio in the index. Zonaprop's barrio chart puts Recoleta well below Palermo, under the US$2,837 of Chacarita. On yields, Zonaprop puts the city's average gross yield at 5.7% and Palermo's at 4.6%. Puerto Madero is 3.2%, and only cheaper southern barrios such as Lugano (9.6%) reach high single digits. Monthly building fees (expensas) averaged about ARS 354,700 in July 2026 (iProfesional, citing a survey of 15,000 buildings). The owner carries them in any vacant month, so check the building's accounts and the lease terms before underwriting.

Property in Argentina is priced in US dollars, and most deals are cash. The Colegio de Escribanos reported that 17% of August 2026 deeds used a mortgage. That protects the purchase price from peso swings. Foreigners can buy urban property with no special permit. Ley 26.737's limits on foreign ownership apply only to rural land, and a Supreme Court order of October 2026 has put them back in force for now.

Argentina has no property-purchase residency route. The rentista temporary residence needs passive income of at least 5 times the minimum wage, which is ARS 1,956,000 a month from October 2026 (about US$1,275 at the official rate). It is granted for up to 3 years and is renewable. Non-MERCOSUR nationals can apply for permanent residence after 3 years of temporary residence. A foreign owner with no Argentine residence pays the bienes personales wealth tax at 0.50% a year of the property's value. It is collected through a local substitute taxpayer. The recent cuts lowered the rates for residents, not this non-resident rate.

Head to Head: Which Market Fits Your Profile?

Choose Medellín if gross yield matters most and you are comfortable holding a peso asset. Average yields run about a point higher than Buenos Aires (6.9% vs 5.7%), and El Poblado and Laureles both sample above 6%. A property worth about US$193,000 or more can also support an investor visa. The trade-offs: after the peso's 22% rise you are buying in dollars at a less favourable rate than a year ago, and the US advisory is Level 3.

Choose Buenos Aires if you want a dollar-priced asset that still trades below its historical peak, in a city with a Level 1 travel advisory. Expect lower gross yields in the barrios foreigners favour (4.6% in Palermo), little mortgage finance, and no residency from the purchase itself. Argentina's long record of policy swings and currency controls is the main risk to weigh.

In both cities foreigners can own city apartments with the same rights as citizens, with no local partner or prior residency required. For weekly data on how both markets are moving through 2026, our newsletter tracks prices, yields and policy changes across Latin America.

FAQ: Medellín vs Buenos Aires for Investors

Which has higher rental yields, Medellín or Buenos Aires?

Medellín, on current data. Global Property Guide puts Medellín's average gross rental yield at 6.88% (Q3 2026). Zonaprop puts the Buenos Aires average at 5.70% and Palermo at 4.6% (September 2026). Both are gross figures before fees, vacancy and tax.

Is property cheaper in Medellín or Buenos Aires?

Medellín's metro-area average is about COP 4.5 million per m², roughly US$1,400, against US$2,484 per m² in Buenos Aires. In the prime areas the gap is smaller: El Poblado runs about US$2,530 per m² (Ciencuadras), and Palermo US$3,454. Medellín's dollar prices rose as the peso gained about 22% against the dollar in a year.

Can buying property give me residency in Colombia or Argentina?

In Colombia, yes. The M Inversionista visa accepts real estate registered as foreign investment worth at least 350 minimum wages, about US$193,000 in 2026. Argentina has no property-purchase visa; its rentista residence requires passive income of 5 times the minimum wage, ARS 1,956,000 a month from October 2026.
