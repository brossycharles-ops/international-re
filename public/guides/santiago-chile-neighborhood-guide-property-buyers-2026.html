Best Neighborhoods to Buy Property in Santiago, Chile (2026)

By Charles Brossy October 16, 2026 9 min read

The best neighborhoods to buy property in Santiago, Chile depend on your goal. Las Condes and Providencia are the established mid-to-upper choices, with new apartments averaging about 114 and 103 UF/m². Vitacura is the priciest at about 117 UF/m². Ñuñoa is a cheaper residential alternative, and Santiago Centro is the entry-level option, with the highest measured yield at roughly 5% gross. Foreigners buy on the same terms as Chileans. Below: sourced prices, rents, taxes and the risks.

Exchange rates used throughout: 1 UF = CLP 41,168.03 (16 October 2026, SII UF table) and US$1 = CLP 972.60 (dólar observado of 1 October 2026, SII table of Banco Central rates). So 1 UF ≈ US$42.33. Dollar figures are approximate and move with the peso. Prices are asking or developer prices, not closed sales.

Vitacura: Santiago's Most Expensive Comuna

Vitacura topped TOCTOC's ranking of new non-subsidised apartments at 117 UF/m² (≈US$4,950) in the rolling quarter to July 2026. TOCTOC's analysis of the eastern market says Vitacura is dominated by higher tickets and larger units. Even so, in TOCTOC data to October 2025, two-bedroom apartments were its best-sellers, ahead of three- and four-bedroom units. Three-bedroom rents were the highest of any comuna in TOCTOC's 2025 rent study, at about CLP 2.49 million a month. Vitacura suits buyers who will live in the property more than buyers chasing yield.

Las Condes: The Liquid Executive Market

Las Condes averaged 114 UF/m² (≈US$4,830) for new apartments in the same TOCTOC data. In TOCTOC data to October 2025, one-bedroom, one-bathroom units of about 38 m² led its sales, with the comuna's lowest tickets, followed by two-bedrooms. Three-bedrooms turn over much more slowly. If you want an asset that is easy to rent to professionals and easy to resell, a small unit in Las Condes is the default choice.

Providencia: Best Balance for Rental Investors

Providencia averaged 103 UF/m² (≈US$4,360), about 10% below Las Condes. TOCTOC's data to October 2025 showed units of 40 to 70 m² absorbed fastest there, mainly two-bed, two-bath and one-bed, one-bath layouts. Three-bedroom units sell only sporadically and stay on the market longer. A three-bedroom rented for about CLP 1.14 million a month in 2025, a little over half the Las Condes level, so check rents for the exact building before you buy. Providencia suits buyers who want a central location without Vitacura or Las Condes prices.

Ñuñoa: The Residential Value Play

Ñuñoa was the third-best-selling comuna for new apartments in Greater Santiago from January to July 2026, with 1,680 units, behind Santiago Centro and La Florida. TOCTOC's April 2026 report also listed Ñuñoa among the comunas with the largest apartment price rises (9.4%). We found no 2026 comuna-level UF/m² figure from a reliable source. Two 2025 studies put Ñuñoa at about 89 UF/m² (Activo Más, via La Tercera) and 98 UF/m² (BDO, via BioBioChile), or roughly US$3,770–4,150. A three-bedroom rented for about CLP 786,000 a month in 2025. Ñuñoa suits long-stay residents who want more space for the money.

Santiago Centro: Cheapest Entry, Highest Measured Yields

Santiago Centro led new-apartment sales from January to July 2026 with 2,512 units, up 23.1% year on year. 2025 studies put it at about 69–77 UF/m² (≈US$2,920–3,260). Global Property Guide's Q3 2026 survey covers neighborhoods here such as the Centro Histórico, Santa Isabel, Bulnes, Parque Almagro and Barrio Diez de Julio. It measured an average gross yield of 5.01% there. The trade-off: in TOCTOC's August 2026 report, Santiago had the most apartment desistimientos (buyers who fail to complete or cannot close their mortgage) of any comuna, at 121 units.

Lo Barnechea

Lo Barnechea's three-bedroom rents were the second-highest in TOCTOC's 2025 study, at about CLP 2.19 million. We found no reliable 2026 UF/m² figure for it, so we have left it out of the table. If you are looking there, ask for recent comparable sales from the Conservador.

How Foreigners Buy Property in Chile

Article 57 of Chile's Civil Code states that the law makes no distinction between Chileans and foreigners in acquiring civil rights. The one nationality-based limit is in article 7 of Decreto Ley 1939. It bars nationals of bordering countries (and companies they control, or that are 40% or more owned by them) from acquiring property in zones declared border zones under DFL 4 of 1967, unless the President authorises it by supreme decree. It does not apply to Santiago apartments bought by North Americans or Europeans. Our legal guide on whether foreigners can buy property in Chile covers this in more depth.

  1. Get a RUT. The SII says foreigners without domicile or residence must register for a RUT if they invest in Chile, and it lists buying real estate as an example. Those registering show a valid passport if they have no Chilean ID card.
  2. Check title. Have a lawyer review the title history and liens at the Conservador de Bienes Raíces, plus whether the contribuciones are paid up.
  3. Sign the escritura pública before a notary. Under article 1801 of the Civil Code, a real-estate sale is not legally complete until a public deed has been executed.
  4. Register at the Conservador. Under article 686, ownership of real estate passes only when the title is inscribed in the Conservador's register. Until then, you do not own the property.

Buyer Costs, Contribuciones and IVA

Closing costs. Global Property Guide (September 2026 update) puts buyer-side costs at 2.3%–3.4%: notary 0.3–0.4%, legal fees about 1% and an agent's fee of 1–2%. If you take a mortgage, the SII's stamp tax (impuesto de timbres) applies to the loan at 0.066% a month, capped at 0.8%.

Contribuciones (property tax). The SII taxes only the part of the fiscal appraisal (avalúo fiscal) above an exempt amount. For homes in the second half of 2026, that exemption is CLP 61,711,570 (≈US$63,450). The rate is 0.893% up to CLP 220,398,431 and 1.042% above it. The tax is paid in four instalments (April, June, September and November), and surcharges and municipal refuse fees can be added. Example: an appraisal of CLP 150 million gives (150M − 61.7M) × 0.893% ≈ CLP 788,000 a year (≈US$810). The fiscal appraisal is not the price you paid, so ask the seller for the latest SII certificate.

IVA on new homes. Since 1 January 2016, sales of homes by habitual sellers such as developers have been subject to 19% IVA. It is charged on the price minus the land value, and the deduction for land is capped. Resales by private owners who are not habitual sellers are generally outside IVA. The Reconstrucción Nacional law, promulgated on 9 October 2026, suspends IVA on the first sale of finished new homes for one year, with no cap on price or size. On 13 October, Diario Financiero reported that the law was still awaiting publication in the Diario Oficial. El Mercurio's Emol reported that if publication happens in October, the exemption would start on 1 December 2026. If you are buying new, ask the developer in writing how the exemption affects your price. From 2027 the same law also fully exempts the primary home of owners aged 65 or older from contribuciones.

Rental income. Global Property Guide notes that non-residents pay Chile's 35% Additional Tax on net Chilean-source income, with credits for some taxes already paid. Get Chilean tax advice before you rent out a property.

Rental Yields

Global Property Guide puts Chile's average gross rental yield at 4.80% (Q3 2026) and Santiago's at 5.01%, based on listings in central Santiago. Net yields are typically 1.5 to 2 points lower. Eastern comunas are not in its published Santiago table, so do not assume 5% in Las Condes or Vitacura. Compare listed rents with the purchase price for the specific building.

Market Conditions and Risks

  • Large but falling stock. At the end of July 2026, the Metropolitan Region had 49,834 new apartments for sale, down 18.2% from 60,893 a year earlier. 46.8% were ready for immediate delivery. With that much finished stock, buyers may find room to negotiate, especially on completed units. For the supply side, see our analysis of Santiago's housing supply squeeze.
  • Sales are recovering slowly in a weak economy. 16,561 new homes sold in the Metropolitan Region from January to August 2026, up 1.7% year on year. Apartment desistimientos fell to 15.2% in August. In the same Emol report, the Imacec activity index fell 1% in August and unemployment rose to 9.6%.
  • Prices depend on the measure. TOCTOC's metro-wide new-apartment average rose 5.4% in a year to 83 UF/m². Because prices are quoted in UF while the peso floats against the dollar, price your purchase in both UF and dollars.
  • UF-indexed mortgages. Chilean home loans are usually set in UF at a fixed rate. The UF payment is fixed, but the peso payment rises with inflation, while your dollar cost moves with the exchange rate.
  • Crime. The US State Department rates Chile Level 2: Exercise Increased Caution (May 2026) because of crime and civil unrest. It notes that street crime is common and violent crime is rising. Chile's prosecutor's office recorded 1,091 homicide victims in 2025, a rate of 5.4 per 100,000, down 11.5% from 6.1 in 2024. Ask about building security and visit at night before you buy.
  • Earthquakes: the code is a plus. After the 2010 earthquake, fewer than 0.5% of roughly 10,000 buildings in the affected zone were damaged and almost none collapsed, according to Universidad de Chile. In August 2026, MINVU approved the updated NCh433 seismic standard, which requires extra studies to build on active faults. Santiago's San Ramón fault runs about 50 km along the eastern foothills, so ask for the building's structural approval documents.

Which Neighborhood Should You Choose?

For a home you will live in, look at Vitacura or Las Condes for space and services, or Ñuñoa for value. For a rental investment, compare small Providencia units with central Santiago, where measured yields are highest but buyer demand is softer. Frequently Asked Questions

Which Santiago neighborhood is best for a foreign buyer?

It depends on the goal. Las Condes and Providencia are the mid-to-upper eastern choices, with new non-subsidised apartments averaging about 114 and 103 UF/m² in TOCTOC's data to July 2026. Vitacura is the most expensive at about 117 UF/m². Ñuñoa and Santiago Centro are cheaper, and central Santiago is where Global Property Guide measures gross yields of about 5%.

Can a foreigner buy an apartment in Santiago without living in Chile?

Yes. Chile's Civil Code (article 57) makes no distinction between Chileans and foreigners in acquiring civil rights. A non-resident needs a RUT tax number from the SII. The only nationality-based limit is for nationals of bordering countries buying in declared border zones, which needs presidential authorisation (DL 1939, article 7). Santiago apartments are not affected.

Do new apartments in Santiago include IVA?

Since 2016, sales of homes by habitual sellers such as developers have been subject to Chile's 19% IVA, charged on the price minus the land value. The Reconstrucción Nacional law, promulgated on 9 October 2026, suspends IVA on the first sale of finished new homes for one year once it takes effect. It was still awaiting publication in the Diario Oficial in mid-October 2026.
