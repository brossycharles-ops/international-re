Panama City vs San José 2026: Yields, Prices, Taxes

By Charles Brossy April 22, 2026 · Updated October 16, 2026 7 min read

Panama City or San José for expat investors in 2026? On Global Property Guide's latest data, their gross rental yields are close: about 7.7% in Panama City (Q4 2026) and 8.0% in San José (Q2 2026). The real differences are elsewhere. Panama is fully dollarized and its asking prices sit around $1,880/m². San José has a floating colón, faster recent price growth and lower property tax. Below is a sourced side-by-side.

Updated October 16, 2026. Price and yield figures are gross and based on asking prices and rents. Global Property Guide notes that net yields are usually 1.5–2 percentage points lower.

Panama City: Dollarized and Cheaper per Square Metre

Panama uses the US dollar as legal tender, with the balboa at par. A dollar-based investor carries no exchange-rate risk on rent or resale, which few other markets in the region can offer. Panama City's average asking price was about $1,881/m² in September 2025, up 6.8% year on year, according to Global Property Guide's analysis of RIAL/Di Tella data.

Global Property Guide's Q4 2026 survey (October 2026) puts Panama City's average gross yield at 7.66%. The best-yielding pocket is now El Cangrejo, at about 9.5% on a studio or one-bedroom (median price ~$189,000, rent ~$1,500/month). Bella Vista has slipped to about 7.8% (~$185,000, ~$1,200/month), and San Francisco one-bedrooms yield about 7.2%. Two-bedrooms rent for about $1,400 a month in San Francisco and $1,450 in El Cangrejo. Yields move from survey to survey, so treat any single neighborhood figure as a snapshot. There is no authoritative price index for luxury districts such as Punta Pacifica and Costa del Este, so check comparable sales before you buy there.

The economy is a support. INEC's preliminary figures put Panama's real GDP growth at 4.4% in 2025, and the IMF, in its August 2025 Article IV review, projected about 4% a year over the medium term.

San José: Faster Price Growth, Floating Currency

Prices in San José have risen faster. Global Property Guide reports San José house asking prices up about 17.6% year on year in Q1 2026 and 12.5% in June 2026 (Encuentra24 data). New-build and prime apartments in the metro area averaged about $2,900/m² in a July 2026 survey. San José's average gross yield of 8.05% (Q2 2026) is slightly above Panama City's. A typical one-bedroom costs about $170,000 and rents for about $1,100.

Within the metro, yields vary. Santa Ana one-bedrooms yield about 8.5% (median ~$155,000), and nearby Heredia averages about 8.7%. Escazú, the most expensive expat suburb, yields about 6.4% on a ~$225,000 one-bedroom. Multinational employers such as Intel, whose campus is in San Antonio de Belén (Heredia), sit in the Greater Metropolitan Area rather than San José proper and support rental demand from professionals.

The key risk for dollar-based investors is currency. Costa Rica's colón is not pegged: the central bank (BCCR) has run a managed float since 2015. In August 2026 the colón strengthened to about ¢449.5 per dollar on the MONEX market, the strongest level in roughly two decades. Rents are often quoted in dollars, but local costs, taxes and part of the tenant base are in colones.

Ownership Rules: Both Markets Are Open to Foreigners

Both countries let foreigners own titled property on the same terms as citizens, with no local partner or residency required. Panama's Constitution bars foreigners, and companies with any foreign ownership, from owning property within 10 km of its borders. In Costa Rica, the first 200 m inland from the high-tide line is the maritime-terrestrial zone (Ley 6043). The first 50 m is public and cannot be owned by anyone. The remaining 150 m can only be held through a municipal concession. Concessions cannot go to foreigners who have lived in the country for less than five years, to entities more than 50% foreign-owned, or to entities set up in the country by foreigners. None of this affects condos in San José.

Property Taxes and Closing Costs

Panama (Ley 66 of 2017): a primary residence registered for the exemption pays nothing on the first $120,000 of value, 0.5% on the portion up to $700,000 and 0.7% above that. Second homes and investment property pay 0% up to $30,000, then 0.6% to $250,000, 0.8% to $500,000 and 1% above. The exemption must be requested from the DGI. The 2% transfer tax (ITBI) is charged on the higher of the sale price and the cadastral value. Who pays it is often negotiated, so state it in the contract. Global Property Guide puts buyer-side costs at about 4.1% when the buyer bears the transfer tax, plus about 2% legal fees and 0.1% notary. Under Ley 546 of August 2026, a new home priced up to $200,000 is exempt on its first $120,000, and the excess is taxed at reduced rates of 0.5–1.8%. The sale must close within two years of the occupancy permit.

Costa Rica: the municipal property tax is 0.25% of registered value a year (Ley 7509). Homes whose construction value exceeds ¢143 million in 2026 (about $315,000) also pay the solidarity tax (impuesto solidario) at progressive rates of 0.25%–0.55%. The 1.5% transfer tax (Ley 6999) is charged on the deed value, never below the recorded fiscal value. By law, buyer and seller are jointly liable for it, and in practice the split is negotiated. Add stamps, registry and notary fees with VAT. Global Property Guide estimates buyer-side costs at about 4.25%.

The Verdict: What Type of Investor Are You?

If you want no currency risk and a lower price per square metre, Panama City is the better fit. El Cangrejo currently has the best measured yield in the city, and the economy is growing at about 4% a year.

If you want stronger recent price growth and slightly higher average yields, and can live with colón exposure, San José is the better fit. Santa Ana and Heredia currently yield more than Escazú.

In both markets, foreigners can buy outright, and closing costs are moderate by international standards. Before you commit, budget for the net yield (gross minus about 1.5–2 points) rather than the headline figure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can foreigners buy beachfront property in Costa Rica?

Not freehold within the 200 m maritime-terrestrial zone. The first 50 m is public, and the remaining 150 m can only be held by municipal concession, which foreigners cannot obtain until they have lived in Costa Rica for five years (Ley 6043). Condos in San José are not affected.

How much property tax will I pay on a home in Panama?

A registered primary residence is exempt up to $120,000, then pays 0.5% up to $700,000 and 0.7% above. Second homes and investment property pay 0% up to $30,000, then 0.6%, 0.8% and 1% in brackets (Ley 66 of 2017).

Is the Costa Rican colón pegged to the US dollar?

No. Costa Rica's central bank has run a managed float since 2015. The colón strengthened to about ¢449.5 per dollar in August 2026, the strongest level in roughly two decades. Panama, by contrast, uses the US dollar as legal tender.
