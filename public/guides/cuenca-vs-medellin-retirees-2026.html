Cuenca vs Medellín for Retirees 2026: Costs, Visas, Property

By Charles Brossy October 21, 2026 9 min read

Cuenca vs Medellín is the classic choice for Americans who want to retire in the Andes, and in 2026 the short answer is this: Cuenca is cheaper and asks less pension income, while Medellín is warmer, bigger and carries more currency risk. Ecuador's retiree visa needs US$1,446 a month and the country uses the US dollar. Colombia's pension visa needs about US$1,660 a month at today's rate, and everything there is priced in pesos, which rose 22.56% against the dollar in the year to mid-October. Both countries carry real security warnings. Here is the side-by-side, checked against official and market sources on October 21, 2026.

Visas: Ecuador Asks for Less, in Dollars

Ecuador's jubilado (retiree) temporary residence visa needs a pension of at least three Salarios Básicos Unificados. The Ministry of Labour set the 2026 SBU at US$482, so the floor is US$1,446 a month, plus US$250 a month for each dependent. You also need health insurance, national or foreign, valid for the visa period. The visa lasts 2 years and costs US$50 to apply and US$270 when granted. After at least 21 continuous months of temporary residence you can apply for permanent residence (US$50 plus US$225). Applicants aged 65 and over get 50% off those permanent residence fees. See the full Ecuador pensioner visa checklist.

Colombia's M Pensionado visa needs a lifetime pension of at least three monthly minimum wages. The 2026 minimum wage is COP 1,750,905, so the floor is COP 5,252,715, about US$1,660 at the October 21 TRM. The visa lasts up to 3 years and does not allow work. The fees are US$56 for the study and US$279 for the visa. You need health coverage valid in Colombia, and after 5 years as an M holder you can move to a Resident visa.

The currency is the key difference. Because the Colombian threshold is set in pesos, its dollar cost rises when the peso strengthens. Portafolio, citing Bloomberg, reports the peso gained 22.56% against the dollar between October 15, 2025 and October 15, 2026. At the start of that period the same requirement cost noticeably fewer dollars. Ecuador's threshold is set in dollars and moves only when the SBU changes.

Property can also open a door. Ecuador's investor visa accepts a registered property worth at least 100 SBU, or US$48,200 in 2026. Colombia's M Inversionista accepts property held solely in your name worth at least 350 minimum wages (about US$193,700), registered as foreign investment with Banco de la República. Costs and Property: Cuenca Is Cheaper, Medellín Is Deeper

Numbeo's crowd-sourced data puts a single person's monthly costs before rent at just under US$500 in Cuenca and about US$840 in Medellín. Rent comes on top, and so does health insurance. See our Cuenca cost-of-living breakdown for line items.

For buyers, the data gap is real. Cuenca has no reliable public price index. The best recent data point is Primicias (September 2025): asking prices reach about US$1,500 per m² in the high-appreciation areas along the Tomebamba river, quoting David Flores of the Azuay realtors' association. He blamed speculation and informality for the rises, and described gentrification linked to foreign arrivals. Treat any Cuenca price as a negotiation starting point and compare listings street by street. Our Cuenca neighborhood guide covers the main districts.

Medellín's market is larger and better measured. Metrocuadrado's August 2026 listings put asking prices at COP 5.1–6.5 million per m² (≈US$1,620–2,055) in estratos 3–4, the middle socio-economic tiers. In estratos 5–6, the upper tiers, prices run COP 7.7–12.3 million (≈US$2,440–3,880). Yields are also better documented. Global Property Guide puts Colombia's average at 7.01% (Q1 2026) and samples El Poblado one-bedrooms at 7.15%; we found no reliable recent yield figure for Cuenca. For a retiree who may rent the property out later, that data matters. But rent is paid in pesos, and the peso's swings cut both ways.

Taxes and Closing Costs

In Ecuador, the municipal alcabala transfer tax is 1% of the taxable value (COOTAD, art. 535). Sellers pay a separate municipal tax of 10% on the gain from selling urban property. The annual predial runs between 0.25 and 5 per thousand of the property's value, set by each council. PwC's Ecuador summary (reviewed July 2026) lists no net wealth tax on individuals.

In Colombia, Antioquia charges a 1% registration tax on property deeds, split equally between the parties unless they agree otherwise. Notary fees and registry fees come on top. A seller who is an individual generally faces 1% withholding at the notary (Estatuto Tributario art. 398; DIAN Concepto 11625 of 2025); higher rates can apply to non-resident sellers. Annual predial generally runs 0.4% to 1.2%, per PwC. Colombia also has a wealth tax: from 72,000 UVT of net worth (COP 3,770,928,000, about US$1.19 million, for 2026). Non-resident individuals pay it on assets held in Colombia. A 2025 emergency decree tried to cut the threshold to 40,000 UVT for 2026, but the Constitutional Court struck it down in April 2026 (Sentencia C-079 of 2026), so the 72,000 UVT threshold applies. Ask a Colombian tax adviser how the tax applies to you.

Healthcare: IESS Is Open to Residents, Colombia's Pension Visa Is Not

Ecuador's IESS lets foreigners join voluntarily if they hold an Ecuadorian cédula, are 18 or over and have no debts with the institute. For foreign retirees, the contribution base follows the pension received from their home country and cannot be below one SBU. Voluntary members pay 17.60%, so the 2026 minimum is US$84.83 a month, plus 3.41% of the SBU (about US$16.44) to extend cover to a spouse.

Colombia is stricter for this visa. Cancillería's M Pensionado page says the visa does not let its holder join the Colombian social security system, except under bilateral or multilateral agreements. You must show Colombian health coverage or a full-risk policy for your stay. Budget for private insurance in Medellín from day one.

Climate and Altitude

Cuenca sits at an average of about 2,530 m in an inter-Andean basin. Medellín sits at 1,479 m, and the city government gives an average temperature of 24 °C. For retirees with heart or lung conditions, that 1,000-plus metre difference is worth raising with a doctor before a scouting trip.

Risks: Security, Power and the Peso

Ecuador security. Ecuador recorded 9,216 intentional homicides in 2025, a rate of 50.91 per 100,000 and the highest on record (Primicias, citing the Interior Ministry). Homicides fell 6.4% nationally in January to August 2026. But Azuay, Cuenca's province, was one of only two Sierra provinces where they rose, from 18 to 33. The US State Department rates Ecuador Level 2, "Exercise Increased Caution" (August 18, 2026). Its Do Not Travel and Reconsider Travel areas are on the coast and borders, including parts of Guayaquil, Durán, Esmeraldas, Manabí and Santa Elena; Azuay is not named.

Ecuador power. Hydropower covered about 70% of Ecuador's demand in 2024, so drought hits the grid hard. In October 2024 Ecuador raised scheduled blackouts to 14 hours a day during its worst drought in six decades (Vistazo). The problem is not over: on the night of October 13–14, 2026, residents reported unscheduled outages in Cuenca and other Azuay cantons. Extra reported that Mazar was temporarily offline and thermal plants covered only part of demand. El Oriente reports analysts' estimate of a 1,300–1,800 MW national shortfall, and President Noboa expects the hardest months from late November to February. A backup power source is worth budgeting for.

Colombia security. Colombia is Level 3, "Reconsider Travel", since March 31, 2026, for crime, terrorism, civil unrest, kidnapping and natural disasters. Its Do Not Travel areas are Arauca, Cauca (except Popayán), Valle del Cauca (except Cali), Norte de Santander and the area within 10 km of the Venezuelan border. Antioquia, Medellín's department, is not on that list, but the country-wide Level 3 still applies.

Peso volatility. A 22.56% move in one year is large. If you live on a US pension in Medellín, a strong peso raises your costs in dollars; a weak one lowers them. Cuenca removes that variable, since Ecuador has used the dollar since January 9, 2000.

Which City Fits Your Retirement?

Choose Cuenca if you want the lowest income threshold (US$1,446), no exchange-rate risk, lower day-to-day costs and a path to IESS cover. Accept the altitude, a thin property data market, a fragile power grid and Ecuador's security trends. More in our Retire in Ecuador 2026 guide.

Choose Medellín if you want a warmer, larger city with a deeper, better-measured property market with published gross yields around 7%, and you can handle peso swings and private health insurance. Your pension needs to clear about US$1,660 at today's rate, and the Level 3 advisory applies nationwide. More in our Retire in Colombia 2026 guide and the Medellín city guide.

FAQ: Cuenca vs Medellín for Retirees

Which pension visa needs less income, Ecuador or Colombia?

Ecuador. Its jubilado visa needs a pension of at least 3 SBU, which is US$1,446 a month at the 2026 SBU of US$482. Colombia's M Pensionado visa needs at least 3 minimum wages, COP 5,252,715 a month, or about US$1,660 at the October 21, 2026 TRM of COP 3,163.76. Colombia's dollar figure moves with the exchange rate.

Is Cuenca cheaper than Medellín for retirees?

On Numbeo's crowd-sourced figures, yes. A single person's monthly costs before rent run just under US$500 in Cuenca and about US$840 in Medellín. Numbeo figures change often and do not include rent or health insurance.

Can I join public healthcare as a retiree in Cuenca or Medellín?

In Ecuador, foreigners with an Ecuadorian cédula can join IESS voluntarily; for foreign retirees the contribution base follows their home-country pension and cannot be below one SBU, so the minimum is about US$84.83 a month in 2026. In Colombia, Cancillería says the M Pensionado visa does not let its holder join the Colombian social security system, except under bilateral or multilateral agreements, so plan on private cover.
