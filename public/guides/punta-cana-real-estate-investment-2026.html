Punta Cana Real Estate Investment 2026: Prices, Yields and Rules for Foreigners

By Charles Brossy October 23, 2026 9 min read

Punta Cana real estate investment is open to foreigners on the same terms as Dominicans, and entry prices are moderate for the Caribbean: a July 2026 analysis of active listings put the median asking price for an apartment in Punta Cana–Bávaro at US$2,278 per m² (median unit US$195,000) and in Cap Cana at US$3,465 per m² (median US$374,500). Global Property Guide puts average gross rental yields in Punta Cana and Bávaro at about 9%. The catches are real: 12,854 apartment listings were active in Punta Cana–Bávaro alone, the zone sits in the Atlantic hurricane belt, and a June 2026 tax law changed how property sales are taxed while its rules are still being written.

The most detailed recent price data comes from CLIC Inmobiliaria’s July 2026 report, which took active apartment-for-sale listings aggregated from Dominican portals (data cut-off 23 June 2026), removed outliers and computed medians. These are asking prices, not closing prices, so expect to negotiate below them, especially on resales.

Rental Yields: What the Data Supports

Global Property Guide’s Q3 2026 survey (September 2026) gives Punta Cana and Bávaro an average gross rental yield of 9.07%, against a national average of 9.21%, up from 7.98% and 8.53% in its Q1 2026 survey. Those are gross figures built from asking prices and asking rents; GPG notes that net yields are typically 1.5 to 2 percentage points lower once taxes, repairs and other expenses come out, and a furnished short-term rental also carries management and vacancy costs. We could not find an independent, verifiable source for the 10%-plus short-term-rental returns that appear in many developer brochures, so we do not repeat them. Before you buy, ask for 12 months of actual booking, occupancy and payout records for the unit or a comparable unit in the same building.

The demand side is strong. The Ministry of Tourism reported 7,700,118 visitors from January to July 2026 (5,885,259 by air and 1,814,859 cruise passengers), up 7% on the same months of 2025, and Punta Cana International Airport handled 58% of the country’s arriving flights in July. In January–September 2025 it received 59% of arrivals. Tourism volume, however, does not guarantee that your particular condo fills; it competes with hotels and thousands of other rentals.

Can Foreigners Buy? The Rules

Yes. Article 51 of the Dominican Constitution protects the right to property for citizens and foreigners alike, and Foreign Investment Law 16-95 lets a foreign individual or company invest on the same conditions and with the same rights as a Dominican national. The main exception, under Article 10, is land within 50 km of the borders, far from Punta Cana. In practice:

  1. Check the title. Your own lawyer, not the developer’s, should pull the Certificado de Título and its status at the Registro de Títulos and check for liens, mortgages and disputes, and confirm the unit’s condominium regime.
  2. Check CONFOTUR status on new projects (below): ask for the project’s classification resolution, not a brochure line.
  3. Pay the 3% transfer tax (unless exempt) and register the deed. The DGII requires the seller to request that the property be removed from their tax record within six months of the sale.

IPI in practice. At the Banco Central’s 19 October 2026 buying rate of RD$61.36 per dollar, the 2026 exemption is worth about US$174,000. It applies to your total Dominican holdings, not per property, and to the value the DGII assigns. If your combined holdings were valued at RD$15,000,000, the tax would be 1% of RD$4,304,506, or RD$43,045 a year. A home owned by someone aged 65 or over is excluded when it is their only real estate.

The capital-gains change. Law 30-26, promulgated on 18 June 2026, replaced the old treatment of individuals’ property gains with a flat 10% final tax and added exemptions when the proceeds from selling your habitual residence are reinvested in a new one within six months, and for sellers over 65 selling their habitual residence. Some well-known references still quote the old regime: Global Property Guide’s tax page, updated in September 2026, still says “generally 27%”. Because the regulation that defines how the gain is calculated is still a draft, get a written opinion from a Dominican tax adviser before you model an exit.

CONFOTUR: What the Tourism Incentive Really Covers

Law 158-01 on tourism development (CONFOTUR) exempts classified tourism projects from income tax, from transfer taxes on real-estate rights, and excludes the classified property from the IPI base. Under Article 7, the exemption lasts 15 years from the date construction and equipment of the project are finished, not from the date you buy, so a unit in a project completed years ago has less of the window left. For buyers, the relevant benefit is the exemption on the first transfer of units in a classified project, extended to purchasers by Law 195-13.

Two cautions. First, not every project or every transfer gets the same treatment: Pellerano & Herrera, a leading Dominican law firm, advises checking the project’s classification resolution and each unit’s conditions. Second, Law 30-26 added a rule that no one can benefit from more than one incentive regime for the same activity, investment or operation at once. It does not expressly repeal CONFOTUR, but its practical scope is not yet settled. Our CONFOTUR explainer summarizes the rule.

Residency: Not Included With the Condo

Buying a unit does not by itself make you a resident. Law 171-07 offers income-based categories with tax incentives: a pensionado must receive a foreign pension of at least US$1,500 a month, and a rentista at least US$2,000 a month, plus US$250 a month for each dependent applying with them. Beneficiaries apply through the investment residence program; confirm current requirements with an immigration lawyer before you plan around them.

The Risks to Price In

Supply. Some 12,854 active apartment listings in Punta Cana–Bávaro is a lot of competition for any resale or rental. In April 2026, Frank Rainieri, chairman of Grupo Puntacana, called the area’s uncontrolled real-estate growth “unsustainable”, pointing to projects in areas without basic services or treatment plants and to large buildings on narrow streets, and said the area lacks a territorial land-use plan. Favor finished buildings with a track record, and check roads, water and sewage for the specific site.

Hurricanes. In September 2022, Hurricane Fiona’s eye made landfall near Boca de Yuma on the east coast with estimated sustained winds of 90 mph; Punta Cana International Airport reported a gust of 79 mph. Get a quote for windstorm insurance and ask how the building handled Fiona before you sign.

Safety. The US State Department rated the Dominican Republic Level 2: Exercise Increased Caution, because of crime, in its June 2025 reissue, noting that resort areas tend to be better policed than cities such as Santo Domingo. Check the current advisory before you travel.

Currency. The listing prices above are quoted in US dollars, but the IPI exemption and many local costs are set in pesos, so the dollar value of the threshold moves with the exchange rate. Plan your funds transfer carefully; see how to transfer money abroad to buy property.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can foreigners buy property in Punta Cana?

Yes. Article 51 of the Dominican Constitution protects the right to property for everyone, and Foreign Investment Law 16-95 gives foreign investors the same rights as Dominican nationals. The main exception is land within 50 km of the borders, far from Punta Cana. Your own lawyer should check the Certificado de Título at the Registro de Títulos.

What rental yield can you expect in Punta Cana?

Global Property Guide puts the average gross rental yield for Punta Cana and Bávaro at about 9% (9.07% in its Q3 2026 survey), based on asking prices and asking rents. It notes net yields are typically 1.5 to 2 percentage points lower. Building-level results vary widely, so ask for real occupancy and rent records for the specific unit.

What taxes do you pay when buying property in Punta Cana in 2026?

The buyer pays a 3% transfer tax on the property's value (Law 173-07, due within six months of the sale), unless a CONFOTUR exemption applies to the first transfer. Each year individuals pay 1% IPI on the combined value of their Dominican real estate above RD$10,695,494 in 2026. When an individual sells, Law 30-26 of June 2026 sets a 10% final tax on the capital gain, although the regulation defining the taxable base is still in draft.
