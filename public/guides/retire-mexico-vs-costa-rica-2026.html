Retire in Mexico vs Costa Rica 2026: Visas, Health Care, Taxes and Property

By Charles Brossy October 28, 2026 9 min read

If you want to retire in Mexico vs Costa Rica, the first gap is the visa: Costa Rica's pensionado residence needs a pension of US$1,000 a month, while Mexico's temporary residence asks for 680 UMA days of after-tax income, about MXN 79,770 (≈US$4,280) a month in 2026. Costa Rica is the easier visa; Mexico is usually cheaper day to day and lets you qualify on savings instead. Below we compare visas, health cover, buying rules, taxes on a sale and safety, with every figure checked against the law or an official source on October 28, 2026.

Visas: Costa Rica Asks Far Less Income

Costa Rica's Law 8764 sets the pensionado bar at a monthly pension from abroad of at least US$1,000 "or its equivalent", which must be permanent and stable. People living on investments use the rentista category instead, at US$2,500 a month, and the rentista route can include a spouse and children under 25. Both are temporary residences granted for up to two years and renewable. Our Costa Rica retirement guide covers the paperwork.

Mexico's thresholds come from the Lineamientos for issuing visas, published in the Diario Oficial in July 2025 and still unchanged on these figures after the May 2026 amendment. A temporary resident must show after-tax employment or pension income above 680 UMA days a month for the last six months. Alternatively, you can show an average monthly balance of 11,460 UMA days over the last 12 months, or own Mexican property worth more than 91,710 UMA days (about MXN 10.76 million, ≈US$577,000). Retirees can apply directly for permanent residence with a pension above 1,140 UMA days a month or an average balance of 45,850 UMA days (≈MXN 5.38 million). Because the rule is set in UMA and pesos, its dollar cost changes with the exchange rate and with each year's UMA update. Our retirement visa income table compares both with eight other countries.

One Costa Rica change matters for new arrivals. Law 9996, published in July 2021, gave new pensionados, rentistas and investors duty-free household goods and other tax breaks if they applied within the law's first five years. The law was published on July 14, 2021, so that five-year window ended in July 2026, but people who already obtained the benefits keep them for 10 years.

Health Care: Mandatory in Costa Rica, Optional in Mexico

Costa Rica requires temporary and permanent residents to prove they are insured with the CCSS, the national health system, from the moment residence is granted and without interruption until they renew their residence card. Budget for the monthly contribution as a fixed cost of living there.

Mexico's visa rules cited above set no health-insurance condition for residence. Its IMSS offers a voluntary family health insurance (Seguro de Salud para la Familia) with annual fees that rise with age: MXN 14,850 at 50–59, MXN 20,600 at 60–69, MXN 21,500 at 70–79 and MXN 22,150 from 80, in force since March 1, 2026. People with certain pre-existing conditions cannot enrol, and some treatments have waiting periods. IMSS's page says the plan is for families in Mexico and asks for a CURP (population registry number) for each member. It does not say outright whether foreign residents qualify, so confirm with your local IMSS office.

Buying Property: Trusts in Mexico, Concessions in Costa Rica

Mexico's Constitution (Article 27) bars foreigners from holding direct title to land within 100 km of the borders and 50 km of the beaches. There, foreigners buy through a bank trust (fideicomiso) that needs a permit from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (SRE) and runs for up to 50 years, renewable on request. Outside that zone, a foreigner can hold title directly after agreeing with the SRE to be treated as a Mexican national for that property. See can foreigners buy property in Mexico.

Costa Rica's Constitution (Article 19) gives foreigners the same individual rights as citizens, so titled property inland can generally be bought directly, subject to the exceptions the law sets. The coast is different: Law 6043 makes the first 200 m from the ordinary high-tide line a maritime zone. The first 50 m are public, and the remaining 150 m can only be used through a municipal concession. Concessions are not granted to foreigners with fewer than five years of residence, to companies more than 50% owned by foreigners, or to entities domiciled abroad or set up in Costa Rica by foreigners. Check whether a beach listing is titled land or a concession before you make an offer. Our Costa Rica buying rules guide explains the difference.

Taxes When You Own and When You Sell

Costa Rica charges 0.25% a year on the registered value of property, and 1.5% transfer tax on a purchase (Law 6999). Homes valued above ₡143 million (the 2026 exempt amount) also pay the annual solidarity tax on luxury homes. Mexico's property tax (predial) is set locally and varies widely by city.

The bigger difference comes when you sell. A non-resident selling in Mexico pays 25% of the gross price with no deductions, or can pay on the net gain at the top personal rate (35%) through a qualifying Mexican representative. Tax residents can exempt the sale of their own home up to 700,000 UDIs, once every three years. Costa Rica taxes capital gains at 15%. On the first sale of a property owned before July 1, 2019, the seller can instead pay 2.25% of the sale price. The sale of your habitual home is exempt, and when the seller is not domiciled in Costa Rica the buyer withholds 2.5% of the price.

Cost of Living and Safety

Numbeo's crowd-sourced data (updated October 26–27, 2026) puts monthly costs for a single person, excluding rent, at about US$768 in Mexico City and US$715 in Mérida, against US$1,004 in San José. A one-bedroom in the centre averages MXN 20,993 (≈US$1,125) in Mexico City and MXN 11,522 (≈US$620) in Mérida, against about US$1,186 in San José. Treat these as rough guides: they are averages of user reports, not official statistics.

The US State Department rates both countries Level 2, "Exercise increased caution". Costa Rica's advisory, updated April 2, 2026, cites petty crime and violent crime that also affects tourists. Mexico's, updated May 29, 2026, cites terrorism, crime and kidnapping, with higher risk in some areas. According to press reports (Fox News, June 2026), six states remain at Level 4, "Do not travel": Colima, Guerrero, Michoacán, Sinaloa, Tamaulipas and Zacatecas. Read the state-by-state map before you choose a town.

Which Should You Choose?

Choose Costa Rica if your pension is modest, around US$1,000–4,000 a month, and you want a single national health system and simple direct title inland. Choose Mexico if you have savings rather than a large pension, want lower day-to-day costs, or want to apply straight for permanent residence. In either country, have your own lawyer review the title and, on the coast, the trust or concession. Still undecided? Take the market quiz, or browse the Mexico City and San José city guides.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it cheaper to retire in Mexico or Costa Rica?

Usually Mexico. Numbeo's October 2026 data puts monthly costs for a single person, excluding rent, at about US$768 in Mexico City and US$715 in Mérida, against US$1,004 in San José. Costa Rica's retirement visa is cheaper to qualify for, though: US$1,000 a month of pension against about US$4,280 for Mexico's temporary residence.

How much income do I need to retire in Mexico in 2026?

For temporary residence, 680 UMA days a month of after-tax pension or employment income over the last six months, about MXN 79,770 (≈US$4,280 at the October 27, 2026 Banxico FIX). Instead, you can show an average balance of 11,460 UMA days (≈MXN 1.34 million) over 12 months. Permanent residence for retirees needs 1,140 UMA days a month.

Can foreigners own beachfront property in Mexico and Costa Rica?

In Mexico, yes, through a bank trust (fideicomiso) of up to 50 years, renewable, within 50 km of the beaches. In Costa Rica, the first 200 m from the high-tide line is the maritime zone: the first 50 m are public, and the next 150 m can only be held through a concession, which is not granted to foreigners with fewer than five years of residence or to companies more than 50% foreign-owned.
