Best Retirement Visa in Latin America? Panama Pensionado vs Ecuador Pensioner Visa (2026)

By Charles Brossy October 30, 2026 9 min read

If you are looking for the best retirement visa in Latin America, Panama and Ecuador are two of the most-searched options, and they differ in one key way. Panama's pensionado permit needs a lifetime pension of US$1,000 a month (or US$750 if you own Panamanian property worth more than US$100,000) and grants permanent residence from the start. Ecuador's jubilado visa needs three basic wages, US$1,446 a month in 2026, and gives a two-year temporary residence that can become permanent after more than 21 months. Below we compare income, fees, process, retiree discounts, property rules and safety, each checked against the law, the government page or, for travel advisories, official and press reports on October 30, 2026.

Income: Panama Asks Less, Especially If You Buy

Panama's official procedure page on Panamá Digital says the monthly pension or annuity "may not be less than" B/.1,000 and must be granted for life (vitalicia). If you prove that you bought property in Panama in your own name for more than B/.100,000, the minimum drops to B/.750 a month. Spouses can add their pensions together to reach the threshold, provided each pension meets the requirements. The rule is a pension, not savings or investment income, so early retirees living off a portfolio usually need a different permit. Our Panama US$750 pension tip explains the property route.

Ecuador ties its bar to the unified basic wage (salario básico unificado, SBU). The Foreign Ministry asks for a monthly pension from abroad "equal to or higher than" three SBU, plus US$250 a month for each dependant. The Ministry of Labour set the 2026 SBU at US$482, so the threshold is US$1,446 a month for a single applicant and US$1,696 for a couple applying as main applicant and dependant. Because the SBU is reset every year, check the figure in the year you apply. Our Ecuador pensioner visa checklist goes through the documents.

On income alone, Panama is the easier of the two for a single retiree at US$1,000 to US$1,446, and the US$750 property route has no equivalent in Ecuador. For context, our retirement visa income table sets both against eight other countries.

Permanence: Panama Grants It on Day One

This is the biggest structural difference. Panama's permit is a permiso de residente permanente: you apply for permanent residence directly, citing Decree-Law 3 (Arts. 18, 28 and 30) and the articles of its executive decree listed on the procedure page.

Ecuador's jubilado visa is a temporary residence valid for two years. To stay longer, you apply for permanent residence after more than 21 continuous months on temporary residence, and you must file before your temporary status expires. The permanent visa has indefinite validity and costs US$50 to apply plus US$225 when granted, with the same 50% discount from age 65. In practice that means two applications, two sets of fees and the risk of missing the window, against one application in Panama.

Process, Documents and Fees

Panama. The application is filed through a lawyer (mediante apoderado legal). You need a copy of your passport certified by a Panamanian notary, and every foreign document must be apostilled or authenticated by a Panamanian consulate and, if not in Spanish, translated by an authorized public translator. Amounts in another currency need a conversion to dollars from a competent entity. The official page lists no government fees and no processing time, so ask your lawyer for a written breakdown of state fees and professional charges before you start.

Ecuador. The jubilado visa is filed only online through e-VISAS. You need a passport valid for at least six months, a criminal-record certificate (applicants over 18) from your country of origin or residence covering the last five years (apostilled or legalized, and translated), an official document from the paying institution proving the pension and its monthly payment (apostilled or legalized), health insurance valid for the whole visa period, and proof of lawful means of support under Ministerial Agreement No. 70 of June 28, 2024. Fees are US$50 for the application and US$270 for the visa, with 50% off for people aged 65 or more and 100% off for people with a disability of 30% or more who hold a Ministry of Public Health card.

Retiree Discounts and Benefits

Panama's Law 6 of 1987 gives discounts to "all retirees and pensioners" as well as to residents over a set age, Panamanian or foreign. The copy published by the Ministry of Commerce lists, among others, 50% off entry to recreation and entertainment, 30% off interurban buses and boats, 25% off airfares, 50% off hotels Monday to Thursday and 30% at weekends, 25% off individual meals in restaurants (except fondas), 15% off private hospital bills if you have no hospitalization insurance, 10% off prescription medicines, 20% off doctors' consultations, and 25% off electricity bills of up to B/.50 a month in your name. This copy shows amendments up to 2007, so check how each provider applies the discounts before you count on the savings.

Ecuador's benefits come with age rather than the visa. Its Organic Law on Older Persons (LOPAM, 2019) applies to "Ecuadorian and foreign" older persons in Ecuador (Art. 2), defined as anyone aged 65 or more (Art. 5). Article 13 gives 50% off air, land, sea and river fares and public, cultural and sports events, free museum entry, and 50% off electricity up to 138 kWh a month, water up to 34 m³ and the basic landline fee. Article 14 exempts people aged 65 or more from fiscal and municipal taxes if their monthly income is at most 5 SBU or their net worth does not exceed 500 SBU; above those limits you pay only on the excess. National taxes collected by the SRI get only the breaks their own laws set, so this mainly helps with municipal taxes such as the annual property tax. Buying Property Under Each Visa

In Panama, property ownership can lower the pension bar, as above. When you buy, the 2% transfer tax (ITBI) falls on the seller under Law 106 of 1974, and Law 546 of August 31, 2026 exempts the first B/.120,000 of the taxable base on the first sale of a new home formalized within two years of its occupancy permit, with a reduced scale up to B/.200,000; resales are not covered (see our Law 546 tip). Our closing-costs table compares the other costs, and the Panama City guide covers prices and neighbourhoods.

In Ecuador, owning property does not lower the jubilado visa's income bar. Foreigners can buy in most of the country, but the Public and State Security Law bars foreigners from acquiring land in the 40 km border security zone, with exceptions for populated and urban areas; our Ecuador buying guide covers the border-zone rule, the transfer taxes and the steps. Safety and Practical Risks

The US State Department rates Panama Level 2, "Exercise increased caution", because of crime, in an advisory reissued on August 19, 2026, with "Do not travel" warnings for the Mosquito Gulf and parts of the Darién Region. Ecuador is also Level 2 overall, but its August 18, 2026 update, as reported by Primicias, lists Level 4 "Do not travel" areas: parts of Guayaquil (Nueva Prosperina, Pascuales and south of Avenida Portete de Tarqui), Durán, Huaquillas and Arenillas, Quevedo, Quinsaloma and Pueblo Viejo, and Esmeraldas city and the areas north of it. It also asks travellers to reconsider travel to several other areas, including all of Manabí and Santa Elena provinces, which include popular coastal retirement towns. Neither Cuenca nor Quito is named in those warnings, but read the current advisory before choosing a town.

Which Retirement Visa Should You Choose?

  • Choose Panama if your pension is between US$1,000 and US$1,446, if you want permanent residence in one step, or if you plan to buy a property worth more than US$100,000 anyway and your pension is at least US$750.
  • Choose Ecuador if you want an online application with published government fees (US$320 in total, with a 50% discount from age 65), and if you will use the age-based discounts and municipal-tax exemption that apply to any resident aged 65 or more.
  • Either way, budget for apostilles, translations and, in Panama, a lawyer, and confirm the current figures on the government page before you file. Our Retire in Panama and Retire in Ecuador guides cover costs of living and health care.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is easier to get, Panama's pensionado or Ecuador's pensioner visa?

On income, Panama: it needs a lifetime pension of US$1,000 a month (US$750 if you own Panamanian property bought for more than US$100,000), against US$1,446 a month for Ecuador in 2026. Panama also grants permanent residence in one step, while Ecuador starts with a two-year temporary visa.

How much does Ecuador's retirement visa cost in 2026?

The Foreign Ministry charges US$50 to apply and US$270 when the visa is granted, with a 50% discount for applicants aged 65 or more. Moving to permanent residence after more than 21 months costs another US$50 plus US$225, with the same discount.

Do foreign retirees get discounts in Panama and Ecuador?

Yes. Panama's Law 6 of 1987 gives retirees and pensioners discounts such as 25% at restaurants and 25% on airfares. Ecuador's law on older persons applies to foreigners in Ecuador aged 65 or more: 50% off transport fares and public events, 50% off basic utility consumption, and a municipal-tax exemption within income and net-worth limits.
