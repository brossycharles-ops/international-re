Retirement Visa Income Requirements: LatAm 2026 Table

By Charles Brossy October 26, 2026 8 min read

Retirement visa income requirements in Latin America range from US$1,000 a month in Panama and Costa Rica to about US$4,270 for Mexico's temporary residence, and two countries, Uruguay and Chile, set no fixed figure at all. The table below gives the minimum monthly pension each country's own law, decree or government portal states for 2026, converted to US dollars where the rule uses local units, with the exchange rate and date we used. Every figure was checked against the primary source on October 26, 2026.

How to Read This Table

Pension versus other income. Most retiree routes are built around a pension, and several countries run a separate category for people living on investments, usually at a higher figure. Costa Rica's pensionado needs a pension from abroad of US$1,000, while its rentista category asks for US$2,500 of stable income. The Dominican Republic splits the same way, at US$1,500 for pensioners and US$2,000 for rentistas. Argentina's rentista income must come from assets such as financial instruments, property or company shares, and its rule excludes pay from personal work. Brazil lets other regular income top up a pension to reach US$2,000. Mexico's temporary residence accepts after-tax income from employment or a pension, or a savings balance instead.

Local units move. Four countries tie the threshold to a wage or index: Ecuador to the basic wage (SBU), Colombia to the legal minimum wage, Mexico to the UMA and Argentina to the minimum wage (SMVM). Colombia, Mexico and Argentina also state it in local currency, so the dollar cost changes with the exchange rate even when the rule does not. A pension that clears the bar at application can fall short at renewal. Leave a margin.

Paperwork. Expect to prove the pension with an official certificate from the paying institution. Ecuador asks for it to be apostilled, or legalized if the issuing country is not part of the Apostille Convention. Colombia asks for it apostilled and translated into Spanish, or issued by the consulate of the country that pays the pension. Check each consulate's list before you order documents, because the details differ by country.

Country Notes

Panama. The Pensionado is a permanent residence permit, not a temporary visa. The Panamá Digital portal sets the floor at B/.1,000 a month and lowers it to B/.750 for applicants who bought property in Panama, in their own name, for more than B/.100,000. See our Pensionado $750 tip and the Panama City guide.

Costa Rica. Article 81 of the immigration law (Law 8764) sets the pensionado floor at US$1,000 "or its equivalent", from a monthly, permanent and stable pension from abroad. It is temporary residence; our pensionado tip covers the renewal and CCSS rules, and the Costa Rica retirement guide covers costs.

Argentina. The National Migration Directorate fixes both the pensionado and rentista minimums at five times the minimum wage. Resolution 4/2026 set the wage at ARS 391,200 from October 1, 2026, rising each month to ARS 437,000 in April 2027, so the requirement climbs in pesos every month.

Ecuador. The jubilado visa needs 3 SBU, US$1,446 a month in 2026, plus US$250 for each dependant. Because Ecuador sets the SBU in dollars, the threshold only changes when the basic wage does. Details in our Ecuador pensioner visa checklist.

Dominican Republic. Law 171-07 sets US$1,500 a month for pensioners and US$2,000 for rentistas, plus US$250 per dependant applying with the main applicant.

Colombia. The M Pensionado needs a lifetime pension of at least three legal minimum wages. The 2026 wage is COP 1,750,905, so the floor is COP 5,252,715, about US$1,663 at the October 24–26 TRM. The visa does not let its holder join the Colombian social security system, except under bilateral or multilateral agreements. See our Colombia pension visa tip and the Medellín city guide.

Brazil. Resolution 40 of 2019 from the National Immigration Council requires proof of a monthly transfer to Brazil of at least US$2,000 from a retirement or survivor's pension, with other regular income allowed as a top-up.

Mexico. Mexico's rule is the highest in dollar terms. The July 2025 visa Lineamientos set temporary residence at 680 UMA days a month and permanent residence for retirees at 1,140 UMA days. Consulates convert those into their own dollar figures, so check the one where you will apply. Our Mexico temporary residency quick-read compares consulate sheets.

Uruguay and Chile. Neither sets a fixed number for the main route. Uruguay's Foreign Ministry asks for income "sufficient for maintenance" and Migración sets no amount; the US$1,500 figure (a minimum, under Law 16.340) belongs to that separate regime, which adds customs benefits and requires a US$100,000 home or bonds. Chile's SERMIG asks for a pension or steady rental or financial income that covers at least basic needs. In both cases the authority decides what is enough.

FAQ: Retirement Visa Income in Latin America

Which Latin American country has the lowest retirement visa income requirement in 2026?

Among the countries with a fixed figure, Panama and Costa Rica ask the least: a pension of US$1,000 a month. Panama's floor drops to B/.750 if you bought property there in your own name for more than B/.100,000. Argentina's 5 minimum wages came to about US$1,280 in October 2026, but that dollar figure moves every month. Uruguay and Chile set no fixed amount and ask for income sufficient to live on.

Do retirement visa income requirements change every year?

In several countries, yes. Ecuador ties its floor to the basic wage (SBU), Colombia to the legal minimum wage, Mexico to the UMA and Argentina to the minimum wage, which rises month by month under Resolution 4/2026. Colombia, Mexico and Argentina also set the threshold in local currency, so its dollar cost moves with the exchange rate. Panama, Costa Rica, the Dominican Republic and Brazil state fixed US dollar amounts.

Can I qualify with investment income instead of a pension?

Often, but usually at a higher figure. Costa Rica's rentista category needs US$2,500 a month of stable income and the Dominican Republic's rentista route US$2,000. Argentina's rentista category asks for the same 5 minimum wages as its pension route, from assets rather than work. Mexico accepts savings with an average balance of 11,460 UMA days over 12 months instead of income. Chile's rentista route sets no fixed amount.
