Where to Buy Property in Cuenca, Ecuador: 2026 Neighborhood Guide

By Charles Brossy Updated October 26, 2026 10 min read

Where should a foreigner buy property in Cuenca, Ecuador in 2026? Most buyers look at El Centro, the UNESCO-listed historic core, and the newer riverside districts around San Sebastián and Yanuncay. Prices are modest by regional standards: Numbeo's October 2026 data put apartments at about $1,270/m² in the centre and about $940/m² outside it, and Primicias reports up to about $1,500/m² in the most sought-after areas. Rental returns are lower than many guides claim — Global Property Guide's last Ecuador survey found an average gross yield of about 4.7% in Cuenca — so buy for the life you want, not for income.

Why Cuenca in 2026?

Three things draw foreign buyers. First, Ecuador's Constitution (article 9) gives foreigners the same rights as Ecuadorians, so you can buy in your own name with no trust, local partner or minimum investment — the main exception is the 40 km border security zone, which does not affect Cuenca (see can foreigners buy property in Ecuador?). Second, Ecuador has used the US dollar since 2000, so there is no exchange-rate risk on a dollar purchase. Third, the historic centre — the Historic Centre of Santa Ana de los Ríos de Cuenca — has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1999, set in a valley irrigated by four rivers: the Tomebamba, Yanuncay, Tarqui and Machángara.

Construction is also a government priority: President Daniel Noboa has said he wants 2026 to be the "Año de la Construcción", and, as reported by El Diario, he said construction grew 20% and real estate 17.8% in January–March 2026 against the same period of 2025. Treat that as national momentum, not a Cuenca price forecast — Ecuador has no official house-price index. If you want to identify which Latin American market fits your goals, the neighbourhood notes below give you the specifics.

El Centro — Historic Core (UNESCO)

The New Cathedral's blue domes, Plaza de las Flores and the Tomebamba riverbank define the Centro Histórico. Primicias, citing Plusvalía listing data, reported prices around $1,200/m² in the historic centre, and Numbeo's city-centre average is about $1,270/m². Restored colonial buildings come with heritage-protection rules, so check with the municipality what you may change before you buy. Numbeo puts a central one-bedroom at about $537 a month and a central three-bedroom at about $723; Primicias found furnished Centro apartments and suites from about $300 to $550.

The trade-offs are street noise and older building stock. El Centro suits buyers who want to walk everywhere and live inside the heritage district more than buyers who want quiet or modern amenities.

El Ejido — Established Expat Zone

Immediately south of El Centro across the Tomebamba, El Ejido has wide avenues, supermarkets and restaurants and is close to the Universidad de Cuenca. We could not find any published price-per-m² or yield figure specific to El Ejido, so compare listings on the portals and ask an independent appraiser before you offer. It is a practical first choice for buyers who want existing infrastructure within walking distance of the centre.

Yanuncay — Riverside Modern Living

The Yanuncay river corridor in the south-west of the city has seen much of Cuenca's newer apartment construction. Primicias reported in December 2022 about $1,000–1,100/m² in Misicata (Baños parish, on the Yanuncay river), citing the Azuay realtors' association, and up to $1,500/m² for new units in the Parque Tarqui-Guzho area — older data, so check current listings. Primicias also noted that parts of Misicata still had unpaved streets, so look at the specific block, not just the building.

San Sebastián — Ordóñez Lasso and Puertas del Sol

North-west of El Centro, the San Sebastián parish includes the Ordóñez Lasso and Puertas del Sol corridors, where many of Cuenca's higher-end towers stand. Primicias reported $1,100–1,300/m² there in 2022 (Plusvalía data) and, in September 2025, that Puertas del Sol could be reaching about $1,500/m². It suits buyers who want newer buildings with amenities and are willing to pay the city's top prices.

Turi — Hilltop Views

Turi sits on the southern hillside above the city, with views over the domed skyline. Housing is lower-density and a car is close to essential. We found no published price or yield data for Turi, so rely on comparable listings and an independent appraisal.

Taxes and Costs When You Buy and Sell

  • Transfer tax (alcabala), paid by the buyer: 1% of the taxable base — the contract price or the cadastral value, whichever is higher (COOTAD arts. 532 and 535). The law allows additional surcharges collected with it — each up to 50% of the basic rate and together no more than 100% (art. 536).
  • Notary and registry: set by official tariffs and charged on top; ask the notary for a written quote. The Registro de la Propiedad requires an encumbrance certificate (certificado de gravamen) and proof that the alcabala and gain taxes are paid before it registers the sale.
  • Municipal gain tax (utilidades / plusvalía), paid by the seller: Cuenca charges 10% of the gain, reduced by 0.5 percentage points for each year of ownership, reaching zero after 20 years.
  • Income tax on the gain: Ecuador's 2021 reform exempts individuals' occasional sales of homes and land from income tax; commercial property such as offices and shops is taxed. Confirm the current rule with an Ecuadorian tax adviser before you sell.
  • Annual property tax (predial): each council sets a rate between 0.25 and 5 per thousand (0.025%–0.5%) of the cadastral value (COOTAD art. 504). Cuenca's bill also carries a citizen-security fee, a fire-service surcharge and special improvement contributions.
  • Sending money abroad: Ecuador's currency-exit tax (ISD) stays at a general 5% in 2026, which matters when you repatriate sale proceeds.

Risks to Weigh

Security. The US State Department rates Ecuador Level 2 (Exercise Increased Caution); its higher-risk zones are mostly on the coast and borders, and Azuay and Cuenca are not named. Ecuador's homicides fell 6.4% in January–August 2026 after a record 9,216 in 2025, but Azuay and Carchi were the only highland provinces where they rose (Azuay from 18 to 33). Power. Cuenca and other Azuay cantons had unscheduled blackouts on October 13–14, 2026. Title. Hire an independent Ecuadorian attorney to pull the registry certificates and check for liens, unpermitted construction and title gaps before you sign; the registry certificates themselves are inexpensive, while lawyers' fees vary.

The Bottom Line

Cuenca offers dollar pricing, equal ownership rights for foreigners and a UNESCO-listed centre at prices around $940–1,500/m². It is not a high-yield market — gross yields average about 4.7% — and buyers should weigh Ecuador's security situation and recent blackouts. For walkability, look at El Centro and El Ejido; for newer buildings, Yanuncay and the Ordóñez Lasso / Puertas del Sol corridors in San Sebastián. Whatever you choose, budget the 1% alcabala and fees, and get an independent title check.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can foreigners buy property in Cuenca, Ecuador?

Yes. Ecuador's Constitution gives foreigners the same rights as Ecuadorians, so you can buy in your own name with no trust, local partner or minimum investment. The main restriction is the 40 km border security zone, which does not include Cuenca.

What taxes do I pay when buying property in Cuenca?

The buyer pays the municipal alcabala of 1% of the higher of the price and the cadastral value, plus notary and registry fees. The seller pays Cuenca's 10% municipal tax on the gain, reduced by 0.5 points per year of ownership.

What rental yield can I expect in Cuenca?

About 4.7% gross on average, according to Global Property Guide's last Ecuador survey (2022 data). Numbeo puts a central one-bedroom at about $537 a month and a central three-bedroom at about $723 in October 2026.
